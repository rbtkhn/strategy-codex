# Governed Judgment and Mixed-Initiative Lineage

Purpose: establish that the repo's human-governed, mixed-initiative, and
judgment-support logic has clear academic precedent.

## Why This Family Matters

The repo does not only distribute reasoning across roles. It also insists that
human or institutional authority must remain legible and preserved.

That combination has academic precedent too.

## Primary Sources

### 1. Horvitz 1999

[Eric Horvitz, *Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces*](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/chi99horvitz.pdf)

Why it matters:

- Horvitz explicitly frames human-computer interaction as an elegant coupling
  of direct manipulation and automated services
- the paper treats mixed initiative as a design principle rather than a
  one-sided automation story

Closest repo resemblance:

- human-machine shared initiative
- automation that assists without erasing human steering
- routing of effort to the party best suited to act

Distance:

- **partial ancestor**

### 2. Novick and Sutton 1997

[David G. Novick and Stephen Sutton, *What is Mixed-Initiative Interaction?*](https://cdn.aaai.org/Symposia/Spring/1997/SS-97-04/SS97-04-021.pdf)

Why it matters:

- the paper shows that initiative is not a crude binary
- it analyzes initiative as a structured interaction problem involving speaker,
  task, and outcome

Closest repo resemblance:

- initiative as a compositional design question
- governance of who steers what, when, and why

Distance:

- **partial ancestor**

### 3. St. Amant 1997

[Robert St. Amant, *Navigation and Planning in a Mixed-Initiative User Interface*](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1997/AAAI97-010.pdf)

Why it matters:

- the paper describes a mixed-initiative planner where users can delegate
  details without giving up the ability to guide and review decisions

Closest repo resemblance:

- delegation without surrender of review
- shared planning between human and machine

Distance:

- **close architectural relative**

### 4. Kamar et al. 2014

[Human-Agent Collectives](https://cacm.acm.org/research/human-agent-collectives/)

Why it matters:

- the article explicitly names systems built from close partnership and
  flexible social interaction between humans and agents
- it highlights the balance of control between users and agents as a critical
  design problem

Closest repo resemblance:

- human-agent collective reasoning
- negotiated balance of control
- provenance and coordination concerns in long-running systems

Distance:

- **close architectural relative**

### 5. Ribeiro, Singh, and Guestrin 2018

[The Challenge of Crafting Intelligible Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04263)

Why it matters:

- this paper treats intelligibility as a design requirement for AI used by
  people
- it argues that opaque intelligence is a systems problem, not just a model
  problem

Closest repo resemblance:

- intelligibility as architecture
- making judgment-support systems inspectable

Distance:

- **partial ancestor**

### 6. Green and Chen 2020

[Algorithm-in-the-Loop Decision Making](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/7115)

Why it matters:

- the paper explicitly centers human decision making in algorithm-assisted
  systems
- it treats algorithmic aid as a framework for studying social impacts and
  practical limits

Closest repo resemblance:

- judgment support rather than full replacement
- human-centered framing of assisted decision making

Distance:

- **close architectural relative**

### 7. De-Arteaga et al. 2020

[A Case for Humans-in-the-Loop: Decisions in the Presence of Erroneous Algorithmic Scores](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08035)

Why it matters:

- the paper studies how experts change decisions when using algorithmic tools
- it concludes that decision pipelines should preserve human autonomy

Closest repo resemblance:

- governance and autonomy as architectural concerns
- resistance to smooth full automation in high-stakes settings

Distance:

- **close architectural relative**

### 8. Chakraborti et al. 2016/2017

[Proactive Decision Support using Automated Planning (RADAR)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07841)

Why it matters:

- RADAR is a direct human-in-the-loop decision-support architecture
- the planner aids the human by surfacing constraints and plan risks rather than
  replacing the decision maker

Closest repo resemblance:

- proactive judgment support
- system helps structure choice while authority remains human

Distance:

- **close architectural relative**

## What This Proves

This family is enough to prove that the repo's authority-preserving and
mixed-initiative logic is not unique in kind.

The academic literature already contains:

- explicit mixed-initiative design theory
- human-agent collectives
- intelligibility as a design requirement
- decision-support systems that preserve human oversight and autonomy

That means the repo is not alone in insisting that assistance and authority are
different things.

## What This Does Not Prove

This family does **not** prove that:

- any one prior system already unified reflective learning, blackboard-style
  collaboration, and governed judgment support in the repo's exact way
- all human-in-the-loop systems are genuinely accountable
- the repo's governance logic is reducible to ordinary UX or oversight patterns

It establishes a strong lineage for the governance side of the design family,
not a complete blueprint of the repo.
