## Shared Frame

If Pope Leo is trying to speak seriously about AI, the repo should not collapse
the question into either tech ethics or acceleration anthropology alone.

The live pressure is whether synthetic systems are kept inside a prior human
order of meaning, office, and answerability — or allowed to inherit judgment,
mediation, and legitimacy merely by sounding competent.

That makes Leo a **cross-channel** object until the primary text is pinned and
one emphasis clearly dominates.

## Statecraft Emphasis

The `statecraft` question is not mainly whether the Church has a useful tech
ethic. It is whether Rome is trying to preserve a human office of judgment
against a world in which advice, administration, persuasion, and moral mediation
are being made machine-scalable.

That matters when the issue becomes:

- who still carries authority
- which offices remain non-delegable
- how legitimacy survives synthetic mediation
- whether Vatican speech acts as a civilizational counterclaim to a
  machine-administered order

Rome is not merely commenting on technology. It is speaking as:

- a civilizational residue
- a transnational office
- a legitimacy-bearing institution
- a rival anthropology to convenience-native technical order

The strongest statecraft seam:

```text
one of the world's oldest legitimacy-bearing offices may be trying to say
that machine competence is not enough to inherit human judgment
```

Likely statecraft argument paths (choose after primary text is pinned):

1. Rome is trying to preserve human office against synthetic succession.
2. The Vatican is offering a legitimacy-language critique AI that secular
   governance systems currently lack.
3. Pope Leo is implicitly contesting a civilizational order in which scale and
   convenience outrank formation and answerability.
4. The Church is marking a boundary around the kinds of judgment no system
   should inherit merely by sounding persuasive.

Statecraft return paths when this emphasis dominates:

- compact objects where synthetic cognition compresses command or judgment

## Singularity Emphasis

The `singularity` question is what AI does to mediation, judgment, authorship,
office, and human formation — whether AI is named as a tool inside a prior moral
order or as a new claimant on those functions.

A papal intervention changes the register beyond productivity, alignment risk,
labor displacement, or state-control framing. It asks whether AI alters:

- human dignity
- the nature of judgment
- the meaning of authorship
- the moral status of mediation
- the legitimacy of replacing presence with simulation

The singularity version stays centered on:

- can synthetic mediation remain subordinate to personhood
- what kinds of answerability collapse when machine speech substitutes for
  formed presence
- whether AI is framed as assistance, simulation, delegation, or latent succession
- what happens to office when advice, rhetoric, and witness become
  machine-scalable

The most promising singularity seam:

```text
when synthetic systems can speak persuasively,
what remains non-delegable about formed human presence?
```

Likely singularity argument paths (choose after primary text is pinned):

1. The Church is trying to domesticate AI within classical Christian anthropology.
2. The Church is marking off sacred and pastoral functions that should not be
   ceded to synthetic systems.
3. The Church is offering a counter-doctrine to Silicon Valley's implicit
   anthropology of convenience, substitution, and scale.
4. The Church is naming AI as a crisis of mediation — confusion about who is
   truly speaking, judging, or bearing responsibility.
