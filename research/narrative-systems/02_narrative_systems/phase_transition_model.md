# Geopolitical Phase Transition Model
## Monoidal Disagreement Curvature Extension of NST

---

# 1. Motivation

Narrative Systems Theory (NST) models geopolitical discourse as a:

- monoidal category of epistemic systems
- functorial mapping into temporal histories

However, this structure alone does not capture:

- sudden narrative shifts
- escalation cascades
- systemic breakdowns of alignment
- rapid regime changes in discourse

To model these phenomena, we introduce:

> 🌐 Phase transitions driven by monoidal disagreement curvature

---

# 2. Core Intuition

We reinterpret geopolitical systems as:

> a curved geometric field over monoidal narrative space

Where:

- flat regions → stable geopolitical narratives
- high curvature → instability zones
- curvature singularities → phase transitions

---

# 3. Monoidal Disagreement Field

Recall NST monoidal structure:

A ⊗ B ⊗ C

Each system has disagreement measure:

D(A), D(B), D(C)

We define:

## 3.1 Monoidal Disagreement

D(A ⊗ B) ≠ D(A) + D(B)

This non-linearity defines interaction energy between systems.

---

# 4. Disagreement Curvature

We define curvature over monoidal space:

## 4.1 Discrete curvature operator

K(A, B) = D(A ⊗ B) − (D(A) + D(B))

---

## 4.2 Interpretation

| K value | Meaning |
|--------|--------|
| ~0 | independent narrative systems |
| >0 | amplification / resonance |
| <0 | dampening / stabilisation |
| large | instability / transition risk |

---

# 5. Phase Transition Condition

A geopolitical phase transition occurs when:

## 5.1 Critical curvature threshold

|K(A ⊗ B ⊗ ...)| ≥ λ_c

Where:
- λ_c = critical instability threshold

---

## 5.2 Interpretation

When curvature exceeds λ_c:

> the narrative system undergoes structural reconfiguration

Examples:

- rapid narrative alignment collapse
- synchronized belief shifts
- escalation cascades between theaters
- institutional narrative breakdown

We define three regimes:

## 6.1 Stable Phase
