# Sovereignty Under Acceleration

WORK only; not Record.

This bridge sheet connects singularity-academy to repo-root `statecraft/` as the live authority-and-settlement receiver. Use it when compute, models, agents, memory, identity, permissions, data centers, audit, rollback, or automation are becoming strategic infrastructure and the question is no longer only technical: **who can still inspect, refuse, pause, reverse, contest, audit, and govern the system?**

When the signal matures into carrier, legitimacy, treaty, policy, or sovereignty architecture, treat repo-root [statecraft](/C:/dev/strategy-codex/statecraft/README.md) as the operative drafting surface rather than the older academy compatibility namespace.

Core thesis: sovereignty under acceleration means legitimate human or institutional authority remains meaningful after systems become faster, cheaper, more autonomous, more scalable, or more recursive.

When the acceleration object is specifically **high-skill labor compression** rather than model capability in the abstract, use the downstream statecraft bridges:

- [statecraft/compact/high-skill-labor-compression-and-sovereign-judgment.md](../../../statecraft/compact/high-skill-labor-compression-and-sovereign-judgment.md)
- [statecraft/civ-state/high-skill-labor-compression-and-civilizational-statecraft.md](../../../statecraft/civ-state/high-skill-labor-compression-and-civilizational-statecraft.md)

That object should usually be read as a judgment-infrastructure and legitimacy problem, not just a productivity or labor-market problem. The warning sign is simple: the outputs still arrive, but fewer human offices can truly inspect, narrow, refuse, or inherit what the system is doing.

## Quick Pass

Use this when a live signal needs a fast statecraft read.

1. **Acceleration signal.** What is becoming faster, cheaper, more autonomous, more scalable, or more recursive?
2. **Authority pressured.** Which human, institution, state, community, or command chain is losing meaningful control?
3. **Control plane.** Where do compute, memory, identity, permissions, audit, rollback, and command actually live?
4. **Human Command Test.** Who can inspect, refuse, pause, reverse, contest, and govern the system?
5. **Output.** Produce one authority map, treaty clause, policy memo paragraph, crisis brief, compute sovereignty note, or warning against fake control.

## Coffee D Handoff Rule

For Coffee D, treat this sheet as the canonical route-away trigger when all three are true:

1. the main object is a state, institution, alliance, regulator, military command, public infrastructure authority, or other legitimacy-bearing carrier;
2. the needed output is treaty, policy, negotiation, institutional design, compute compact, authority map, or sovereignty note rather than another acceleration diagnosis; and
3. the singularity-side control-plane read is already mature enough to name objective, memory, permissions, audit, pause, rollback, and the human command gap.

When those conditions hold, say so plainly and route to repo-root [statecraft](/C:/dev/strategy-codex/statecraft/README.md). Do not keep the work in Coffee D just because the source signal began as an AI or acceleration story.

### 7. Legitimacy

Control is not enough. Ask:

- Who accepts this authority as legitimate?
- Who can challenge it?
- Who is accountable for harm?
- What public explanation can survive scrutiny?
- What must never be normalized as convenience?

### 8. Emergency Powers

Acceleration creates pressure for emergency authority. Make expiration explicit:

- What can be done during emergency?
- Who declares the emergency?
- What expires automatically?
- Who reviews it?
- What cannot be suspended?
- What data, logs, or decisions must be preserved for later accountability?

### 9. Failure Modes

- identity spoofing;
- autonomous escalation;
- audit theater;
- rollback impossibility;
- dependency lock-in;
- emergency-power normalization;
- human approval that cannot actually understand or stop the system.

### 11. Human Command Test

A system is not meaningfully governed if the human role is ceremonial.

- Who can inspect?
- Who can refuse?
- Who can pause?
- Who can reverse?
- Who can contest?
- Who can audit?
- Who can govern?
- Who bears responsibility?

## Control-Plane Clause

Use this clause pattern as a starting point for treaties, procurement standards, institutional rules, or policy papers:

> No high-autonomy system may be deployed for consequential public, military, financial, educational, identity, or infrastructure decisions unless the competent authority is named; system actions are logged; affected persons or institutions can identify the responsible authority; inspection and audit are possible; pause and rollback procedures exist; appeal or contestation is available where rights or obligations are affected; and accountable human command remains legally and operationally meaningful.
