## Provisional Thesis

If Pope Leo is trying to give the Church a serious doctrine of AI, the important move will probably not be a generic blessing or denunciation of the technology. The real question is whether AI is being named as a tool inside a prior moral order, or as a new claimant on judgment, authorship, authority, and human formation.

That is why this line of inquiry belongs in `singularity/essays/` rather than
only in a religion or news shelf. A Catholic doctrine of AI, if stated clearly
enough, would not only comment on machines. It would state what kind of
creature the human remains when synthetic systems begin to speak, advise,
imitate, persuade, and perhaps displace.

## Why This Matters

Most secular AI discourse still oscillates between:

- productivity optimism
- alignment risk
- labor displacement
- state-control questions

A papal intervention changes the register. It asks whether AI alters:

- human dignity
- the nature of judgment
- the meaning of authorship
- the moral status of mediation
- the legitimacy of replacing presence with simulation

That makes the question larger than "is AI good or bad?" It becomes a question
about whether machine assistance is being kept inside a human order of meaning,
office, and accountability, or whether the machine is quietly being allowed to
inherit functions that once depended on formation of conscience.

## What This Version Owns

## What This Essay Will Likely Need To Argue

The future full version should probably decide among at least four candidate claims:

1. The Church is trying to domesticate AI within classical Christian anthropology.
2. The Church is trying to mark off sacred and pastoral functions that should not be ceded to synthetic systems.
3. The Church is offering a counter-doctrine to Silicon Valley's implicit anthropology of convenience, substitution, and scale.
4. The Church is naming AI as a crisis of mediation, where the danger is not only bad outputs but confusion about who is truly speaking, judging, or bearing responsibility.

The strongest version may combine all four, but the final essay should not pretend that combination is already proven before the primary text is integrated.

## The Most Promising Interpretive Seam

The most promising seam is not "Catholics finally react to AI." It is the
deeper claim that AI is a test of whether mediation can remain subordinate to
personhood.

In that frame, the decisive issue is not merely whether a model can produce text, images, or advice. The issue is whether societies begin treating synthetic mediation as a substitute for formed human presence in places where answerability matters. That opens several high-value routes:

- priest, teacher, and counselor as offices that cannot be reduced to information transfer
- machine language as a pressure on authorship and witness
- convenience as a solvent of formation
- simulated presence as a challenge to sacramental and embodied understandings of relation

That seam could make this essay useful well beyond Catholic commentary because
it touches one of singularity's central questions:

```text
when synthetic systems can speak persuasively,
what remains non-delegable about formed human presence?
```
