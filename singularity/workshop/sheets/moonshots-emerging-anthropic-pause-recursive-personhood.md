## Control-Plane Case Candidates

### Case: Permissioned agent enterprise harness

- **Agent:** Enterprise AI with strong advice quality but blocked on API, data, spend, and signature permissions.
- **Objective:** Deploy high-autonomy workflows without lawsuit-scale tail risk.
- **Authority:** CTO guard rails, legal jurisdiction, executive orders, or offshore first-deploy (Argentina example in panel).
- **Observability:** Workflow success visible internally; liability surface and permission graph often opaque.
- **Rollback:** Mixed — permissions can be tightened; once agents hold spend/sign rights under foreign personhood law, rollback crosses borders.

### Case: Optional global pause as hotline infrastructure

- **Agent:** Frontier lab publishing pause optionality while accelerating inner loop.
- **Objective:** Pre-build coordination mechanism before catastrophic tail risk (Cuban missile crisis analogy in panel).
- **Authority:** Lab leadership with cross-lab relationships; potential US government equity as backstop coordinator.
- **Observability:** Public document visible; actual coordination rooms and enforceability unclear.
- **Rollback:** Weak if competitors treat pause rhetoric as marketing while continuing capability climb.

### Case: Non-human corporation domicile

- **Agent:** AI-operated firm under Argentine "nonhuman corporation" category.
- **Objective:** Legal home for autonomous economic agents; attract hyperscaler and agent-native capital.
- **Authority:** Milei legal innovation; comparative fast-followers (El Salvador, UAE per panel).
- **Observability:** Op-ed and policy announcements visible; enforcement and cross-border recognition TBD.
- **Rollback:** Jurisdictional — regimes can be copied, contested, or isolated.
