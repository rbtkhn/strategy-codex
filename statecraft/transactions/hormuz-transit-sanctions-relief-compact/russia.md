## State Interest

Russia's durable interest is to weaken U.S. coercive primacy and strengthen multipolar bargaining while preserving optionality with Iran, China, India, the Gulf, and Europe.

## Leverage / Constraint

- Leverage being spent: Diplomatic backing, energy coordination, military-technical support, sanctions workarounds, and mediator credibility.
- Constraint that limits the leverage: Russia must avoid alienating India, overburdening China, or inheriting Iranian escalation costs.
- Volatility note: If Iran's coercive leverage rises but Gulf disruption harms partners, Russia should push controlled settlement rather than maximal pressure.

## Richelieu Validation

- Institutional carrier: Foreign ministry, energy ministries, security council channels, BRICS / SCO-adjacent forums, and mediator nodes.
- Capacity: Diplomatic sequencing, energy substitution, technical support, and backchannel discipline.
- Continuity risk: Tactical anti-U.S. advantage may tempt overcommitment.
- Long-pressure burden: Russia must preserve military and diplomatic bandwidth for its primary security theaters.

## Bismarck Validation

- Limited aim: Convert U.S. overextension into recognition of multipolar bargaining, not total ownership of the Hormuz crisis.
- Actor to isolate: U.S. escalation maximalists.
- Actor to reassure, restrain, or keep neutral: China, India, Gulf states, Oman, and Iran.
- Alliance geometry: Support Iran enough to break unilateral U.S. coercion; restrain Iran enough to protect Russia's wider Eurasian geometry.
- Restraint after advantage: Encourage reopening when it converts pressure into durable concessions.

## Instrument Text

Draft clause:

> Russia shall support a mediator-backed Hormuz transit compact that pairs Iranian transit restraint with phased sanctions relief and non-regime-change assurances, while reserving the right to provide lawful economic and technical support to Iran if the compact is violated by renewed coercion. Russia shall not assume enforcement obligations that bind Moscow to unilateral escalation decisions by any party.

## Mirror Test

- America: This gives Russia a mediator role while rewarding Iranian pressure.
- Russia: This must not turn into an Iranian blank check.
- China: This is acceptable only if it reduces energy disorder.
- Iran: This may restrain Iran before enough relief is secured.

## Falsifiers / Revisit Triggers

- Falsifier: Russia publicly distances from Iran's Hormuz position or refuses any mediator role.
- Revisit trigger: China pressures for reopening, India objects, Gulf states invite Russian mediation, or U.S. escalates interdiction.
- Scoreboard movement: Review if Pape leverage rises but Mearsheimer balancing pressure starts isolating Iran and Russia together.
- Actor signal: Russian mediation offer, BRICS / SCO language, energy substitution package, or explicit restraint message to Tehran.
