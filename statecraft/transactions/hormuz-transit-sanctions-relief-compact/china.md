## State Interest

China's durable interest is to reopen predictable energy flows while reducing U.S. coercive reach and avoiding public ownership of Iran's escalation.

## Leverage / Constraint

- Leverage being spent: Energy purchases, finance, insurance alternatives, port and shipping capacity, diplomatic convening, and quiet pressure on partners.
- Constraint that limits the leverage: China is energy-exposed and cannot let symbolic multipolar gain become industrial disorder.
- Volatility note: Pape-style crisis leverage may benefit China geopolitically, but only until shortages threaten Chinese stability.

## Richelieu Validation

- Institutional carrier: Foreign ministry, energy firms, state banks, insurers, port operators, shipping firms, and regional forums.
- Capacity: Payment channels, insurance guarantees, cargo prioritization, quiet diplomatic pressure, and supply-chain monitoring.
- Continuity risk: Public credit can turn China from stabilizer into responsible owner of the crisis.
- Long-pressure burden: China must stabilize without inheriting U.S.-style policing obligations.

## Bismarck Validation

- Limited aim: Restore energy predictability while preserving bargaining leverage against U.S. coercion.
- Actor to isolate: Actors pushing uncontrolled escalation or blockade expansion.
- Actor to reassure, restrain, or keep neutral: Iran, Gulf states, India, Russia, and major importers.
- Alliance geometry: Keep Iran supported but not unconstrained; keep Russia aligned but not dominant over the settlement.
- Restraint after advantage: Avoid triumphal language; let the compact look like order restoration.

## Instrument Text

Draft clause:

> China shall support a neutral Hormuz transit compact through energy-purchase commitments, shipping-insurance stabilization, and payment-channel guarantees conditioned on predictable passage for non-belligerent commercial vessels. China shall encourage phased sanctions relief and non-regime-change assurances while reserving quiet consultation rights if transit disruption, interdiction, or unilateral escalation threatens energy stability.

## Mirror Test

- America: This lets China become the indispensable stabilizer while weakening U.S. control.
- Russia: This may close the pressure window before the U.S. pays a larger strategic price.
- China: This must not make Beijing the guarantor of every Gulf violation.
- Iran: This may manage Iran's leverage more than recognize it.

## Falsifiers / Revisit Triggers

- Falsifier: China chooses public neutrality and refuses insurance, payment, or energy-stability involvement.
- Revisit trigger: Chinese imports, insurance, or payment channels shift; Gulf states seek Chinese mediation; Iran changes transit terms.
- Scoreboard movement: Review if Pape leverage keeps rising but Chinese industrial stress rises faster.
- Actor signal: Chinese energy compact, yuan settlement mechanism, insurance alternative, or quiet pressure on Tehran.
