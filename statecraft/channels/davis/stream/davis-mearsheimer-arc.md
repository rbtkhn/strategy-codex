## Why this guest run matters

John Mearsheimer matters here less as a generic realist brand than as a recurring war-feasibility, coercive-limits, and bargaining-geometry voice inside a host stream otherwise centered on military practicality, escalation pressure, and U.S. policy risk.

Inside the Davis stream, that makes the run especially useful when the notebook needs continuity between:

- military feasibility and structural political limits
- bargaining space and escalation ladders
- Ukraine settlement logic and Iran-war coercion logic
- what Washington can threaten and what it can actually sustain

What makes this a real speaker arc is that the same host x guest shape keeps returning:

- Davis frames the crisis through force posture, operational reality, and escalation risk
- Mearsheimer translates that crisis into structural incentives, bargaining leverage, and the limits of coercive power
- the lane repeatedly turns war talk into an argument about whether the United States can actually impose a favorable outcome, or is instead trapped by structure, inventories, and political geometry

## Orthogonality guide

Quick separation rule:

- `Davis x Mearsheimer` is the feasibility, coercive-limits, and bargaining-geometry lane.
- `Diesen x Mearsheimer` is the structural-realist architecture and order-transition lane.
- `Napolitano x Mearsheimer` is the defeat-accounting and self-entrapment lane.

Do not use this arc as a shortcut for broad realist world-order explanation or Napolitano's sharper defeat-accounting register. Its distinctive job is to test whether coercive escalation can still produce a politically usable outcome.
