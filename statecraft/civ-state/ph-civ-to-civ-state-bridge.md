# PH-CIV to CIV-STATE Bridge

WORK only; not Record.

This note explains the method link between `ph-civ` and `civ-state`.

`ph-civ` is the public civilizational source world. `civ-state` is the local civilization-state source-memory substrate used for drafting. They align in substance, but they do different work. `ph-civ` turns historical material into public pattern and live pressure reading. `civ-state` turns that pattern into continuity-bearing comparative statecraft memory.

## Asymmetry

- `ph-civ` is public, source-bounded, chaptered, and route-facing.
- `civ-state` is compact, comparative, continuity-first, and drafting-facing.
- `ph-civ` may orient `civ-state`, but `civ-state` should not silently rewrite `ph-civ`.
- `civ-state` may stage explicit upstream review candidates, but ambient drift between the two layers is not allowed.

Short form:

`ph-civ exposes -> civ-state distills -> statecraft drafts`

## What Carries Across

The bridge is not transcript transfer. It is mechanism transfer.

What may cross from `ph-civ` into `civ-state`:

- stable civilizational patterns
- continuity arguments that survive beyond one lecture moment
- geography as pressure and carrying condition
- legitimacy structure
- current-carrier logic
- bounded failure modes and counterweights

What should usually stay in `ph-civ` unless explicitly translated:

- lecture sequencing
- commentary rhetoric
- source-capture scaffolding
- chapter-specific review caveats
- unstable forecasts tied to a narrow news moment

## Conversion Rule

Promote a `ph-civ` insight into `civ-state` only when it can be rewritten as a reusable civilization-state mechanism.

Use this test:

1. Identify the public `ph-civ` pattern, chapter, or pressure surface.
2. Strip away lecture-local rhetoric and transcript scaffolding.
3. Ask what continuity, legitimacy, geography, or carrier mechanism actually survives.
4. Rewrite that mechanism in compact comparative form.
5. Add a counterweight so the pattern does not harden into propaganda or false analogy.
6. Place it in the appropriate `civ-state` layer: volume, `civilization-<civ>`, `empire-<civ>`, `statecraft-<civ>`, `geo-strategy-<civ>`, `game-theory-<civ>`, `secret-history-<civ>`, sacred grammar, state-memory, or review queue.

If the result is still mainly a commentary on a lecture, it belongs in `ph-civ`. If it has become a reusable civilization-state mechanism, it may belong in `civ-state`.

## Method Map

The bridge usually works like this:

- `ph-civ` chapter or card -> `civ-state` source object
- `ph-civ` civilizational pattern -> `civilization-<civ>` opening essay or sacred-grammar note
- `ph-civ` empire or projection pattern -> `empire-<civ>` outward-instrument essay
- `ph-civ` live diplomatic synthesis -> `statecraft-<civ>` present-tense statesman's guide surface
- `ph-civ` geo-strategy pressure -> `civ-state` `geo-strategy-<civ>` carrying-condition layer
- `ph-civ` game-theory pressure -> `civ-state` `game-theory-<civ>` incentive and interaction layer
- `ph-civ` secret-history pressure -> `civ-state` `secret-history-<civ>` hidden-inheritance and activation layer
- `ph-civ` present-tense pressure reading -> current-carrier or failure-mode diagnosis
- `ph-civ` route-level synthesis -> comparative retrieval or transaction hook

This is why the two shelves feel aligned without being duplicates. `ph-civ` remains the public teaching world. `civ-state` keeps only the parts that can survive compression into operator memory.

## Geo-Strategy Example

The clearest bridge is `geo-strategy`.

In `ph-civ`, geo-strategy means geography becoming live condition through institutions, resources, chokepoints, legitimacy, and maintenance burdens. In `civ-state`, geo-strategy means the geographic pressure and carrying conditions through which a sovereignty chain survives, expands, fractures, or recomposes.

So the translation rule is:

- public pressure reading in `ph-civ`
- becomes continuity-bearing carrying logic in `civ-state`

The two are aligned, but the destination form changes. `ph-civ` explains. `civ-state` stores. `statecraft` applies.

The same compression rule now applies across the civilization-facing and application families:

- `ph-civ` civilization pattern becomes a civilization-state's continuity-bearing opening argument in `civilization-<civ>`
- `ph-civ` empire pattern becomes a civilization-state's projection and overreach argument in `empire-<civ>`
- `ph-civ` present-tense diplomatic pattern becomes a civilization-state's live statecraft synthesis in `statecraft-<civ>`
- `ph-civ` Game Theory becomes a civilization-state's recurring incentive and interaction structure
- `ph-civ` Secret History becomes a civilization-state's hidden memory and symbolic activation structure

## When To Use Which

Use `ph-civ` when you need:

- public source capture
- chaptered exposition
- reader-facing orientation
- source-bounded commentary
- route-level civilizational pressure framing

Use `civ-state` when you need:

- compact comparative retrieval
- sovereign continuity diagnosis
- current-carrier framing
- counterweights against analogy drift
- drafting-ready memory for statecraft instruments
