## Pattern

Iran's Hormuz leverage is legitimate only when it can be presented as sovereign regulation of a vital passage, not as permanent veto over global commerce. Geography gives Iran systemic bargaining power; civilizational memory requires that restraint be recognized as dignity-preserving sovereignty rather than surrender; empire risk appears when chokepoint leverage becomes overused disruption that alienates partners and confirms adversary claims.

Short form:

`geography creates leverage -> dignity requires recognition -> restraint must be reciprocal -> overuse degrades sovereignty`

## Arc Pattern

- **Origin:** Persian / Iranian state memory joins plateau endurance, Gulf access, and recognition as a sovereign actor rather than an administered object.
- **Continuity:** Successive Iranian state forms inherit the same terrain problem: outside powers can pressure Iran through energy, ports, sanctions, and maritime access, but cannot erase Hormuz salience.
- **Transformation:** Sanctions, missile and drone deterrence, nuclear latency, and partner channels turn geography into a coupled bargaining object: passage, coercive signaling, and negotiation-room leverage move together.
- **Current carrier:** Iranian foreign ministry, Supreme National Security Council, IRGC / maritime forces, port authorities, energy institutions, mediator channels, and sanctions-relief mechanisms.
- **Failure mode:** Iran overuses Hormuz, splits its announced commercial-passage line from material conditions, alienates China / India / Gulf states, or lets resistance language block a governable settlement.

## Statecraft Use

Use this object when drafting or testing an Iran-lane instrument involving:

- safe commercial transit through Hormuz
- sanctions relief tied to observable restraint
- non-regime-change or non-attack assurances
- reciprocal maritime notification and complaint channels
- security recognition that can be carried by Iranian state institutions
- mediator-visible compliance windows

The statecraft test is whether Iran can convert disruption capacity into recognized restraint before leverage becomes isolation.

## Counterweight

The same pattern becomes dangerous when Hormuz is treated as a reusable coercive theater without an off-ramp. Full closure, ambiguous interference, or uncoupled coercive signaling may prove Iran can impose costs, but it can also destroy the value of the bargaining asset by making partners price Iran as a source of permanent uncertainty.

Counterweight rule:

`controlled transit restraint increases recognition value; uncontrolled chokepoint pressure consumes it`

## Transaction Hooks

- **Clause shape:** Renewable safe-transit guarantee tied to reciprocal security assurances, phased sanctions relief, complaint handling, and suspension standards.
- **Recognition hook:** Name Iran as a necessary sovereign security actor in the Gulf rather than a compliance object.
- **Relief hook:** Make relief observable and sequenced before Iran spends irreversible leverage.
- **Verification hook:** Use mediator-visible transit notices, complaint contacts, and compliance records rather than confession-style verification.
- **Red-line warning:** Transit reopening without recognition reads as capitulation pressure; recognition without observable restraint reads as status theater.
- **Review trigger:** Revisit every 30 days or whenever interdiction, sanctions reversal, transit interference, or partner distancing changes the bargaining value of the object.
