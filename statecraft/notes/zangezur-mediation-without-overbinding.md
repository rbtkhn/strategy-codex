# Zangezur Mediation Without Overbinding


This is the first Russia-native local exemplar for the Russia transaction bench. It tests whether Moscow can preserve mediator relevance, strategic depth, and corridor influence without letting the object harden into permanent coercive ownership.

## Transaction Name

Zangezur mediation without overbinding.

## State Lane

`russia`

## Source Inputs

- Shared branch provenance: [Russia - Zangezur Transit / Sovereignty Framework](../../transactions/zangezur-transit-sovereignty-framework/russia.md)
- Scoreboards used: Mearsheimer for buffer and overextension risk; Pape for corridor leverage
- Prior statecraft drafts: Russia crisis test casebook corridor entry

## Realism / Incentive Filter

Russia benefits from mediator relevance and security depth, but loses if the corridor becomes an open-ended command obligation or a trigger for Iran, Turkey, Armenia, and outside powers to align against Moscow.

## Historical Continuity

Russian continuity concern: borderland access and buffers matter, but unlimited control can recreate the encirclement pressure Russia claims it is preventing.

## Civilization / Empire Helix Input

- Civilization input: encirclement and security-depth memory
- Empire input: mediator leverage, borderland influence, and recognition formulas
- Statecraft regulation: optionality, reassurance, and reciprocal limits
- Relevant lane helix: [Russia helix](../helix.md)

## Pattern / Narrative Conversion

- Civilizational pattern or narrative: strategic depth without severance
- Authority: local parties plus reviewable mediator framework
- Restraint: no permanent coercive command, no corridor ownership
- Settlement: monitored transit with reciprocal security limits and periodic review

## State Interest

Preserve mediator leverage and regional depth without becoming hostage to corridor escalation.

## Leverage / Constraint

- Leverage being spent: diplomacy, security links, recognition formulas, and corridor management
- Constraint that limits the leverage: overbinding, partner distrust, and balancing pressure
- Volatility note: corridor instability may raise Russian relevance while worsening structural balancing pressure

## Richelieu Validation

Institutional carrier: foreign ministry, security council, and mediator structures that can survive tactical shifts.

## Bismarck Validation

Limited aim: keep a role in settlement, not own the corridor. Reassure Iran and Armenia that access is not territorial severance and reassure Turkey that mediation is not permanent Russian command.

## Instrument Text

Russia shall support a corridor formula limited to transit, inspection, notification, and periodic mediator review, with reciprocal non-basing commitments and explicit language that access shall not become territorial severance or permanent coercive command.

## Mirror Test

- America: Is Russia using mediation as control?
- Russia: Does this preserve optionality without ownership?
- China: Does this stabilize route investment without bloc hardening?
- Iran: Does this block extraterritorial corridor logic?

## Falsifiers / Revisit Triggers

- Falsifier: Russian role requires permanent coercive command or unilateral corridor ownership
- Revisit trigger: deployment escalation, party rejection, or mediator review collapse
- Scoreboard movement that would require review: overextension risk rises or balancing pressure hardens
- Actor defection or compliance signal: local acceptance of reciprocal limits
