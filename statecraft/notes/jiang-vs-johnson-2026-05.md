# Jiang vs Johnson - May 2026

This note captures the main disagreement between **Jiang Xueqin** and **Larry Johnson** in the May 2026 Iran / Middle East escalation window.

## Main Split

The headline split is over **what the Iran war is for** and **whether it ends in Israeli regional supremacy or imperial overreach and failure**.

Jiang's line:

- The war is part of a larger imperial strategy.
- The US empire is still strategically purposeful even when it looks chaotic.
- Israel can emerge as the last standing regional power and move toward a **Greater Israel** outcome.
- Ground escalation, a national draft, and long-war imperial management remain thinkable if needed.

Johnson's line:

- The US and Israel are not executing a viable master plan; they are degrading their own position.
- Iran is stronger politically and strategically than Washington admits.
- Israel cannot even fully subdue Gaza or Hezbollah, so **Greater Israel** as a near-term regional conquest thesis is delusional.
- US ground-war / draft scenarios against Iran are logistically absurd and militarily unserious.

## Specific Disagreements

### 1. Greater Israel

This is the sharpest disagreement.

Jiang presents Greater Israel as a plausible strategic end-state once the US exits and regional opponents are destroyed or exhausted.

Johnson rejects this flatly. His local argument is simple:

- Israel has not demonstrated the capacity to decisively control Gaza.
- Israel is still taking serious friction from Hezbollah.
- A state struggling on those fronts is not positioned to dominate Turkey, Syria, Iraq, Jordan, Saudi Arabia, and the wider region.

So the disagreement is not about tone alone. It is about whether **Israeli military power is expansive and scalable** or **already near its real limit**.

### 2. The Nature of American Power

Jiang treats present American behavior as the action of an empire still trying to preserve structural dominance through managed chaos, choke points, debt absorption, subcontracted wars, and selective bargains.

Johnson treats present American behavior as strategically incoherent and increasingly detached from military and economic reality.

Short form:

- Jiang: **empire still has a plan**
- Johnson: **empire is failing in real time**

### 3. China

Jiang's line leaves more room for China to behave short-term, transactionally, and pragmatically even while the US seeks to subordinate it.

Johnson's line gives more weight to Chinese structural strength and to the idea that China is not going to rescue Washington from its own strategic trap.

This creates a second-order disagreement:

- Jiang is more open to **US-China bargaining inside imperial decline**
- Johnson is more focused on **US weakness relative to China and the failure of coercive leverage**

### 4. Escalation and Draft Logic

Jiang allows for scenarios where the US expands the war through a draft, ground-force preparation, and long-war management.

Johnson argues those scenarios collapse under basic force, logistics, and basing math. His objection is not only moral or political; it is operational.

## Deeper Pattern

The deeper worldview difference is:

- **Jiang** tends toward large civilizational and systemic scripting, where imperial elites still possess meaningful steering power even inside decline.
- **Johnson** tends toward material constraints, ORBAT-style realism, and operational friction, where bad assumptions and degraded capacity matter more than grand script.

That means Jiang often sounds stronger on **macro-intent** and Johnson stronger on **practical execution limits**.
