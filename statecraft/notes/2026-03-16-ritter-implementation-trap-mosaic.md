## How the three legs latch

### Pape → Ritter

Pape says center of gravity moves to Hormuz economics because military decapitation did not close the war. Ritter adds the **implementation corollary**: if Hormuz is the lever, the U.S. cannot reliably **execute** Hormuz reopening by naval force — so trap pressure lacks a mechanical off-ramp.

- Pape chain: "Control Hormuz → economic shocks → political pressure"
- Ritter constraint: coercer cannot unilaterally **restore** Hormuz flow for Western insurers even if it wants to — allies talk to Iran (Ritter: "best way… is what the Europeans are doing, talking to the Iranians")

Trap without implementation leg risks sounding like **markets alone** will force Trump to concede. Ritter says **military options to fix markets are not in inventory.**

### Crooke → Ritter

Crooke says Iran runs phased mosaic war — intercept drain, silo missiles, gating not total blockade. Ritter supplies **why Western force structure cannot answer each phase**:

- Phase 2–3 (radar/intercept exhaustion): Johnson inventory math; Ritter on unsustainable CAP and carrier standoff.
- Phase 4 (heavy missiles): airfields at risk; no sustained suppression without ground occupation Ritter treats as invasion-scale.
- Crooke Hormuz occupation math (2,000 km, honeycombed coast) = Ritter littoral combat read in Marine-officer voice.

Mosaic without implementation leg risks sounding **purely cognitive** (measurement error). Ritter proves **measurement error is reinforced by material limits.**

### Ritter → Pape + Crooke (feedback)

Implementation failure **feeds** trap ratchet (no cheap exit) and **validates** mosaic (opponent designed for duration). Seventeen-day gap (Ritter/Davis/Pape) is where all three legs intersect on March 16.
