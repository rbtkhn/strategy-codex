## Why this guest run matters

Ted Postol matters here less as a generic military critic than as a recurring **technical-destruction and deterrence-correction** voice inside a host stream that presses live Iran-Israel and missile-defense crisis questions hard.

Inside the Dialogue Works stream, that makes the run especially useful when the notebook needs continuity between:

- dramatic escalation claims and actual engineering constraint
- air-defense mythology and physical interception limits
- bunker-busting rhetoric and the real problem of penetration, survivability, and repair
- headline war fear and the slower logic of deterrence failure or technical overclaim

What makes this a real speaker arc is that the same host x guest shape keeps returning:

- Alkorshid presses the present-tense crisis as immediate military risk and emotional pressure surface
- Postol answers by stripping the event back to mechanism, physics, and real system capability
- the lane repeatedly turns strategic anxiety into a question of what radars, interceptors, missiles, and hardened targets can actually do

## Orthogonality guide

Quick separation rule:

- `Dialogue Works x Postol` is the live-pressure, technical-destruction, and deterrence-correction lane.
- `Davis x Postol` is the engineering-constraint and destructive-capacity-truth lane.
- `Diesen x Postol` is the deterrence-consequence and escalation-horizon lane.

Do not use this arc as a shortcut for longer-form engineering adjudication or order-level deterrence architecture. Its distinctive job is to strip live military panic back to radars, interceptors, bunker limits, and deterrence realism under current-event pressure.

## Routing use

Use this speaker arc when an Alkorshid ingest or batch pass is doing one or more of the following:

- converting war panic into missile-effects, radar, and interceptor realism
- testing whether air-defense or bunker-busting claims are physically credible
- reading deterrence through actual system performance rather than prestige or narrative
- asking whether a crisis is being misdescribed because the engineering layer is missing
