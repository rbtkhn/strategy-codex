## State Interest

America's durable interest is to end the Iran crisis without proving that decapitation is the only way Washington bargains or that Iran can only obtain respect through maximal resistance.

## Leverage / Constraint

- Leverage being spent: Sanctions relief, no-regime-change assurances, no-partition language, channel protection, mediator support, and restraint of allied escalation.
- Constraint that limits the leverage: If Washington appears to validate leadership killing as leverage, Iranian institutions have incentive to harden, decentralize, and reject any settlement as surrender.
- Volatility note: Pape may show high Iranian coercive leverage while Mearsheimer warns that American overextension rises; that combination favors channel protection over maximalist escalation.

## Richelieu Validation

- Institutional carrier: President, State Department, Treasury, Congress, intelligence community, allied liaison cells, and mediator secretariat.
- Capacity: Sanctions waiver mechanics, protected-channel orders, allied deconfliction, congressional notification, and mediator verification.
- Continuity risk: Executive volatility or donor / allied pressure can turn a protected channel into a tactical pause before renewed decapitation.
- Long-pressure burden: America must restrain spoilers long enough for Iran to believe the assurance is not a trap.

## Bismarck Validation

- Limited aim: Protect channels and stop regime-change signaling; do not demand immediate comprehensive settlement.
- Actor to isolate: Decapitation advocates, partition advocates, and allied spoilers who profit from keeping the war open.
- Actor to reassure, restrain, or keep neutral: Iran, Oman, Gulf states, Congress, China, Russia, and energy-market actors.
- Alliance geometry: Keep allies inside a de-escalation frame by making channel protection a prerequisite for any later negotiation, not a concession to Iran alone.
- Restraint after advantage: If Iran accepts protected-channel talks, do not convert the opening into humiliation theater.

## Instrument Text

Draft clause:

> 1. The United States shall publicly affirm that this framework is not a regime-change or partition instrument and that its purpose is bounded de-escalation, civilian continuity, and restored bargaining authority.
>
> 2. Upon activation, the United States, through the Secretary of State and the designated mediator, shall identify protected diplomatic, mediator, and high-authority communication carriers whose physical security, communications access, and public standing shall not be deliberately degraded while the framework is active.
>
> 3. The United States shall not target, facilitate targeting of, sanction into unusability, or publicly humiliate named protected carriers while the framework is active. Any strike, proxy action, or public threat against those carriers shall be treated as a material breach requiring immediate mediator notice.
>
> 4. Activation of the protected-channel designation shall be paired with one reversible but visible first-step measure: a narrow humanitarian or technical sanctions waiver, a temporary deconfliction assurance, or another named marker sufficient to show that channel protection is not merely process without reciprocity.
>
> 5. Alleged violations shall first trigger confidential mediator notice, urgent consultation, congressional notification, and a written legal-basis review before any sanctions snapback or military escalation, except where a documented force-protection override is invoked under the operational annex and reported immediately after use.

## First-step package

Minimum American opening package:

- public no-regime-change / no-partition assurance
- named protected diplomatic and mediator carriers
- material-breach rule for attacks on those carriers
- one reversible but visible first-step relief or deconfliction marker
- congressional notification and short review clock so the instrument reads as bounded command, not executive drift

## Mirror Test

- America: Does this look like retreat after failed coercion?
- Russia: Does this preserve too much U.S. control over the end-state language?
- China: Does this stabilize energy and bargaining enough to justify support?
- Iran: Is the assurance credible after leadership and negotiator targeting?

## Falsifiers / Revisit Triggers

- Falsifier: Iran collapses because of decapitation, or accepts talks without any channel protection or no-regime-change language.
- Revisit trigger: New assassination attempt, mediator proposal, U.S. public assurance, congressional war-powers move, or Israeli strike on a bargaining figure.
- Scoreboard movement: Review if Pape leverage falls because Hormuz and Iranian retaliation lose salience, or if Mearsheimer overextension warnings intensify.
- Actor signal: Formal no-regime-change statement, protected envoy appointment, sanctions waiver, Israeli objection, or Iranian refusal.
