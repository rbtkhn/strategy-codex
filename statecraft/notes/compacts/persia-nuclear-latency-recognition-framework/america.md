## Civilization / Empire Helix Input

- Civilization input: American legitimacy needs lawful authority, nonproliferation credibility, allied confidence, and domestic consent.
- Empire input: America wants control over sanctions, inspection terms, military deterrence, coalition alignment, and escalation timing.
- Statecraft regulation: Contain latency without making hidden regime change the real instrument.
- Relevant lane helix: [America helix](../../../america/helix.md).

## State Interest

America's durable interest is to prevent Iranian breakout and regional proliferation without triggering a wider war that proves coercion cannot restore order.

## Leverage / Constraint

- Leverage being spent: Sanctions relief, inspection recognition, security assurances, allied guarantees, and military deterrence.
- Constraint that limits the leverage: Preventive attack may accelerate weaponization, fracture allies, and deepen Russia / China support for Iran.
- Volatility note: If Pape escalation clocks rise while Mearsheimer overextension warnings rise, America needs verification architecture rather than additional coercive theater.

## Richelieu Validation

- Institutional carrier: State Department, Treasury, IAEA-facing channels, Congress, allied guarantors, and mediator secretariat.
- Capacity: Sanctions waivers, inspection protocols, congressional reporting, allied enforcement, and violation review.
- Continuity risk: Presidential impulse can destroy a verification bargain if relief is reversible at whim.
- Long-pressure burden: America must carry nonproliferation credibility without turning every threshold state into a war target.

## Bismarck Validation

- Limited aim: Freeze weaponization risk and preserve inspection access; do not demand regime transformation.
- Actor to isolate: Breakout advocates and preventive-war maximalists.
- Actor to reassure, restrain, or keep neutral: Gulf states, Israel, Europe, China, Russia, and Congress.
- Alliance geometry: Make allies share verification costs and accept relief obligations.
- Restraint after advantage: If access improves, do not add maximal demands that collapse compliance.

## Instrument Text

Draft clause:

> The United States shall support phased sanctions relief and non-regime-change assurances in exchange for Iran's renewable commitment not to weaponize nuclear material, not to expel agreed inspectors, and not to enrich beyond specified threshold limits without prior review. Any snapback shall require documented violation, allied consultation, and a 14-day urgent consultation period unless direct weaponization evidence is presented.

## Mirror Test

- America: Does this concede too much to latency?
- Russia: Does this preserve U.S. control over the verification frame?
- China: Does this stabilize the region or keep sanctions leverage too volatile?
- Iran: Does this recognize Iran's rights, or only freeze vulnerability?

## Falsifiers / Revisit Triggers

- Falsifier: Iran openly chooses weaponization or rejects inspection access under any relief formula.
- Revisit trigger: U.S. attack threat, IAEA access shift, sanctions offer, Gulf guarantee, or Israeli unilateral action.
- Scoreboard movement: Review if Pape escalation risk rises or Mearsheimer balancing pressure makes containment impossible.
- Actor signal: Congressional authorization, allied guarantee, Iranian inspection commitment, or mediator proposal.
