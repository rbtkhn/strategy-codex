# Davis x Postol speaker arc

Purpose: compact reuse note for the `Davis x Postol` run inside the Davis stream. This is not a substitute for the full raw-input shelf. It exists so future routing can quickly answer: what kind of Postol lane Davis elicits, which full-form episodes matter most, and why this has become one of the clearest recurring Postol branches.

## Why this guest run matters

Ted Postol matters here less as a generic anti-war voice than as a recurring **technical-destruction, deterrence-realism, and missile-effects** lane inside a host stream otherwise centered on military feasibility, bargaining pressure, and escalation management.

Inside the Davis stream, that makes the run especially useful when the notebook needs continuity between:

- feasibility claims and actual system performance
- bunker-buster or missile-defense rhetoric and what can physically be penetrated or intercepted
- Iran-war panic and the engineering limits on what either side can really achieve
- nuclear or radar escalation and the actual deterrence consequences of technical misreading

What makes this a real speaker arc is that the same host x guest shape keeps returning:

- Davis frames the crisis through feasibility, bargaining, and risk
- Postol answers by stripping the crisis down to mechanism, system performance, and physical constraint
- the lane repeatedly turns military argument into a question of destructive capacity, survivability, radar logic, and interceptor truth

## Orthogonality guide

Quick separation rule:

- `Davis x Postol` is the engineering-constraint and destructive-capacity-truth lane.
- `Dialogue Works x Postol` is the live-pressure, technical-destruction, and deterrence-correction lane.
- `Diesen x Postol` is the deterrence-consequence and escalation-horizon lane.

Do not use this arc as a shortcut for live crisis narration or wider order-level nuclear consequence. Its distinctive job is to test whether strikes, bunkers, radars, and missile defenses can physically do what public rhetoric claims.

## Routing use
