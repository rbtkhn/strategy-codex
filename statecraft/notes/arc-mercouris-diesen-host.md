## Orthogonality guide

This file should be read as the **Diesen-pressure branch** of Mercouris, not as a generic Europe file and not as a proxy for the native Mercouris stream.

Quick separation rule:

- `Diesen x Mercouris` = order-transition and legitimacy-crisis pressure on Mercouris's institutional read
- [arc-mercouris-continuity.md](arc-mercouris-continuity.md = native bounded arc shape
- [arc-mercouris-continuity-threads.md](../../../statecraft/voices/mercouris/arc-mercouris-continuity-threads.md = canonical recurring-strand atlas

If a Mercouris item matters mainly because Diesen is asking what Europe's crisis means for order transition, sovereignty, and post-unipolar placement, it belongs here. If it matters mainly because Mercouris is extending one of his native recurring strands independent of host pressure, prefer the native arc or thread atlas.

## Why this guest run matters

Alexander Mercouris matters here less as a generic European politics commentator than as a recurring **institutional-legitimacy and diplomatic-sequencing** voice. Inside the Diesen stream, that makes the appearance useful when the notebook needs continuity between:

- European legitimacy crisis and foreign-policy overextension
- elite denationalization and domestic political volatility
- Ukraine/Russia policy fixation and economic stagnation
- Europe as possible Eurasian backwater versus Europe as western Eurasian entry point

What makes this a real host-local arc is the fit between the two objects:

- Diesen frames the crisis through order transition, multipolarity, and Europe's place after unipolarity.
- Mercouris translates that frame into concrete British, German, French, and EU political legitimacy problems.
- The lane turns macro order-transition language into a diagnosis of domestic institutional brittleness.

## Routing use

Use this speaker arc when a Diesen ingest or synthesis pass is doing one or more of the following:

- reading Europe through legitimacy crisis rather than only election arithmetic
- connecting Ukraine/Russia policy fixation to domestic stagnation and political anger
- comparing Britain's Churchillian foreign-policy myth with Germany's postwar Russia relationship
- testing whether Europe can still adapt to a post-American or post-Western order
- routing the same raw input into both the Diesen host shelf and the Mercouris speaker shelf
