## Realism / Incentive Filter

- Does the alleged actor benefit more than it loses? America benefits from emergency continuity but loses if private platform control or automated permissions replace constitutional authority.
- Is the target something the actor still depends on? America still depends on lawful identity continuity for benefits, banking, travel, courts, payroll, military service, and command legitimacy.
- Is the act consistent with demonstrated behavior, or only with attributed capability? The failure mode is less hostile intent than brittle dependence, emergency improvisation, and private-system capture under pressure.
- Accident / negligence / private actor / third-party alternative: platform outage, vendor lock, cyber incident, bad model deployment, emergency suspension cascade, or fragmented public-private command.
- Evidence needed to distinguish intent from exploitation: outage logs, denial classes, vendor permissions, emergency orders, appeal failure data, state-federal conflict, and manual-fallback viability.
- Reclassified premise if needed: even absent sabotage, identity failure becomes a sovereign crisis once appeal, rollback, or manual continuity disappears.

## Solvency / Legitimacy Filter

- Protected domestic obligation: benefits, payroll, banking access, legal standing, travel, and service continuity.
- Creditor or patron with extraction leverage: large platforms, cloud vendors, payment rails, and data intermediaries that can become de facto sovereign choke points.
- Sovereign asset at risk of fire-sale transfer: identity rails, credential authority, data custody, and public-permission infrastructure.
- Legitimacy-destroying reform risk: emergency automation that locks citizens out without appeal or due process.
- Minimum public-order floor: basic identity recognition, payment continuity, and mobility rights must survive the disruption.
- Face-saving assistance phrase: continuity protocol, audit restoration, fallback certification, or emergency civil continuity authority.

- Limited aim: continuity and audit, not permanent emergency identity regime.
- Actor to isolate: actors pushing irreversible platform sovereignty, opaque automated lockout, or privatized emergency command.

- Alliance geometry: preserve interoperability without outsourcing sovereign identity authority to platform or foreign-standard lock-in.

- Off-ramp: public audit, manual restoration, sunset, rollback, and appeal completion.

## Instrument Text

Draft clause:

> The United States shall establish an emergency identity-continuity protocol that preserves benefits, banking, travel, legal standing, and service continuity through public authority, independent audit, manual fallback, appeal rights, and rollback of automated permission changes after the crisis. No platform or contractor operating under this protocol shall acquire irreversible authority to suspend personhood, payment access, mobility, or claims rights without a public legal basis, a time-bounded review clock, and a documented restoration path.
