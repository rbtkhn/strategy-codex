## Provisional Thesis

If Pope Leo is trying to speak seriously about AI, the `statecraft` question is
not mainly whether the Church has a useful tech ethic. It is whether Rome is
trying to preserve a human office of judgment against a coming world in which
advice, administration, persuasion, and even moral mediation are being made
machine-scalable.

That makes this a `statecraft` object whenever the issue becomes:

- who still carries authority
- which offices remain non-delegable
- how legitimacy survives synthetic mediation
- whether Vatican speech is acting as a civilizational counterclaim to a
  machine-administered order

## Why This Belongs In Statecraft

The same Leo text can generate two different valid essays.

`Singularity` should ask:

- what AI does to personhood, authorship, and mediation

`Statecraft` should ask:

- what happens when one of the oldest surviving offices in the world attempts
  to mark limits on machine substitution

That is a different question because Rome is not merely commenting on
technology. It is speaking as:

- a civilizational residue
- a transnational office
- a legitimacy-bearing institution
- a rival anthropology to convenience-native technical order

## What This Version Owns

The `statecraft` version should stay centered on:

- papal office under acceleration
- Rome as a surviving authority form
- legitimacy versus convenience
- advisory substitution as a pressure on public judgment
- the Vatican as a carrier that can still deny total machine succession

It should **not** become the main repo owner of:

- the deeper anthropology of mediation
- whether synthetic language can count as witness
- office as a question of formation rather than legitimacy
- general acceleration-side theory of AI and personhood

Those belong in the `singularity` version.

## The Strongest Statecraft Seam

The most promising statecraft seam is not:

```text
the Church comments on AI
```

It is:

```text
one of the world's oldest legitimacy-bearing offices may be trying to say
that machine competence is not enough to inherit human judgment
```

That matters because the live global pressure is not only automation.

It is the thinning of:

- sovereign judgment
- professional authority
- public reasoning
- institutional answerability

If Leo frames AI in those terms, then his intervention becomes part of a larger
statecraft problem:

- can authority survive acceleration without becoming either ornamental or
  machine-administered

## Likely Argument Paths

The strongest eventual full version will probably need to choose among these
statecraft claims:

1. Rome is trying to preserve human office against synthetic succession.
2. The Vatican is offering a legitimacy-language critique of AI that secular
   governance systems currently lack.
3. Pope Leo is implicitly contesting a civilizational order in which scale and
   convenience outrank formation and answerability.
4. The Church is marking a boundary around the kinds of judgment no system
   should inherit merely by sounding persuasive.

Any final version may combine these, but the essay should not pretend that
combination is already proven before the primary text is pinned.

## Statecraft Return Paths

If the eventual primary text leans toward office, legitimacy, and civilization,
this essay should return into:

- Rome-facing `civ-state` retrieval
- statecraft notes on authority under acceleration
- compact objects where synthetic cognition compresses command or judgment

If the text turns out to be mostly pastoral or anthropological in force, the
`singularity` version should remain primary and this essay should stay narrower.
