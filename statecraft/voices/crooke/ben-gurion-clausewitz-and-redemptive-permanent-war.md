
# Ben-Gurion, Clausewitz, And Redemptive Permanent War

## Purpose

Track Crooke's recurring contrast between bounded political war and the redemptive, permanent-security logic that absorbs politics into exterminatory mission.

## Time Boundary

`2025-01-01 -> present`, with the first materialized Crooke evidence appearing in June 2025.

## Theme Definition

This theme gathers the historical and quasi-historical figures Crooke uses to diagnose a severe shift: from force as an instrument of politics toward permanent war, territorial redemption, and destruction at the root.

## Primary Crooke Examples

- Ben-Gurion as the bounded political-war alternative
- Clausewitz as force subordinate to politics
- German Second World War permanent-security logic
- Nuremberg as the legal-moral line against exterminatory doctrine

## What Crooke Is Arguing

This is probably the most distinctively Crooke second-seed theme because it carries sacred, legal, and strategic diagnosis all at once. Ben-Gurion and Clausewitz let him name an older doctrine of limit: a small state stays within its power, uses force when necessary, and still understands war as subordinate to political relation. Against that, Crooke places a fused modern doctrine of permanent security and messianic redemption. In his reading, once those are joined, war stops being an instrument and becomes a redemptive mechanism that cannot accept ordinary political limits.

The historical examples matter because they let Crooke say this is not merely "hardline policy." The Germany / Nuremberg reference turns the issue into a genealogy of condemned doctrine. The Ben-Gurion / Clausewitz side preserves the lost alternative. Together they allow the shelf to distinguish between severe realism and a redemptive or exterminatory logic that has broken free of politics. For the lattice, this is one of the strongest candidates for a genuinely new question family beyond Freeman.
