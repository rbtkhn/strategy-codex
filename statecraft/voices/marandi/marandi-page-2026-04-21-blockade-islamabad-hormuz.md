### Verbatim

**A —** The central Marandi claim is that the United States has already violated the ceasefire by imposing a siege on Iranian ports and seizing or damaging Iranian shipping. In that frame, Iran is not refusing talks in the abstract; it is refusing to negotiate while a coercive regime is still active. So the Islamabad question is presented less as a diplomatic scheduling issue than as a legitimacy issue: no new round while blockade conditions continue.

**B —** Marandi treats Hormuz as permanently altered by the war. His line is not simply that Iran has temporary leverage, but that the postwar status of the Strait has changed and will not be negotiated back into the old order. The one explicit exception he allows is Oman, which he names as the only state with which Iran would coordinate on Strait status. That makes this object useful less as a shipping-facts page than as a red-line page about how Tehran-facing English discourse is defining sovereignty after the war.

**C —** The interview is also a high-rhetoric escalation object. Marandi warns that renewed U.S. attacks on critical Iranian infrastructure would trigger wider retaliation against Gulf infrastructure, shipping, and the regional economic system. He frames this not as a speculative side possibility but as the natural result of U.S. escalation. The value here is that it shows the pressure language and deterrent theater of the lane. The limit is that many of the concrete downstream claims are presented at a much hotter level than the evidence on the page can independently sustain.

**D —** A second useful layer is the regime-register distinction. Marandi repeatedly speaks in a Tehran-facing English register that is close to official red-line language without being identical to an IRI primary. That matters because the same April cluster also includes Araghchi’s diplomatic wording and later Marandi glosses on Hormuz conditions. This page is therefore most useful when read as a bridge object between official IRI primary lines and commentator-level advocacy, not as a substitute for either one.

### Reflection

This is a strong **register and red-line page**, not a high-confidence fact page. What is reusable now is Marandi’s framing of:

- negotiation under siege as illegitimate
- Hormuz status as changed rather than merely contested
- Oman as the narrow coordination exception
- Tehran-facing deterrent language toward the Gulf and the United States

What is not yet safe to load heavily as fact without primaries is the exact blockade / seizure chain, the scale and timing of shipping damage, the implied permanence of the new Strait regime, and several of the hotter downstream catastrophe claims. The right way to use this page is to preserve the distinction between **Marandi’s voice as signal** and **verified state or field facts** as evidence.
