## Jiang's Recurring Claim

Jiang's recurring line is that deeper China-Russia interdependence creates a real strategic problem for Beijing.

In that frame:

- Russia can help China with overland energy and northern routes
- but heavy reliance on Russia reduces Chinese flexibility
- dependence on Russian-controlled corridors can become a strategic liability
- Beijing therefore prefers balancing and route pluralism rather than deep bloc commitment

This is why Jiang often treats Russian control of the **Northern Sea Route** or heavy reliance on Russian territory as something China should approach cautiously rather than celebrate.

## Where the Critique Bites

The criticism is not that Jiang misses geography altogether. It is that he appears to **weight the cost side more heavily than the protection side**.

His framing often looks asymmetric:

- dependence on Russia is vivid and dangerous
- exposure to US maritime coercion is real but does not always receive equal structural weight

That makes Russia appear mainly as a **constraint on autonomy**, when a more Eurasian reading would see Russia as part of the answer to China's core vulnerability.

If the main long-run threat to China is:

- maritime choke-point pressure
- sanctions enforcement
- interdiction risk
- dollar-system coercion

then deeper alignment with Russia may be less a liability than a **continental protection system**.

## Continental-Depth Counter-reading

The counter-reading starts from a different premise:

**China does not merely need trade. It needs controlled overland connectivity with west Eurasia.**

Here "west Eurasia" means:

- Russia
- Persia / Iran
- Turkey
- the Caucasus, Central Asia, and onward corridors into Europe and the Middle East

From that angle, Russia offers China several strategic benefits Jiang tends to understate:

- a secure northern rear
- overland energy resilience
- reduced dependence on vulnerable sea lanes
- corridor protection under a friendly neighboring land power
- strategic redundancy against blockade

So what Jiang often calls "dependence on Russia" can also be read as **the normal and beneficial interdependence of neighboring continental powers**.

## Northern Sea Route

This is the clearest sharp-edge case.

Jiang's tendency is to say:

- Russian control of the Northern Sea Route creates dependence
- therefore China should be cautious about relying on it too heavily

The counter-reading is almost the reverse:

- Russian control is part of what makes the route valuable
- a route protected by Russia is less exposed to US naval coercion than open maritime routes under American pressure
- therefore Russian control is not only a liability; it is also **anti-blockade shelter**

So the live disagreement is not about whether control matters. It is about how to interpret it:

- **Jiang:** control by Russia = autonomy loss risk
- **Counter-reading:** control by Russia = strategic shelter from hostile maritime power

## Historical Support

The civ-emp support for the counter-reading is that protected continental routes have often been a strength rather than a weakness.

Useful historical anchors:

- the **Silk Road**, as a model of interior Eurasian exchange
- **Mongol Eurasian integration**, where secure land corridors created large-scale commercial and strategic resilience
- **World War II**, where continental depth and interior lines were decisive against encirclement and external pressure

These do not prove that every China-Russia dependency is healthy. But they do support a more favorable reading of land-power interdependence than Jiang usually offers.

## Relation to the US System Question

This disagreement is tied to a second one:

**How much does China still need the US-centered order?**

If Jiang is right that Chinese elites and institutions remain deeply tied to the old system, then caution toward Russia has more logic.

If the counter-reading is right that China has already built enough of an alternative system, then the central question changes:

- not how to preserve maximum access to the American order
- but how to secure China's own system against American coercion

Under that reading, Russia rises in value because it helps defend:

- continental depth
- west-Eurasian connectivity
- energy continuity
- anti-blockade resilience

## Best Compression

The shortest useful compression is:

- Jiang sees Russia mainly through the lens of **dependence risk**
- the counter-reading sees Russia as a source of **strategic depth**

Or even shorter:

**Jiang underweights the alliance value of Russia to China because he treats continental interdependence too much as a liability and not enough as a protection system.**
