## Core thesis

Baud’s international-law lane is not merely that he cites legal categories. The stronger continuity is that he repeatedly treats **international law as a strategic architecture**:

- a restraint on arbitrary force
- a test of whether states still accept reciprocal limits
- a condition for credible negotiation
- a dividing line between lawful order and raw coercive hierarchy

When this lane is active, Baud usually makes one of four moves:

1. distinguishes **legality** from floating claims of **legitimacy**
2. treats sanctions or coercive measures without due process as evidence of **lawless governance**
3. reads military acts through **law-of-war categories** rather than purely moral outrage
4. argues that states claiming to be **above international law** are undermining the post-1945 settlement itself

## Recurring claims

### 1. Extrajudicial sanctions are a rule-of-law breach

Baud repeatedly uses his own sanctions case as proof that the EU can now apply foreign-policy punishment domestically without trial, due process, or ordinary legal remedy. In this lane, the important point is not personal grievance alone; it is the claim that **politics has been placed above law**.

Distinct from Crooke: Crooke may also speak about legal breakdown, but Baud here is tighter on **sanctions procedure, juridical breach, and due-process failure**.

### 2. The West increasingly substitutes rules for law

He often contrasts a purported **rules-based order** with actual **international law**, treating the former as discretionary power language used by stronger states to bypass reciprocal legal limits.

Distinct from Crooke: Baud treats this as a **legal and institutional substitution problem**; Crooke more often treats the same move as a crisis of legitimacy, reciprocity, and settlement credibility.

### 3. International law is a strategic, not decorative, constraint

Baud’s legal talk is usually tied to battlefield or diplomatic consequence:

- blockade implies war logic
- attacks on civilians or protected actors corrode restraint
- co-belligerency changes state liability
- nuclear or escalatory choices run into proportionality and distinction problems

Distinct from host arcs: this topical thread isolates the reusable legal grammar itself. Open [davis-baud-arc.md](/C:/dev/strategy-codex/statecraft/channels/daniel-davis/stream/davis-baud-arc.md) when the question is military feasibility under legal constraint; open [nima-baud-arc.md](/C:/dev/strategy-codex/statecraft/channels/dialogue-works/stream/nima-baud-arc.md) when the question is live crisis pressure.

### 4. Above-the-law rhetoric is civilizationally destabilizing

When political actors openly say that international law does not apply to them, Baud reads that not as mere hypocrisy but as a direct attack on the 1945 architecture meant to prevent unilateral domination.

Distinct from Crooke: Crooke would more often stress what this does to the possibility of settlement; Baud here stresses the **attack on legal architecture itself**.
