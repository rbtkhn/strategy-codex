## Broken Assurances And Doomed Settlements

### 1. Budapest Memorandum and Minsk as false guarantees and failed political carriers

- Example: `Budapest Memorandum / Minsk Accords`
- Source: [2025-03-20 Daniel Davis](../../../source-archive/statecraft/2025-03-20/source-daniel-davis-john-mearsheimer-therell-be-no-meaningfulf-ceasefire-in-ukraine-russia-war-2025-03-20.md)
- Exact quote:

> "the Minsk accords were doomed from the get-go."

> "No, we explicitly did not give them security guarantees."

- Claim Mearsheimer is making: Western discourse treats Budapest and Minsk as if they were decisive moral proofs against Russia, but in his reading both examples reveal that paper assurances without enforceable political carriers or realistic structural terms do not protect the weaker party.
- How the historical example supports the claim: Mearsheimer uses Minsk to show that autonomy and settlement architecture were never carried seriously enough to survive, and Budapest to show that rhetorical assurances were later misremembered as war-guarantee commitments they never were.
- Example type: `precedent / settlement-memory`
- CIV-EMP reading: primary bucket `peace`. The recurring pattern is that ceasefire or assurance language without enforceable authority, survivable terms, or credible guarantees produces strategic theater rather than successor-stable peace.
- Lane relevance: `russia` and `america`
- Transaction use: useful when testing whether a proposed guarantee is real, whether autonomy or neutrality terms have a carrier, and whether an agreement is being remembered more strongly than it was actually written.

## Punishment Does Not Equal Surrender

### 5. World War II bombing, maximum pressure, and conquest as the real surrender threshold

- Example: `Japan / Germany / Iraq / maximum pressure / big battalions`
- Sources:
  - [2026-04-30 Daniel Davis](../../../source-archive/statecraft/2026-04-30/source-daniel-davis-mearsheimer-weve-lost-the-iran-war-were-screwed-2026-04-30.md)

> "We saw this in World War II with the bombing campaigns against Japan and Germany."

> "It's because they wouldn't surrender from a punishment campaign."

> "God is on the side of the big battalions"

- Claim Mearsheimer is making: punishment strategies are chronically oversold. States under existential threat often absorb enormous punishment and surrender only when conquest or overwhelming ground power becomes unavoidable.
- How the historical example supports the claim: Mearsheimer stacks old and recent examples into one realism lesson. World War II and Iraq show what surrender actually required, while maximum pressure on Iran shows what punishment by itself fails to do.
- Example type: `warning case`
- CIV-EMP reading: primary bucket `geo`. The pattern is not just military brutality but the relation between manpower, occupation, invasion capacity, and what coercive instruments can really achieve over distance.

## Synthesis

Mearsheimer's historical-example usage on the current shelf clusters around five recurring jobs:

1. separating paper assurances from real settlement carriers
2. explaining wars through balance-of-power sequencing rather than moral theater
3. marking the shift from bipolar or unipolar habits into a genuinely multipolar order
4. attacking punishment fantasies that ignore conquest requirements
5. disciplining decline analogies so overreach is not lazily mistaken for terminal collapse

The route families are not interchangeable. Davis is strongest when settlement failure, punishment limits, or alliance-blowback become concrete. Diesen is strongest on structural history, order transition, and macro-level Cold War / multipolar comparison. Napolitano still reads more as reinforcing orbit than as a mature historical-theme carrier. Authored Mearsheimer surfaces matter because they often state the ground-force or feasibility principle in the clearest naked form.

The most active `civ-emp` buckets in this v1 sample are `peace` and `empire-instrument`, with `war` and `geo` also carrying important load. That matches the actual Mearsheimer shelf: the historical examples are usually in service of settlement realism, power-distribution realism, or coercive-feasibility realism rather than moral narrative.

Best downstream candidates for later statecraft upgrades:

- `Budapest / Minsk / JCPOA` -> peace-memory and guarantee-discipline retrieval
- `NATO expansion after de-nuclearization` -> alliance-expansion and imperial-instrument counterweight
- `bipolar -> multipolar transition` -> order-transition retrieval
- `punishment campaigns do not produce surrender` -> war-feasibility and coercion-limit retrieval
- `false Suez analogy` -> decline-diagnostic discipline

The audit is strongest where Mearsheimer names the example and immediately says what structural lesson it proves. It is thinner where the shelf still lacks a dedicated downstream `civ-emp` companion object layer. That is the next obvious improvement wedge if this speaker is going to become a heavier academy-statecraft intake source.
