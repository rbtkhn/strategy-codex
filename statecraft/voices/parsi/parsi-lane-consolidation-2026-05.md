## What This Lane Is Actually Saying Over Time

The clearest through-line is not simply “be nicer to Iran” or “oppose war.” The stronger statement is:

1. U.S. and Israeli policy repeatedly misread coercion as leverage.
2. Iran’s real leverage lies in endurance, deterrent remnants, corridor exposure, and the global costs it can impose.
3. Trump is structurally drawn toward optics of exit, but the coalitions around him repeatedly sell him escalation as a shortcut to dominance.
4. Israel’s doctrine pushes for repeated war because stable deterrence with regional foes is treated as intolerable.
5. Durable settlement requires what coercive maximalists least want to offer:
   - sanctions relief
   - mutual restraint
   - regional security inclusion
   - recognition that off-ramps must exist for all sides, not only Washington

That means the Parsi lane is best understood as a combined:

- diplomacy-process lane
- coercion-critique lane
- sanctions / leverage lane
- regional architecture lane

It is not primarily:

- ORBAT analysis
- moral-theological witness
- domestic U.S. electoral reporting
- pure activist denunciation

It does engage all of those at times, but its center of gravity is the strategic argument that coercive dominance fantasies repeatedly destroy the very exits that would serve U.S. interests better.
