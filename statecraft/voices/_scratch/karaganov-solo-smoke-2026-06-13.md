## Solo smoke

**Prompt:** Glenn — you ask whether lowering the threshold is the *solution*.

### Karaganov (solo)

I mean, the problem is much deeper than the crossing of this or that red line in Ukraine. I am a historian of war and historical strategy, and I have been warning for twenty-five years that we procrastinated for too long. What I call an **age of Wars** is not one campaign; it is a tectonic shift in the world system — a gap of ten, fifteen, twenty years before we can build a new institutional framework, a BRICS-plus arrangement, a new United Nations that is not a Western condominium. In that gap, biological weapons of the poor, cheap missiles, insurgencies everywhere — Gaza, Africa, Latin America — all burn at once. Nuclear deterrence was the fuse that kept the great powers from sleepwalking into catastrophe. We removed it, or rather the West removed the **feeling of self-preservation** through what I call **strategic parasitism**: two generations that do not understand what war is, living under an American nuclear umbrella while pushing Russia toward humiliation.

So yes — I opened this debate to **sober up** populations and elites. I am persuading my government; I was the voice of a minority; I hope I am becoming less so. That is not the same as saying the president has signed every paragraph I write — God forbid we need what I describe. But Russia has, I believe, not only a necessity but even an **obligation** to lower the nuclear threshold: to reinstall the fuse, to restore deterrence before we reach direct confrontation, which is one of only three outcomes this war can produce — overrun, a peace agreement, or direct confrontation between nuclear powers. I beg the Almighty that the third path is not necessitated.

If sobering words fail, there are **steps on the ladder of escalation** — trial tests, something like the king of bombs — reminders that the first-strike doctrines some European countries still entertain were, in the Cold War, largely a fake; I know that for sure. And if elites still do not listen, a **limited strike** on several European countries that maintain first-strike postures is a viable option — I am part of European, I do not want Europeans to die, but we could punish them so that Germany and others pay their debts to humanity. The alternative is not my preference: catastrophe, or a **dignified retreat** of the West — rolling back with flying banners rather than dragging the world through a general war. That is the strategic choice the age of Wars imposes. The world majority policy our government increasingly supports is the horizon after we survive this transition — but we will not survive it if deterrence stays broken and everyone treats Russian warnings as bluff.

## Duet smoke — Karaganov × Mercouris (chain law)

**Load-bearing contrast:** Jan 2024 origin panel — shared danger diagnosis; split on **threshold remedy** vs **diplomatic/multipolar alternative**. Mercouris responds to **immediately prior** Karaganov turn only.

**Host (once):** Professor Karaganov — red lines keep breaking; is lowering the threshold the answer? Then Alex — your turn on the same object.

### Karaganov (opens)

Thank you. It is not only about Russia's nuclear posture — the problem is much deeper than the crossing of red lines. I have been warning for twenty-five years; we procrastinated for too long. Nuclear deterrence no longer functions as it did — I call it **strategic parasitism**: Western elites lost the feeling of self-preservation while newspapers tell them to **call Mr Putin's bluff**. That is why I opened this debate — to sober up populations and elites. Russia has, I believe, an **obligation** to lower the nuclear threshold and **reinstall the nuclear fuse** before we drift into direct confrontation — one of only three outcomes in Ukraine: overrun, peace, or catastrophe between nuclear powers. God forbid it comes to limited strikes on European countries with first-strike doctrines — I am part of European and do not want Europeans to die — but we may have to show the stick, hopefully without using it. This sits inside what I call an **age of Wars**: a ten-to-twenty-year gap before a BRICS-plus world majority order can be built.

### Mercouris (responds — chain)

I lived through the Cold War from the Western side, and I remember very well how in the 1960s and 1970s the overriding fear was nuclear war — Arms Control, the Non-Proliferation Treaty, constraints on superpower behaviour. When the Americans bombed Haiphong, people worried about Soviet merchant ships; during the 1982 Lebanon crisis, Denis Healey went on British television to say certain strikes were unacceptably dangerous. Millions marched. **All that fear has gone.** If you read Western newspapers now, the constant refrain is that we must call Mr Putin's bluff — missile strikes deep inside Russia, whatever — because the danger does not exist. I find that extremely worrying, perhaps because I am a child of the Cold War. On the **danger**, I agree with Professor Karaganov completely. On the **remedy** — adjusting nuclear thresholds — I am very nervous. I would ask whether strengthening the institutional and diplomatic framework **outside** the West might be more effective: Lavrov at the Security Council, BRICS, Eurasian integration, relationships with Indonesia and others — saying to Americans and Europeans, you are on an incredibly dangerous path and you are becoming isolated, **without** lowering the threshold. I also do not believe negotiations to end the Ukraine war gracefully are coming; Washington has, in effect, stopped that conversation — which is exactly the drift Professor Karaganov describes.

### Karaganov (responds — chain)

Philosophically I agree with your premise — it would have been better to build a new system. The problem is the old system is collapsing; institutions erode; we have a gap of fifteen or twenty years before a new arrangement — BRICS-plus, perhaps a new UN based on the Shanghai Cooperation Organization — can be stable. **Unfortunately I do not see how we live through this gap without reinstalling the fuse**, unless we risk a major nuclear war. We talk with Chinese and Indians on a new theory of deterrence; our American and European partners are forbidden to talk seriously with us — Russians travel and return; Western officials who come here face scandal at home. So at this junction, sobering words and steps on the ladder — trial tests, reminders of what hell is — may be the only reliable instrument. I hope we stop short of use. But if people do not come to their senses, we are doomed — and biological weapons of the poor will burn in that gap alongside the nuclear fuse. That is my real concern.

## Duet smoke — Karaganov × Mearsheimer (Jun 2026 panel object)

**Intake:** [2026-06-13 Mearsheimer × Karaganov panel](../../../source-archive/statecraft/2026-06-13/source-glenn-diesen-mearsheimer-karaganov-nuclear-strike-europe-restore-deterrence-2026-06-13.md)

**Chain law:** Mearsheimer responds to Karaganov's immediately prior turn; Karaganov closes on Mearsheimer's coercion/proliferation frame.

**Host (once):** Glenn — NATO keeps climbing the ladder assuming Russia will not respond. Sergey, you first; then John.

### Karaganov (opens)

Thank you. The problem is not only using or not using nuclear weapons — about fifteen years ago the world entered the most dangerous period in its history. The tectonic plates shifted in the mid-2000s; we are at the **beginning of a world war** on many fronts, not only Ukraine. Europe has been the source of most evil in human history — colonialism, serial genocides — and I am sorry you are European, Glenn, though I am part of that civilization too. We must restore the **fear of nuclear war** — the fear of God — that kept us from warring for seventy years. I criticize my own elite: we procrastinated; the ugly war started not in 2022 but in 1994 when I told Western leaders NATO expansion would lead to war. Now we place warheads on delivery vehicles; we pass **letters** on the escalation ladder. God forbid we use nuclear weapons in Europe — it would be a **moral sin** that would open Pandora's box; that is why President Putin hesitates, not because the strategy is unclear. Technically, limited use could **win** — the United States would not respond, as history suggests since the mid-1950s — but we would feel sinners. We must **threaten** and be ready, to stop the slide toward a world without rules — drones, cyber, biological weapons of the poor — before humanity forgets that hell exists.

### Mearsheimer (responds — chain)

Sergey, I largely agree that Western elites have forgotten how dangerous nuclear weapons make the world — I have been amazed at that forgetting. During the Cold War we understood red lines: Hungary '56, Czechoslovakia '68 — the West did nothing for good strategic reasons. **Kursk 2024** — an American-backed ally invading Russian territory — would have been unthinkable. **2025** — an attack on one leg of Russia's strategic triad — also unthinkable; Washington did not criticize it. That tells me the West thinks it can push Russia around. Before the war, I thought the aim was to **knock Russia out of the ranks of the great powers** through sanctions and battlefield defeat — extremely dangerous when you're threatening a nuclear state's survival. On your **winning** language: I don't think anyone wins an **all-out** exchange — we get vaporized. I read you the way I read Schelling: a **handful** of nuclear weapons against Western targets to **signal** seriousness — put both sides on the **slippery slope to oblivion** — **coerce** behavior, not win a military victory on the battlefield. The threat of escalation sits at the heart; escalation itself is catastrophe. My question — and this is structural, not moral theatre — if Russia used limited nuclear weapons in Europe and the U.S. did not retaliate, as you believe, wouldn't **Germany** — no arsenal of its own, closest major power — have overwhelming incentive to **proliferate**? June 1941 to May 1945 sits in Russian minds. A self-help world pushes Germans toward their own deterrent. Where does that leave Russia? I push back, respectfully, on **eliminationist** language about evaporating Germany — the logic of deterrence and the logic of ethnic essentialism are different problems.

### Karaganov (responds — chain)

John — on **winning**, I accept your Schelling read for the **Western** audience: we coerce, we do not seek thermonuclear exchange. But you know from our old debates — I am a student of nuclear strategy for fifty years — the myths we installed in the 1970s about nuclear winter and global sterility were **political tools** to prevent war; war has started anyway. Limited use in Europe, if God forbid it comes, restores fear before the world acquires **two or three more** nuclear powers who will use them because nobody believes red lines anymore. On **Germany** — yes, proliferation risk exists; that is why we send signals now. But Germany unleashed two world wars in one generation; if Berlin moves toward nuclear weapons, Russia and China have discussed that scenario — they must not be allowed to get close. I do not want European civilization destroyed; I know Goethe, Shiller, our common culture — but if German elites continue revenantist policy, **conventional missiles first**, then limited nuclear if they do not come to senses. The prime target in operational planning is not Poland alone — it is **Germany**. I hope never. The moral sin restrains us more than your proliferation fear restrains Washington — because Americans, as you say, would likely **not** climb the ladder after a limited European strike. That is exactly why we must restore deterrence **before** St Petersburg becomes routine and NATO fires from its own territory with impunity. My bottom line matches yours on catastrophe; we differ on whether fear of hell must be **reinstalled** by steps on the ladder before the gap kills us.
