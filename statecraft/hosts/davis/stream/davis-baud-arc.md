## Why this guest run matters

Jacques Baud matters here less as a generic NATO or Europe explainer than as a recurring **law-of-war, alliance-mandate, and coercive-feasibility** voice inside a host stream otherwise centered on military practicality, escalation pressure, and policy risk.

Inside the Davis stream, that makes the run especially useful when the notebook needs continuity between:

- operational military talk and legal mandate
- coercive threats and what they can actually force
- Gulf-base liability and alliance co-belligerency
- blockade rhetoric and the wider economic or escalation consequences it would trigger

What makes this a real pair arc is that the same host x guest shape keeps returning:

- Davis frames the crisis through force feasibility, escalation risk, and U.S. choice
- Baud answers by distinguishing blackmail from negotiation, rhetoric from legal basis, and punishment fantasies from durable settlement
- the lane repeatedly turns war talk into an argument about alliance liability, perfidy, co-belligerency, and the strategic incoherence of coercion without a credible end state

## Arc shape

This lane should be read in two layers:

- an older `2025` Ukraine-negotiation root file
- a real but still under-materialized `2026` Iran-war lane

The `2025` file shows the earlier Davis x Baud shape: weak bargaining hands, Western incoherence, and negotiation realism. The `2026` files harden that shape into an Iran-war lane centered on law-of-war breach, GCC liability, Hormuz coercion, and the gap between threat language and strategic leverage.
