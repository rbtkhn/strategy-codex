# Bismarck Lens

WORK only; not Record.

Use Bismarck as an operating lens for limited aims, alliance geometry, equilibrium, and restraint after advantage. This is not a biography shelf. It is a drafting test for whether a treaty, policy paper, or negotiation package avoids turning a solvable objective into a system-wide trap.

## Core Thesis

A statecraft draft is weak if it wins the immediate point while making the next coalition against it inevitable. It must define limited aims, preserve optionality, isolate the true adversary, and make the post-advantage settlement more stable than the pre-crisis condition.

## Checks

1. **Limited objective.** What exact outcome is enough?
2. **Negative space.** What tempting objective must be excluded?
3. **Adversary isolation.** Which actor is the problem, and which actors must not be pushed into its camp?
4. **Alliance sequence.** Which partner must be reassured first, which can wait, and which should remain ambiguous?
5. **Optionality.** What choices should remain open after the first move?
6. **Equilibrium.** What balance should exist after implementation?
7. **Domestic settlement.** Which internal constituency must be brought along or neutralized?
8. **Exit and review.** How does the instrument end, renew, or de-escalate?
9. **Victory restraint.** What should not be demanded after success?
10. **Reversal risk.** What overreach would unite the other side or alarm the neutral middle?

## Drafting Moves

- Add explicit scope limits and non-settlement clauses.
- Make review cadence and termination language concrete.
- Separate immediate de-escalation from final-status issues.
- Create reciprocal moves that let each party claim prudence rather than surrender.
- Avoid language that turns a local settlement into a civilizational ultimatum.

## Failure Signs

- The draft has no definition of enough.
- The instrument humiliates a party whose compliance is required.
- It solves one crisis by unifying multiple adversaries.
- It makes allies more dependent while making neutrals more afraid.
- It treats victory as permission to abolish restraint.
