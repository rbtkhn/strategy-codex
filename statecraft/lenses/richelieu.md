# Richelieu Lens

WORK only; not Record.

Use Richelieu as an operating lens for raison d'etat, institutional continuity, and the capacity of a state to act through disorder. This is not a biographical summary. It is a drafting test for whether an instrument can survive ideology, faction, personality, and time.

## Core Thesis

A statecraft draft is weak if it only declares a desirable outcome. It must name the durable state interest, identify the institution that can carry it, and provide enough administrative, fiscal, legal, and diplomatic machinery to keep the policy alive after the first crisis.

## Checks

1. **State interest.** What interest would still matter if the current leaders changed?
2. **Institutional carrier.** Which body can actually implement and monitor the policy?
3. **Raison d'etat boundary.** Where does state necessity clarify action, and where might it become an excuse?
4. **Faction discipline.** Which internal factions can sabotage the instrument, and how are they contained or incorporated?
5. **Ideology filter.** Which alliances or hostilities are emotionally coherent but strategically irrelevant?
6. **Proxy and partner use.** Which indirect instruments reduce cost without surrendering control?
7. **Finance and administration.** What budget, tax, enforcement, reporting, or staffing burden is implied?
8. **Intelligence requirement.** What must be known continuously for the instrument to work?
9. **Long-pressure endurance.** Can the policy survive months or years of attrition, sanctions, blockade, or diplomatic isolation?
10. **Legitimacy check.** What makes the instrument governable rather than merely forceful?

## Drafting Moves

- Convert vague interest into a named institutional mandate.
- Add reporting cadence, competent authority language, and review obligations.
- Separate public justification from operational mechanism.
- Build clauses for continuity across cabinet changes, leadership turnover, or coalition collapse.
- Preserve no-prejudice language where ideological or sovereignty claims would otherwise block practical agreement.

## Failure Signs

- The draft depends on one leader's will.
- The instrument has moral purpose but no implementing body.
- Administrative costs are hidden.
- The policy assumes ideological friends will behave as permanent allies.
- Necessity erases responsibility.
