# Pape audit lessons


Purpose: record what the first real post-commit audit test taught us about the speaker audit system itself.

## What worked

- The system handled a speaker with strong mechanism and weak visible self-correction without breaking.
- The separation between `credibility` and `accuracy` was useful immediately.
- The testability vocabulary was strong enough to keep fresh or giant claims out of fake closure.
- The bridge categories helped explain why Pape can be highly reusable without being fully outcome-closed.

## What needed active restraint

- It was tempting to promote strong mechanism directly into `hit`.
- The fourth-center thesis is memorable, but it closes much more slowly than the shortages call.
- Interview-side compression can make a shelf feel more certain than the authored ledger actually is.

## Concrete lessons

1. A falsifier-rich shelf can still have a low visible correction score.
2. A clear mechanism is not the same thing as a closed outcome.
3. Public forecast shelves need extra discipline around `partial` versus `unresolved`.
4. The notebook should score the smallest honest closure first before touching the grand thesis.

## Workflow refinement

For future public-forecast speakers, prefer this audit order:

1. start with the authored ledger
2. identify the smallest clearly scoreable forecast row
3. only then add larger interpretive or civilizational rows
4. treat interview appearances as compression or pressure-test surfaces, not automatic proof layers

## Result

The audit system is stable enough for more real tasks, but it works best when it resists premature closure on memorable macro theses.
