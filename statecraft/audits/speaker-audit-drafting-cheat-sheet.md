# Speaker Audit Drafting Cheat Sheet

WORK only; not Record.

Purpose: translate speaker-audit judgments into memo-safe drafting language without overstating closure.

Use this sheet when the audit system is already populated and the real question is:

**How hard can I state this claim, and how much of the speaker's framing can I safely carry into prose?**

## Fast Router

- Open [speaker-credibility-matrix.md](speaker-credibility-matrix.md) when you need to know **who gets provisional trust** for the layer you are drafting.
- Open [speaker-accuracy-ledger.md](speaker-accuracy-ledger.md) when you need to know **how hard you can state a bounded claim**.
- Open [speaker-credibility-accuracy-bridge.md](speaker-credibility-accuracy-bridge.md) when strong method and open closure are getting flattened into one score.
- Open a speaker-specific profile in `credibility/` or `accuracy/` when the shared surfaces are too compressed to carry the sentence safely.

## Wording Rules By Accuracy Status

### `hit`

- justified assertion: direct statement is allowed
- avoid: unnecessary hedging that makes a closed claim sound speculative
- use:
  - `shows`
  - `confirms`
  - `was borne out`
  - `proved accurate on`

Example phrases:

- `Parsi's blockade-failure claim was borne out by the later pressure sequence.`
- `Pape's shortages call was borne out faster than many competing reads expected.`

### `partial`

- justified assertion: substantial support, but not full closure
- avoid:
  - `proves`
  - `settles`
  - `clearly establishes`
- use:
  - `supports`
  - `points toward`
  - `is consistent with`
  - `was partially borne out`

Example phrases:

- `Current fragmentation signals support Pape's fourth-center mechanism without fully closing the larger ranking claim.`
- `The ceasefire terrain is consistent with Parsi's architecture read, but not yet a final confirmation of the full bargain.`

### `miss`

- justified assertion: say the claim was wrong, then separate that from broader shelf value
- avoid:
  - turning one miss into total shelf invalidation
  - quietly omitting the failure
- use:
  - `did not hold`
  - `was wrong on`
  - `failed to materialize`
  - `missed`

Example phrases:

- `The original claim did not hold.`
- `He was wrong on the specific delegation read, even if the broader diplomatic frame remained useful.`

### `mistimed`

- justified assertion: keep timing and mechanism separate
- avoid:
  - treating the whole mechanism as disproven if the timing slipped
  - writing as if the timing was basically right
- use:
  - `the mechanism may survive, but the timing did not`
  - `arrived later than claimed`
  - `arrived earlier than expected`
  - `was directionally right but mistimed`

Example phrases:

- `Parsi's broader diplomacy logic may survive, but the expected walk-back was mistimed.`
- `The mechanism may survive, but the timing did not.`

### `unresolved`

- justified assertion: treat it as a live hypothesis, not a settled fact
- avoid:
  - `clearly true`
  - `already established`
  - `has shown that`
- use:
  - `remains an open hypothesis`
  - `is still too early to close`
  - `has not yet earned full confirmation`
  - `remains live but underclosed`

Example phrases:

- `Pape's escort-credibility warning remains an open hypothesis rather than a closed result.`
- `This claim is still too early to close honestly.`

### `not-yet-scoreable`

- justified assertion: identify it as fresh forecast material
- avoid:
  - assigning it a success or failure status
  - using it as if it were already confirmed
- use:
  - `too fresh to score honestly`
  - `should be treated as a live forecast`
  - `belongs to the next review window`

Example phrases:

- `The oil-repricing phase claim is too fresh to score honestly.`
- `This should be treated as a live forecast, not a closed lesson.`

## Wording Rules By Credibility Posture

### `high-trust, low-cleanup`

- drafting implication: you can carry the speaker's framing relatively directly, but never beyond closed claims
- use the speaker to anchor the paragraph, then let the ledger determine closure strength

### `high-trust, outcome-contingent`

- drafting implication: use the mechanism confidently, but keep outcome language conditional unless the ledger closes it
- default move: strong causal explanation, softer end-state language

### `medium-trust, mechanism-strong`

- drafting implication: use the causal model, but do not inherit full confidence tone or outcome closure
- default move: trust the structure, not the rhetorical altitude

### `medium-trust, verification-heavy`

- drafting implication: use the speaker as an input, not as the carrying authority for the paragraph
- default move: extract one insight, then verify elsewhere before leaning on it

### `low-trust, rhetorically strong`

- drafting implication: do not let the tone write the paragraph
- default move: salvage isolated insights only after outside confirmation

### `insufficiently closed`

- drafting implication: treat mainly as a prompt to investigate, not as reusable authority
- default move: write around the claim or leave it out until the notebook has stronger closure

## Bridge Warnings

Do not:

- treat strong method as a closed outcome
- treat one `I was wrong` as a correction habit
- treat agreement with the notebook as credibility
- treat unresolved macro claims as if they were weak methods

Use the bridge when you feel tempted to say:

- `this speaker is right`

when the real claim is:

- `this speaker is methodologically strong, but the window is still open`

## Mini Example: Pape vs Parsi on Hormuz

Overstated draft:

> Pape proves that the Hormuz crisis has already reorganized world power, while Parsi shows that sanctions relief is the only possible deal.

Safer revision:

> Pape is stronger on the moving mechanism: escort exposure, shortage progression, and the persistence of a Gulf risk premium beyond nominal reopening. Parsi is stronger on the settlement design: blockade pressure has not produced capitulation, so any viable bargain likely turns on sanctions relief, sequencing, and a non-aggression structure. Pape's larger power-rank claims remain only partially borne out, while Parsi's bargaining architecture is stronger than the current level of political closure.

Why this is safer:

- it uses **credibility** to choose who carries which layer
- it uses **accuracy** to soften Pape's larger closure claims
- it uses the **bridge** to stop coherent mechanism from masquerading as settled outcome

## Quick Rule

Before writing a speaker-driven sentence:

1. choose the speaker with `credibility`
2. harden or soften the sentence with `accuracy`
3. use the `bridge` if the speaker sounds right but the closure is still thin
