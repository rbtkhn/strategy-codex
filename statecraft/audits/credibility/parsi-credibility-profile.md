# Parsi credibility profile


## Summary

Parsi is a diplomacy-architecture speaker whose main value comes from settlement design, sanctions logic, guarantee structure, and the ability to show why coercion fails without a political landing zone. The notebook can often extend high provisional trust quickly here because the mechanism is disciplined, but the current corpus shows more conditional openness than explicit first-person revision.

## Correction taxonomy

- Primary label: `conditional openness only`
- Secondary note: current shelf shows hedged openness and architecture discipline more than visible correction habit

## Qualitative assessment

| factor | score | short note |
|---|---|---|
| self-correction | low | current materialized shelf shows little explicit first-person revision beyond hedged openness |
| falsifier discipline | high | claims regularly turn on whether coercion produces concessions, whether sanctions relief is real, and whether implementation architecture exists |
| mechanism clarity | high | blockade failure, leverage sequencing, sanctions relief, and non-aggression guarantee logic are all sharply legible |
| evidence posture | high | argument shape is generally source-aware and distinguishes proposal structure from wishful diplomatic gloss |
| scope honesty | high | Parsi usually stays inside diplomacy, bargaining, and architecture rather than pretending to carry every military lane |
| host consistency | high | authored and interview surfaces preserve the same architecture logic across Davis, Napolitano, Mercouris, Diesen, and Freeman lanes |

## Strongest credibility asset

- Clear settlement architecture discipline that turns abstract diplomacy into concrete sequencing, guarantees, leverage, and implementation questions.

## Main credibility risk

- The shelf can sound fully closed because the architecture is so coherent, even when the actual bargaining window remains politically open and outcome closure is still incomplete.

## Provisional trust class

- `high-trust, outcome-contingent`

## Verification burden

- Low to medium. The notebook can usually reuse Parsi quickly for diplomacy design and coercion-failure logic, but still needs downstream closure on whether the predicted bargain or de-escalation path actually materializes.
