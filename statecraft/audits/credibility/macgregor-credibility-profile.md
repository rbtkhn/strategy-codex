WORK only; not Record.

## Summary

Macgregor is a force-balance and imperial-overreach speaker with a sharp double-helix structure across Davis and Diesen. His best credibility lies in force-feasibility mechanism, but broader civilizational and political severity claims often require more downstream verification.

## Correction taxonomy

- Primary label: `collective wrongness, not personal correction`
- Secondary note: some conditional openness appears, but not a strong visible correction habit

## Qualitative assessment

| factor | score | short note |
|---|---|---|
| self-correction | medium | visible retrospective correction exists, but not as a dominant shelf habit |
| falsifier discipline | medium | some claims are sharply testable, others are broad and severity-heavy |
| mechanism clarity | high | force posture, logistics, and imperial burden are usually explicit |
| evidence posture | medium | good on force logic, looser on broader social or regime-level consequences |
| scope honesty | medium | strongest inside hard military and geopolitical cost lanes |
| host consistency | high | the two host transformations remain stable and distinct |

## Strongest credibility asset

- Hard force-balance mechanism under feasibility pressure.

## Main credibility risk

- Rhetorical scale can outrun evidentiary closure when the lane widens into broader civilizational or political collapse.

## Provisional trust class

- `medium-trust, mechanism-strong`

## Verification burden

- Medium. Reuse the force model quickly, but recheck broader consequence claims before stronger public use.
