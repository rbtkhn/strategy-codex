# Authority Carrier Orthogonality

Use this sheet when comparing current state carrier and authority-carrier benches across the four academy-statecraft lanes. The purpose is not biography. The purpose is to preserve four distinct settlement-credibility problems.

## Core Distinction

| lane | authority problem | main question | distinct risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [America](../america/state/) | fragmented / successor authority | Can the executive bind the state, Congress, allies, military, and possible successor America? | personal executive action that does not survive domestic rupture |
| [Russia](../russia/state/) | presidential-security elite carrier | Is the leader expressing durable Russian state interest or managing elite/security constraints? | personal or elite flexibility mistaken for durable settlement capacity |
| [China](../china/state/) | party-state implementation carrier | Can top leadership bind the party-state, bureaucracy, military, firms, banks, ports, and provinces? | visible leader commitment without enough quiet implementation capacity |
| [Persia](../persia/state/) | split authority structure | Who authorizes, who implements, who can spoil: supreme authority, government, IRGC, parliament? | a deal signed by one carrier but vetoed or hollowed out by another |

## Productive Overlaps

Russia and China both have top-leader-plus-institution structures. Keep the distinction sharp:

- Russia: security depth, recognition, elite/security consensus.
- China: party-state continuity, anti-disorder execution, industrial and logistics implementation.

America and Iran both have multiple veto points. Keep the distinction sharp:

- America: constitutional, alliance, and successor fragmentation.
- Iran: dual revolutionary-state authority and security-spoiler risk.

## Transaction Use

Every statecraft transaction should ask:

> Which authority carrier can actually make this settlement stick?

Default answers by lane:

- **America:** executive alone is rarely enough.
- **America technology-industrial objects:** private capacity is not public authority unless Congress, courts, regulators, procurement law, military implementation, allies, and successor authority can carry or constrain it.
- **Russia:** presidential line needs security-state carrying capacity.
- **China:** top-line agreement needs party-state implementation.
- **Iran:** no agreement is real unless supreme, administrative, and security carriers align.

## Failure Modes

- Treating a leader profile as biography rather than settlement authority.
- Assuming a signature means implementation.
- Confusing public posture with institutional carrying capacity.
- Ignoring succession or factional veto risk.
- Flattening all four lanes into generic "strong leader" analysis.

## Drafting Prompt

Before finalizing a treaty clause, compact, memo, or transaction, write one sentence:

> This instrument is credible in the `[lane]` lane only if `[authority carrier]` can sign, sell, implement, and survive `[continuity risk]`.
