# Pape Coercive-Center Index


This is a statecraft tracking surface inspired by Pape's fourth-center thesis. It is not a published Pape index. Use it to score which powers can force systemic adjustment under crisis conditions, especially when energy, chokepoints, sanctions, escalation, and alliance fragmentation are the battlefield.

The index complements [Comprehensive Power Vs Coercive Center](comprehensive-power-vs-coercive-center.md). It asks a narrower question:

> Which actors can make the system reorganize around them under pressure?

## Scoring Criteria

Score each criterion from 0 to 10, then apply the weight.

| criterion | weight | test |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Chokepoint leverage | 15 | Can the actor disrupt or regulate vital flows: oil, LNG, shipping, food, chips, finance? |
| Systemic spillover power | 15 | Does a crisis around this actor move global inflation, markets, shipping, alliances, or elections? |
| Coercive cost imposition | 12 | Can the actor impose intolerable military, economic, or political costs on stronger powers? |
| Energy security | 10 | Does the actor have domestic supply, reserves, refining depth, or energy-route resilience? |
| Strategic depth | 10 | Can the actor absorb strikes, sanctions, blockade, cyber, or infrastructure disruption? |
| Military denial capacity | 10 | Can the actor deny easy victory through missiles, drones, mines, air defense, cyber, terrain, or asymmetric tools? |
| Alliance / patron geometry | 8 | Can the actor draw support, split adversaries, or make patrons hedge rather than abandon it? |
| Domestic cohesion under pressure | 8 | Can state and society endure sanctions, shortages, bombing, elite splits, or legitimacy pressure? |
| Nuclear latency / escalation control | 7 | Can the actor approach, imply, or manage high escalation thresholds without losing control? |
| Diplomatic monetization | 5 | Can the actor convert leverage into recognition, sanctions relief, guarantees, or architecture? |

Treat durable post-crisis energy repricing as a composite signal rather than a new criterion. It mainly updates `Chokepoint leverage`, `Systemic spillover power`, and `Diplomatic monetization`. If transit resumes but Brent, insurance, and routing behavior stay structurally elevated, the coercive-center case has strengthened rather than faded.

## Revisit Triggers

Update the index when one of these changes:

- Hormuz opens, closes, or becomes partially regulated under new terms.
- GCC states re-cohere under U.S. leadership or visibly hedge toward China / Russia / Iran.
- Russia or China distance from Iran, deepen support, or become trapped by Iran's escalation.
- India shapes the crisis more directly than Iran.
- Diesel, LNG, shipping insurance, or food/fertilizer shortages move from price shock into physical shortage.
- Brent, war-risk insurance, or tanker-routing costs remain structurally elevated after nominal Hormuz reopening.
- Domestic cohesion breaks in one of the top-ranked actors.
- A treaty, ceasefire, transit compact, or sanctions-relief mechanism monetizes coercive leverage.

## Drafting Use

Use this index before drafting a treaty, policy paper, or negotiation brief when the actor's power type is contested.

1. Open the relevant national lane: [America](../america/), [Russia](../russia/), [China](../china/), or [Persia](../persia/).
2. Decide whether the policy problem is about comprehensive power, coercive center leverage, chokepoint denial, or diplomatic brokerage.
3. Use the index to name what would change the ranking.
4. Draft the instrument around the moving criterion, not around a vague claim that one state is "rising" or "declining."

## Guardrail

Do not write that Iran simply replaces India as the fourth world power. The cleaner hypothesis is that India remains the stronger comprehensive-power candidate, while Iran may be the more immediate coercive center when Hormuz, energy, nuclear latency, sanctions endurance, and alliance hedging are the operating variables.

## Non-Equivalence Rule

Do not treat this index and the [Mearsheimer structural-realist scoreboard](mearsheimer-structural-realist-scoreboard.md) as two labels for the same ranking.

The same event can move the two scoreboards in opposite directions. A Hormuz closure may raise Iran's Pape score by increasing crisis leverage, while lowering its Mearsheimer score if it produces a balancing coalition, overextension, loss of restraint, or institutional stress.

## Crosswalk With Mearsheimer

Use this crosswalk when a statecraft draft needs both pressure and structure.

| event | Pape index read | Mearsheimer scoreboard read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hormuz disruption persists | Iran rises if it can force system adjustment. | Iran may fall if disruption unifies adversaries or exceeds carrying capacity. |
| India shapes the crisis directly | India rises if it forces others to adjust now. | India rises if this confirms regional-hegemon or great-power autonomy. |
| U.S. civil fracture deepens | America falls if command credibility, sanctions, or military coordination weaken. | America falls if institutional carrier and strategic autonomy degrade. |
| China brokers a Gulf transit compact | China rises if it monetizes crisis leverage into system adjustment. | China rises if brokerage improves balance-of-power position without overextension. |
| Israel escalates regionally | Israel may rise as an escalation generator. | Israel may fall if escalation exposes lack of depth, restraint, or autonomy. |
