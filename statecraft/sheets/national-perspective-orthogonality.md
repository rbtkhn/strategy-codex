# National Perspective Orthogonality

WORK only; not Record.

Use this sheet when a policy question needs to be analyzed from several state perspectives without flattening them into one generic "strategic" view. The goal is not empathy theater or roleplay. The goal is to isolate each state's durable interest, fear, leverage, institutional carrier, and preferred off-ramp.

## Core Question

What does each state need the same fact pattern to mean?

The fact pattern can be shared. The statecraft question is different for each actor.

## Four-View Matrix

Use this default matrix for the Iran / India / fourth-center problem.

| perspective | primary question | Iran means | India means | main danger | preferred instrument |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| [China](../china/) | How do we preserve Iran's usefulness against U.S. coercion while preventing energy disorder? | Chokepoint lever and sanctions-resilience partner, useful but dangerous if uncontrolled. | Long-horizon rival / pole that may become the Western-backed Asian alternative to China. | Energy shock or Indian diplomatic gain as the "responsible stabilizer." | Quiet support, escalation management, Gulf transit compact, yuan / energy / shipping resilience. |

## Iran View Discipline

When adding Iran to a comparative analysis, do not treat it only as the object of others' policy. Give it an active statecraft logic:

- **Durable interest:** regime survival, sovereignty, territorial integrity, sanctions relief, deterrence, energy/export autonomy, and recognition as a necessary regional actor.
- **Institutional carrier:** Supreme National Security Council, Foreign Ministry, IRGC, regular military, energy ministry, central bank / sanctions-evasion channels, and negotiated backchannels.
- **Leverage:** Hormuz, missiles and drones, denial capacity, nuclear latency, proxy/partner networks, resource self-sufficiency, geography, and endurance under sanctions.
- **Constraint:** escalation can damage China and India, frighten Gulf states, overbind Russia/China, and validate Western coalition-building.
- **Off-ramp:** transit-security bargain, phased sanctions relief, non-regime-change guarantees, regional non-aggression language, and face-saving recognition of legitimate security interests.

## Orthogonality Test

A comparative statecraft analysis is orthogonal when each view answers a different question:

- **United States:** How do we bound the lever?
- **Russia:** How do we exploit the lever without entrapment?
- **China:** How do we preserve the lever without energy disorder?
- **Iran:** How do we monetize and legitimate the lever without losing control?

If two rows answer the same question, the analysis has collapsed into commentary. Re-separate them by interest, fear, instrument, and off-ramp.

## Richelieu Check

- What durable state interest does each state protect?
- Which institution can carry the policy after leaders or coalitions change?
- Where does public ideology hide the real interest?
- What finance, intelligence, energy, or administrative capacity is required?
- Where could necessity become excuse?

## Bismarck Check

- What is enough for each state?
- Which actor must each state avoid unifying against itself?
- What alliance geometry is each state trying to preserve?
- What restraint is required after advantage?
- What off-ramp lets each party claim prudence rather than surrender?
