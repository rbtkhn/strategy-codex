# China Secondary Sources - Colonial

## What This Secondary Shelf Is For

Use this shelf after the colonial China primary shelf when late-Qing breakdown, treaty-port pressure, Taiping fracture, reform failure, or Japan-led humiliation need stronger chronology and counterweight.

## Core Clarifiers

- Jonathan Spence, *The Search for Modern China*, for late-imperial to modern transition clarity and readable chronology.
- Stephen Platt, *Autumn in the Heavenly Kingdom* and related Taiping work, for internal fracture and regime danger.
- Joseph Esherick, Boxer and late-Qing scholarship, for pressure, reaction, and state weakness under intrusion.
- Rana Mitter, work on Japan and the Republican/war transition, for humiliation and occupation context.

## Counterweights And Corrections

- Paul Cohen, *Discovering History in China*, as a correction against importing teleological Western periodization into late-China reading.
- Pamela Crossley and related Qing scholarship, as a correction against flattening Qing rule into simple Han continuity or simple foreign interruption.

## Use Rule

Stay with the primary shelf when the issue is edicts, memorials, treaties, or official language. Open this shelf when chronology slips, when a primary file risks becoming a generic decline story, or when humiliation is being read without institutional breakdown detail.
