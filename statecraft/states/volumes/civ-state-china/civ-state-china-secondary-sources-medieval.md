# China Secondary Sources - Medieval


## What This Secondary Shelf Is For

Use this shelf after the medieval China primary shelf when dynastic restoration, conquest absorption, bureaucratic continuity, or examination order need clearer chronology or institutional context.

## Core Clarifiers

- Denis Twitchett and related Cambridge China volumes, for dynastic sequence, administrative continuity, and restoration patterning.
- Mark Edward Lewis, *China Between Empires*, for the mutation from Han fracture to renewed imperial order.
- Patricia Ebrey and Peter Bol, for examination culture, literati formation, and bureaucratic carry.
- Will Durant, brief supporting use from *The Story of Civilization*, only where broad transition compression helps the shelf without replacing stronger sinological detail.

## Counterweights And Corrections

- Naomi Standen and related frontier scholarship, as a correction against treating medieval continuity as purely inward and untouched by steppe pressure.
- Peter Lorge and related military-state scholarship, as a correction against over-civilian readings of continuity that hide force and conquest.

## Use Rule

Stay with the primary shelf when the problem is court language, law, or state self-description. Open this shelf when the issue is how continuity survives conquest, how restoration actually works institutionally, or where later readings smooth over rupture.
