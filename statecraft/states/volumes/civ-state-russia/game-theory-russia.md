# Russia Game-Theory

## Where This Sits

This chapter is the incentive, bargaining, and escalation layer for the Russia `civ-state` volume. It sits where encirclement, regathering, licensed survival, depth-seeking, and overreach pressure shape the Russian chain.

## Reading Posture

Read this as a present-tense application surface for recurring Russian strategic interaction rather than as a simple profile of one regime. The task is to see what game the current carrier believes survival requires.

## Historical Game Pressures

- exposure, encirclement, and preemptive control pressure
- regathering after fracture and center-restoration incentives
- licensed survival under domination and later internalization of control
- strategic depth, disruption, and buffer-seeking
- expansion for security, exhaustion through overreach

## Limits of the Frame

This chapter tracks recurring game structure, but it does not reduce Russia to cold incentives alone. Sacred ratification, humiliation memory, and sovereign-restoration imagination shape how the players define security and loss.

## Core Thesis

Russia's recurring game is the attempt to turn vulnerability into controlled depth before external pressure can become terminal.

The best compressed formula is:

**The Russian game rewards the carrier that can regather authority faster than its rivals can exploit fracture, then convert that regathered authority into depth, disruption, and recognition without exhausting the state in the process.**

## Game Logic

The recurring Russian game turns on five linked pressures:

- exposed approaches create urgency before safety is secure
- internal fracture invites outside exploitation
- regathered authority becomes preferable to dispersed weakness
- buffers and disruption extend survival odds
- security through expansion risks destroying the carrier that seeks it

This is why the Russian game often looks simultaneously defensive and expansionary. The same moves answer both fear and ambition.

## Encirclement And Compression

A recurring Russian incentive is to reduce the space in which rivals can organize danger.

That produces preferences for:

- buffers
- controlled corridors
- compliant near abroad
- disruption of hostile consolidation

The underlying game is not abstract greed first. It is compressed survival logic.

## Regathering Advantage

Russia repeatedly rewards the actor who can recentralize authority after disorder.

That makes fragmentation politically weak and restoration politically strong. In this game, the regathering center usually claims legitimacy through necessity before it claims it through law or prosperity.

## Licensed Survival Memory

Horde memory matters game-theoretically because it shows a prior survival pattern:

- survive under domination
- learn the coercive grammar
- internalize it later

That makes Russian strategic interaction unusually tolerant of harsh interim forms if those forms promise later sovereign restoration.

## Depth Versus Exhaustion

Strategic depth is one of Russia's greatest recurring advantages. It is also one of its traps.

Expansion and disruption can produce security gains, but they can also accumulate burden, resistance, and sclerosis faster than the carrier can metabolize them.

## Present Game Carrier

Today the Russian game is carried through:

- the Russian Federation
- centralized restoration claims
- disruption and buffer logic
- anti-humiliation signaling
- strategic-depth thinking

The current carrier inherits an old game in modern form: avoid subordination by restoring enough coercive and spatial depth that rivals must negotiate rather than dictate.

## Boundary Rules

- Do not reduce Russian behavior to irrational paranoia.
- Do not confuse every expansionary move with durable strength.
- Do not assume regathering logic automatically produces renewal.
- Do not ignore the exhaustion costs of depth-seeking.

## Drafting Consequence

When drafting from the Russia volume, the governing question is:

**Does a proposal reduce the current carrier's felt encirclement and recognition deficit without rewarding unlimited depth-seeking or forcing Russia into a game it believes only subordinate actors accept?**
