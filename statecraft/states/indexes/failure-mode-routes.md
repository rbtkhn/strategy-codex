# Failure-Mode Routes

WORK only; not Record.

Use this index when the real question is no longer only "what does this civilization say?" but "how does this kind of civilizational pattern fail across cases?" Each route compares primary-source grammar with interpretive difficulty structure and points back into the layered CIV-STATE stack.

## Continuity Without Legitimacy

Primary-source grammar:

- continuity can be inherited, claimed, or procedurally carried long before it is fully believed
- the danger is that legal or civilizational continuity begins to outrun lived legitimacy

Interpretive difficulty structure:

- strongest where modern readers confuse institutional persistence with continued consent
- most vulnerable to flattening into either hypocrisy language or automatic continuity language

## Sacred Grammar Captured by Empire

Primary-source grammar:

- sacred or moral authorization can legitimate order, but can also be absorbed into coercive or imperial carriage
- the danger is reading sacred inheritance as automatically civilizationally clean once empire begins using it

Interpretive difficulty structure:

- strongest where doctrinal or sacred language is still live but tied to contested carriers
- most vulnerable to nostalgia and to secular flattening that erases the sacred substrate altogether

## Restoration After Humiliation

Primary-source grammar:

- humiliation memory can become both a continuity carrier and a justification for restoration
- the danger is that restoration language becomes so total that settlement, restraint, or mixed outcomes become unreadable

Interpretive difficulty structure:

- strongest where modern political narration is still saturated with memory of collapse, partition, or imposed subordination
- most vulnerable to over-identification with the restoration claim or to dismissing it as pure grievance theater

## Outward Instrument Outrunning Inherited Form

Primary-source grammar:

- a civilization or republic can inherit enough legitimacy to build powerful instruments that later exceed the form that once authorized them
- the danger is confusing possession of machinery with ongoing authority to spend it well

Interpretive difficulty structure:

- strongest where empire, alliance, finance, or command machinery survives amid growing base strain
- most vulnerable to either benevolent-administrator myth or declinist theater

## Residual Universality Under Fragmented Carriers

Primary-source grammar:

- universal form can survive in law, church, institution, and prestige after direct sovereign carriage disappears
- the danger is mistaking residual universality for living universal command

Interpretive difficulty structure:

- strongest where the civilization is remembered more as concept, residue, or standard than as direct carrier
- most vulnerable to theatrical revivalism and to false closure that seals the civilization into antiquity
