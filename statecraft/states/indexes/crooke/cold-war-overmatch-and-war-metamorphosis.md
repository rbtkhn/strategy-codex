
# Cold War Overmatch And War Metamorphosis

## Purpose

Provide a `civ-state` source scaffold for the Crooke theme in which the late-Cold-War overmatch model became an inherited but increasingly false theory of how war works.

## Cybernetic Activation

Crooke-side activation surface: [Cold War overmatch and war metamorphosis](../../../voices/crooke/cold-war-overmatch-and-war-metamorphosis.md)

## Backward Historical Question

How do militaries overlearn from one strategic era and then keep fighting according to the old lesson after the economics, technology, and psychology of war have changed?

## Source Basis

- Crooke modern examples: outspend-the-USSR model, Soviet implosion memory, deterrence decay, asymmetry after cheap electronics
- `civ-state` retrieval contract for `war`
- any future lane-local war-memory objects on procurement, overmatch, and doctrine failure

## Arc Pattern

`bounded historical success or perceived success -> institutional lesson hardens -> new conditions arrive -> doctrine persists -> unexpected failure or war-model mismatch`

## Continuity / Transformation / Current Carrier / Failure Mode

- Continuity: successful military systems generalize their winning grammar too far
- Transformation: technology and cost structures shift faster than doctrine and procurement cultures
- Current carrier: military-industrial systems, prestige weapons, training assumptions, strategic mythology
- Failure mode: old overmatch logic survives after the world it fit has disappeared

## Statecraft Use

Use this scaffold when the question is:

- whether a force model is technologically or economically obsolete
- whether procurement is buying prestige instead of strategic adaptation
- whether deterrence memory has decayed while institutions still assume it remains alive

## Counterweight

Not every legacy doctrine is wrong; some inherited patterns still matter. The burden is to show the mechanism of mismatch, not merely to celebrate novelty.

## Transaction Hooks

- ask what exact historical lesson the institution thinks it learned
- ask whether the current conflict shares the same economics and technology
- ask whether doctrine and procurement are still paying homage to an extinct strategic environment

## Citation Preference

1. Use future lane-local war objects when they exist.
2. Use this scaffold for the pattern of overlearned Cold War success and mutated war conditions.
3. Use legacy `civ-mem` only if a specific lineage of overmatch thought needs deeper recovery.
