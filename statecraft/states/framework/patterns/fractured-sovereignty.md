# Fractured Sovereignty

WORK only; not Record.

## Question

What happens when a civilization's continuity remains real, but sovereign authority becomes divided across multiple carriers, jurisdictions, or successor forms?

## Pattern

Fractured sovereignty means the continuity chain survives, but no single carrier fully monopolizes it. Authority remains real, but dispersed, contested, and layered.

Typical signs:

- multiple live claimants or residues
- church/state or successor-order overlap
- continuity without one totalizing center
- settlement language constrained by dispersed inheritance

## Rome

Rome is the strongest case in the late shelf. The pattern becomes especially visible in:

- papacy
- successor monarchies
- empire residue
- Latin-Catholic world civilization under fractured sovereignty

Rome does not lose continuity here. It keeps continuity while losing singular sovereign concentration.

## China

China is comparatively weak here because its strongest mechanism tries to overcome fracture by reconcentration. Fragmentation matters enormously in Chinese history, but it is usually treated as a condition to be overcome rather than a lasting constitutional grammar.

## Persia

Persia shows the pattern partially in some later phases, especially under concession pressure, Great Game compression, and modern external constraints. But Persia's stronger tendency is toward reconstitution of usable sovereignty rather than acceptance of stable long-term fracture.

## Statecraft use

Use this pattern when the live object turns on:

- multiple continuity-bearing authorities
- partial sovereignty rather than total loss
- successor-order settlement
- drafting language that must respect layered rather than singular inheritance
