## Why era law exists

Civilizational Statecraft is not organized by calendar convenience alone. Its era cuts are **form-historical**: they mark shifts in how inherited order, outward instrument, and present carrier are carried — not merely when events happened.

Use era law as **background architecture** when chronology, shelf choice, or post-1991 command form matters. It is **not** a mandatory first router. Identify the [governing layer](doctrine.md) first; open an era shelf when periodization clarifies the read.

## The five eras

| Era | Closes at | Form-historical meaning |
|-----|-----------|-------------------------|
| **Ancient** | `476` | Durable founding form: mythic opening, early sovereignty grammar, law-religion synthesis, first imperial universalism |
| **Medieval** | `1453` | Continuity through transformation: confessional mutation, reconcentration, legal inheritance, bridge forms |
| **Colonial** | `1815` | Outward projection age: expansion, concession pressure, corridor leverage, imperial competition, hemispheric prerogative |
| **Industrial** | `1991` | Mass-state and command form: industrial war, revolution, party-state consolidation, oil sovereignty, Cold War endpoint |
| **Cybernetic** | post-`1991` | System-administered order: deterrence, sanctions, networks, platform authority, signal management, managed interdependence |

Short mnemonic:

```text
Ancient → Medieval → Colonial → Industrial → Cybernetic
476       1453       1815       1991         post-1991
```

## Cybernetic, not Digital

Prefer **Cybernetic** over **Digital** for the post-1991 era.

The governing forms are deterrence geometry, sanctions survival, computation as command, finance and supply-chain pressure, identity rails, and platform-mediated authority — not consumer technology alone. When a live object is clearly post-1991 and organized by system administration rather than by industrial mass mobilization alone, Cybernetic is usually the right overlay.

## Volume era spine

Each volume nests its primary and secondary source shelves through a shared preferred spine. Not every volume literally opens at Ancient.

| Volume | Era files exposed |
|--------|-------------------|
| **China** | Ancient · Medieval · Colonial · Industrial · Cybernetic |
| **Persia** | Ancient · Medieval · Colonial · Industrial · Cybernetic |
| **Rome** | Ancient · Medieval · Colonial · Industrial · Cybernetic |
| **Russia** | Medieval · Colonial · Industrial · Cybernetic |
| **America** | Medieval · Colonial · Industrial · Cybernetic |

**Late opening** does not mean weaker civilization-state seriousness. Russia and America are documentary late-open cases: the sovereignty chain becomes legible before the full ancient shelf does. See the [Volume Map](../volumes/README.md).

Volume order follows **sovereignty-chain emergence** (China → Persia → Rome → Russia → America), not the earliest possible mythic or ethnocultural precursor.

## Industrial / Cybernetic split

Hard rule for bibliography retrieval:

- **Industrial** shelves terminate at **`1991`**
- **Cybernetic** shelves begin **after `1991`**
- Keep post-1991 source doors out of Industrial files

This boundary is load-bearing for Persia (revolution, war, oil, post-Cold War deterrence), China (Deng recomposition through the 1991 horizon), Russia (Soviet collapse and post-Soviet restoration), and America (unipolar command and later system stress).

## How to use era law

Use era law when:

- the operator must choose among era-matched primary or secondary shelves inside one volume
- a live object spans a boundary (e.g. late Industrial vs early Cybernetic command)
- chronology error would misread legitimacy, carrier shift, or instrument form

Do **not** use era law when:

- the governing pair is still unclear — return to the [Civilizational Statecraft Framework](doctrine.md) first
- the problem is already clearly comparative across civilizations — use [Continuity Mechanism](continuity.md) or the [Pattern Library](patterns/README.md)
- the problem is vocabulary or evidence law — use [Glossary](../glossary.md) or [Hybrid References](../hybrid-references.md)

### Read sequence inside a volume

After the volume introduction and when sources govern:

1. open the volume **shelf reader** for era routing prose
2. choose the era file matching the object's form-historical center of gravity
3. read **primary** before **secondary** within that era
4. return upward to **Civilization** or **Empire** once chronology is clarified

Era files clarify and bound the shelf; they do not replace volume chapters.

## Era signals (quick routing)

| If the object turns on… | Start era |
|-------------------------|-----------|
| founding myth, mandate grammar, early law-religion synthesis | **Ancient** |
| confessional transformation, reconcentration, inherited legal bridge | **Medieval** |
| expansion, concession, corridor pressure, imperial competition | **Colonial** |
| mass mobilization, revolution, party-state, industrial war, Cold War carry | **Industrial** |
| deterrence, sanctions, networks, platform authority, post-1991 command | **Cybernetic** |

When two eras both seem active, ask which **form** is governing behavior now — not which century produced the rhetoric.
