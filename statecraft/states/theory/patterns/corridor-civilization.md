# Corridor Civilization

WORK only; not Record.

## Question

How does a civilization gain strategic leverage by occupying the routes between other major centers rather than only by conquering them directly?

## Pattern

A corridor civilization extracts value, influence, and strategic room by sitting between poles. It is neither mere transit space nor merely peripheral. It turns route position into civilizational leverage.

Typical signs:

- intermediary advantage
- trade and route extraction
- east-west or sea-land linkage
- leverage without total dominion over both ends

## Rome

Rome participates in corridor logic, especially in Mediterranean and successor-maritime forms, but it is not best described by this pattern. Rome more often tries to become the center rather than to remain the intermediary. Corridor logic matters, but it is not Rome's defining comparative pattern.

## China

China is a partial case. It is a terminal or originating pole more often than the intermediary itself. Silk, maritime trade, and continental linkages matter, but China is usually one end of the route system rather than the route-bearing hinge in the Persian sense.

## Persia

Persia is the strongest case. It sits as:

- Silk Road nexus
- plateau corridor
- Gulf / Indian Ocean hinge
- intermediary between China/India and the Mediterranean

The pattern remains live even when the route form changes from overland to maritime to sanctions-era economic exit and optional partnership.

Use this pattern when the live question turns on:

- route leverage
- intermediary value
- chokepoint or corridor bargaining power
- why a civilization may prefer protected circulation to terminal conquest
