# Ben-Gurion, Clausewitz, And Redemptive Permanent War

## Purpose

Provide a `civ-emp` source scaffold for the Crooke theme in which bounded political war gives way to permanent-security and redemptive war logic.

## Modern Activation

Crooke-side activation surface: [Ben-Gurion, Clausewitz, and redemptive permanent war](../../../../../speakers/crooke/themes/ben-gurion-clausewitz-and-redemptive-permanent-war.md)

## Backward Historical Question

How does war cease to serve politics and become a permanent or sacred instrument aimed at total security, redemption, or exterminatory certainty?

## Source Basis

- Crooke modern examples: Ben-Gurion, Clausewitz, German permanent-security logic, Nuremberg
- `civ-emp` retrieval contracts for `god`, `peace`, and adjacent law-memory work
- any future sacred-political or legal-moral source objects

## Arc Pattern

`bounded political force -> perceived existential trauma -> security absolutism -> sacred or redemptive fusion -> permanent war or exterminatory logic`

## Continuity / Transformation / Current Carrier / Failure Mode

- Continuity: states under pressure often move from prudence to absolute security claims
- Transformation: the carrier can shift from secular raison d'etat to fused sacred-political mission
- Current carrier: political theology, military doctrine, trauma language, elite security discourse
- Failure mode: politics disappears and war becomes self-justifying

## Statecraft Use

Use this scaffold when evaluating:

- whether force still has a political end state
- whether security doctrine has become redemptive or absolutist
- whether legal and moral limits are being dissolved by existential framing

## Counterweight

Not every severe security doctrine is sacred or exterminatory. The burden is to show where politics actually disappears and totalizing mission takes over.

## Transaction Hooks

- ask what political condition would count as enough
- ask whether civilians, future generations, or whole populations are being absorbed into threat logic
- ask whether the doctrine still recognizes borders, limits, and coexistence
