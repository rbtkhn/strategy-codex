# Bombing Is Not Political Success

## Purpose

Provide a `civ-emp` source scaffold for the Freeman theme in which coercion, bombardment, or battlefield punishment fail to convert automatically into recognized order or durable settlement.

## Backward Historical Question

When does coercion fail to convert into settlement, and what kinds of war memory repeatedly expose the gap between destructive power and political success?

## Arc Pattern

Military punishment can destroy, degrade, and terrify without establishing a political end state that the losing side or the broader system can live with. War memory matters because actors repeatedly relearn this limit at very high cost.

Short form:

`coercive superiority -> destruction -> false expectation of compliance -> endurance or adaptation -> settlement gap`

## Continuity / Transformation / Current Carrier / Failure Mode

- Continuity: war planners often assume physical punishment yields political submission
- Transformation: endurance, logistics repair, external support, or unresolved legitimacy can keep the political problem alive
- Current carrier: bombing doctrines, escalation pressure, coercive signaling, and war-termination fantasy
- Failure mode: the lesson becomes pacifist cliché rather than a specific test of what kind of coercion can and cannot carry settlement

## Statecraft Use

Use this scaffold when:

- testing war-termination logic
- asking whether escalation has a political end state behind it
- identifying when battlefield loss constrains negotiation space

## Counterweight

The point is not that force never matters. The point is that force without a political architecture often misleads its user about what has been achieved.

Counterweight rule:

`distinguish tactical effectiveness from strategic and settlement effectiveness`

## Transaction Hooks

- ask what political end state the coercion is supposed to produce
- ask what evidence would show bombing has failed to create compliance
- ask what settlement structure would still be required if coercion succeeds tactically
