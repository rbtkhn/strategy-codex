## Family Claim

Remembered exclusion turns settlement fragile when symbolic inclusion and material access diverge.

Across all four lanes, the same failure recurs:

- a power is told it is being recognized
- but the route map, guarantor structure, corridor design, or implementation chain still says the decisive room is elsewhere

At that point the settlement starts reading as managed inclusion rather than real participation. The text may sound balanced. The architecture still feels prewritten.

So the set-wide rule is not merely that powers want respect. It is:

- a direct settlement becomes durable only when status recognition and usable access geography point in the same direction

## Cross-Lane Matrix

| Lane | What real inclusion looks like | What exclusion memory reads first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| America | Shared route authorship, lawful burden-sharing, open but bounded access, and settlement terms that do not presume permanent American room ownership | Symbolic consultation paired with corridor or maritime rules that Washington still quietly writes alone |
| Russia | Visible recognition, finite exposure relief, usable access routes, and bounded security geometry that stops before neighbor absorption | Consultation theater that leaves real security design elsewhere, or access language that disguises permanent subordination |
| China | Participation in route and strait design, continuity of flow without public containment, and implementation that does not force visible tutelage | Face-saving words combined with corridor or maritime arrangements that still code China as a supervised mover |
| Iran | Dignified participation in Gulf and corridor design, relief-linked access, and restraint that visibly reduces bypass and permission dependence | Recognition language that leaves routing, relief, or review controlled elsewhere while Tehran is asked to normalize vulnerability |

## Shared Structural Rule

The four lanes converge on one deeper rule:

- exclusion is remembered most strongly through the room's operating map

Formal invitation is too weak a measure. Every lane asks a harder question:

- Who writes the routes?
- Who decides usable access?
- Who carries review?
- Who can reopen, narrow, or suspend the arrangement later?

If the answer remains lopsided, then the excluded party will usually experience the settlement as a stylized version of prior management rather than as a new order.

That is why this family sits naturally between `geo` and `peace`.

- `geo` names the material substrate: corridors, seas, chokepoints, ports, plateau routes, shipping lanes, freight systems
- `peace` names the settlement grammar: recognition, review, successor carry, dignity, lawful ownership
- `empire-instrument` names the outward carrier that actually operates on the route map

The family-level lesson is:

- recognition without access is decorative
- access without recognition is domination
- direct settlement becomes real only when both can be inherited together

## Geographic Carrier Comparison

The lanes also show that exclusion memory is never abstract. It attaches to concrete carrier systems.

- **America** experiences the issue through maritime order, alliance basing, and sanctions-finance geography. Others may hear inclusion language while still feeling that Washington retains the right to write the corridor logic.
- **Russia** experiences it through warning-time corridors, Black Sea/Baltic/Arctic access, and security buffers. Moscow is highly sensitive to settlements that acknowledge Russia verbally while leaving its exposure map politically authored elsewhere.
- **China** experiences it through ports, straits, insurers, finance, and route continuity. Beijing can accept ambiguity, but it is much less willing to accept arrangements that leave China materially dependent inside a vocabulary of containment written by others.
- **Iran** experiences it through Hormuz, Gulf participation, sanctions routing, corridor inclusion, and plateau access. Tehran is unusually alert to bargains that offer dignity rhetorically while treating Iranian material access as revocable permission.

So the empire-side bridge is not incidental here. The empire instrument often supplies the very geography that decides whether inclusion is real or staged.

## Main Failure Modes

Across the set, remembered exclusion most often re-enters through five patterns:

1. **Managed invitation**
   A power is formally present but the decisive architecture is already settled elsewhere.

2. **Symbolic parity / material asymmetry**
   The language says consultation or recognition, but access, routes, or relief remain discretionary and one-sided.

3. **Bypass design**
   The arrangement solves the problem by routing around the actor whose inclusion supposedly matters.

4. **Supervised participation**
   The actor is allowed to remain in the room only under monitoring, transparency, or compliance language it cannot domestically carry as sovereign.

5. **Inherited map-writing**
   Later carriers quietly discover that one side still owns the right to reinterpret corridors, chokepoints, review mechanisms, or normal access.

These failures look different in each lane, but they all describe the same deeper break:

- the settlement speaks in shared-order language while preserving unequal authorship of the geographic room

## Strongest Shared Falsifiers

Across all four lanes, the family is probably being mishandled when one or more of these conditions appear:

1. the settlement cannot say what concrete access pattern has improved for the supposedly included actor
2. review, relief, or route usability still depend on permission from the same outside structure that earlier generated the exclusion memory
3. the bargain describes status recognition clearly but leaves corridor, port, strait, or shipping authorship ambiguous
4. later carriers would have to choose between admitting material asymmetry and pretending symbolic inclusion was always enough
5. the room can only remain stable so long as the excluded actor suppresses domestic recognition of how little changed materially

These falsifiers travel well because they all reveal the same mismatch:

- the text widened the circle
- the map did not

## Drafting Implications

For the helix-first corpus, this family now gives a reusable drafting test:

1. What concrete route, corridor, chokepoint, port, or access object is this settlement actually redistributing or stabilizing?
2. How does the bargain make the supposedly included actor more than a managed participant in that geography?
3. Which review, guarantor, or implementation carrier keeps access real after the initiating crisis passes?
4. What would make the same architecture read domestically as bypass, tutelage, or containment instead?
5. Can later carriers describe the room as jointly inhabitable without hiding who still writes the map?

If a clause cannot answer those questions, it may still quiet a crisis temporarily, but it has not yet solved the stronger statecraft problem of exclusion memory inside direct great-power settlement.
