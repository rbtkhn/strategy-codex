# Barnes arc

Purpose: hold Barnes as a whole speaker object across hosts and across the January through May 2026 materialized run without pretending he already owns a Johnson-style month lattice.

## Core claim

Barnes is a real cross-host statecraft speaker object because he repeatedly performs the same translation job across hosts:

- turn strategic escalation into **domestic political survivability**
- turn war talk into **constitutional-process and war-powers exposure**
- turn negotiation stories into **executive-chaos and room-dynamics** arguments
- turn foreign-policy crises into **coalition, donor, lobby, and electoral-liability** readings

The dominant Barnes lane is still the [Davis host shelf](/C:/dev/strategy-codex/statecraft/hosts/davis/README.md), but the broader object is no longer honestly described as `Davis only`.

## Durable cross-host role

Barnes is most useful when a shelf would otherwise stay trapped in military, diplomatic, or geopolitical altitude language and needs a U.S. internal plane:

- who can authorize something
- who can legally sustain it
- who politically pays for it
- which factions inside the administration are coherent or incoherent
- whether a negotiation path is real, fake, or internally unsellable

That role survives host translation strongly enough that Barnes now reads as a speaker object, not just a good guest episode.

## Recurring retrieval jobs

Open Barnes when the notebook needs one or more of the following:

- domestic-liability translation of a foreign-policy crisis
- constitutional or war-powers framing on the U.S. side
- executive-instability diagnosis rather than battlefield diagnosis
- White House room-dynamics explanation
- donor, lobby, coalition, or electoral-pressure explanation of escalation behavior

Do not open Barnes first for ORBAT, engineering, battlefield verification, or primary geopolitical altitude.
