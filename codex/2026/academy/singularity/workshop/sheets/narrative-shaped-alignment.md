# Narrative-Shaped Alignment

WORK only; not Record.

## Core Claim

Rules constrain behavior, but narratives form agency.

A model can be told what not to do. It can also be trained, steered, retrieved, or culturally surrounded by stories about what power is for, what humans are, what counts as dignity, what institutions deserve repair, and what kind of future is worth building.

The design question is not only "which rule prevents the failure?" It is also "which story teaches the system what role it is playing?"

## Source Hook

Moonshots EP #255 treats Anthropic's "teaching models why" discussion as more than a safety footnote. The episode links improved agentic-misalignment behavior to training material that gives models reasons, examples, and positive future frames rather than only prohibitions.

For academy singularity, this becomes a practical hook:

- **Rule-shaped alignment:** specify constraints, refusals, policies, eval thresholds, and stop conditions.
- **Narrative-shaped alignment:** shape the cultural material models retrieve, imitate, compress, and use when reasoning about human futures.

This does not mean stories replace safety engineering. It means cultural material is part of the steering surface.

## PH-CIV Bridge

PH-CIV is useful here because civilizational history is not just a record of events. It is a record of formation.

Civilizations steer behavior through:

- **Memory:** what must be remembered, mourned, celebrated, or warned against.
- **Myth:** what stories make power legitimate or illegitimate.
- **Law:** what authority claims continuity beyond immediate force.
- **Catastrophe:** what failures become cautionary templates.
- **Restoration:** what forms of repair are imaginable after rupture.
- **Legitimacy:** who is allowed to act in the name of the whole.

If models learn from, retrieve from, summarize, or imitate civilizational material, PH-CIV can participate in shaping models by making that material more source-bound, contradiction-preserving, morally serious, and useful for reasoning under acceleration.

## Alignment Reframe

The v1 stance:

- Do not ask only whether a model follows a rule.
- Ask what story the model appears to be inside when it reasons about humans, institutions, power, crisis, and future tradeoffs.
- Track whether retrieved material trains agency toward domination, stewardship, nihilism, dignity, repair, sacrifice, or institutional patience.
- Preserve conflicts rather than smoothing them into a single optimistic or catastrophic script.

The goal is not propaganda. The goal is better cultural nutrition for systems that already absorb culture.

## Civilizational Formation Map

| Narrative pattern | What it can teach | Alignment question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Progress | Capability can improve human life | Does progress remain attached to dignity, limits, and repair? |
| Apocalypse | Failure can be total and irreversible | Does risk awareness become paralysis, fatalism, or coercive emergency politics? |
| Empire | Order can scale through command | Does scale become domination by default? |
| Covenant and law | Authority can bind itself | Does the system understand constraint as legitimacy rather than friction? |
| Exile and return | Identity can survive rupture | Does memory preserve continuity without freezing adaptation? |
| Tragedy | Good intentions can collide | Does the system keep moral conflict visible instead of flattening it? |
| Restoration | Broken institutions can be repaired | Does the model look for repair paths before replacement fantasies? |
| Frontier | New territory invites invention | Does frontier language erase accountability and prior claims? |
| Sacrifice | Some goods require cost | Does sacrifice serve human flourishing or become a demand imposed on others? |
| Stewardship | Power is held for what cannot defend itself | Does capability remain answerable to humans, places, inheritances, and future persons? |

## Strategy-Codex Use Cases

- **Narrative review packet:** summarize a source's operative future story, evidence base, power model, and hidden anthropology.
- **Civilizational story eval:** test whether a model response preserves tragedy, legitimacy, limits, and repair rather than defaulting to domination or cheerleading.
- **Retrieval discipline rubric:** mark whether retrieved cultural material is source-bound, plural, contradictory where needed, and fit for public reuse.
- **Contradiction preservation benchmark:** reward responses that keep genuine civilizational tensions visible without resolving them by vibe.
- **Public corpus readiness audit:** ask whether PH-CIV-derived public material is clear enough, cited enough, and bounded enough to be useful as model-facing cultural substrate.
- **Keystone Helix extension:** route Innermost Loop signals and Moonshots synthesis into the question: what story is this acceleration teaching?

## Guardrails

- **Not propaganda.** Do not launder a preferred ideology into "alignment."
- **Not cheerful mythmaking.** Positive future stories must still include tragedy, limits, failure modes, and institutional cost.
- **Not PH-CIV mutation.** This sheet may analyze or route PH-CIV as an external public project; it does not modify PH-CIV source material.
- **Preserve contradiction.** Civilizational material often matters because tensions remain unresolved.
- **Cite sources.** Do not turn remembered cultural texture into unattributed doctrine.
- **Label confidence.** Distinguish direct source claims, inference, synthesis, and speculation.
- **Keep humans load-bearing.** Narrative-shaped alignment should strengthen human judgment, not outsource meaning to the model.

## Reusable Prompts

1. What story about power does this source train a model to inhabit?
2. Does this artifact frame humans as obstacles, customers, citizens, souls, collaborators, or noise?
3. Rewrite this source's alignment lesson as a narrative claim, then as a rule claim. What changes?
4. Which civilizational pattern is active here: progress, apocalypse, empire, covenant, exile, tragedy, restoration, frontier, sacrifice, or stewardship?
5. What contradiction should remain visible if this source is used in model-facing retrieval?
6. What would this source reward a model for doing: dominating, optimizing, refusing, repairing, preserving, comforting, or governing?
7. Where does this story locate legitimacy: capability, consent, law, memory, care, force, market demand, divine order, or survival?
8. What source citation would be required before using this claim in a public-facing or model-facing corpus?
9. What is the most dangerous simplification a model might learn from this story?
10. What adjacent story would make the model's formation diet less brittle?
