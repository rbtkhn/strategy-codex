# Churchill And Self-Narrated Victory

WORK only; not Record.

## Purpose

Study grand strategy through a principal actor's narrative. Churchill is indispensable, but also a source problem: statesmen explain themselves while shaping memory.

## Bookshelf Anchors

- **The Second World War (Shelf-0304)** - coalition politics, grand strategy, British state memory, wartime leadership, and memoir as political artifact.
- **George Marshall: Defender of the Republic (Shelf-0255)** - staff command, coalition war, mobilization, and strategic restraint.
- **Eisenhower: Soldier and President (Shelf-0258)** - supreme command, coalition management, and civil-military judgment.

## Writer-Lane Companions

- [Big Serge](../../bigserge/) when wartime narrative needs campaign-shape comparison.

## Working Questions

- What is Churchill explaining, defending, dramatizing, or omitting?
- Where does memoir become evidence, and where does it become advocacy?
- What coalition constraints shaped the decision?
- What did staff systems, industry, and allies contribute that actor narrative underplays?
- What is the difference between victory narrated and victory produced?

## Counter-Reading

Actor memoir is not disposable just because it is self-serving. It can preserve decision texture. Treat it as high-value interpretive evidence, not neutral record.

## Output Prompts

- Mark one claim as memory, one as strategy, and one as self-defense.
- Compare Churchill's narrative to a staff-command lens.
- Write one primary/interpretive lane guardrail for future use.
