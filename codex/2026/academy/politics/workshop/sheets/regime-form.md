## Working Questions

- What kind of order is this: republic, empire, democracy, oligarchy, party machine, bureaucratic state, coalition, or hybrid?
- What does this order reward: virtue, loyalty, wealth, expertise, obedience, popularity, violence, procedural control, or media skill?
- Why should people obey, and who rejects that claim?
- Which institutions can command, fund, certify, count, prosecute, obstruct, mobilize, or veto?
- What language makes the order feel legitimate?
- What party, faction, donor, media, court, agency, or street force translates belief into action?

## Counter-Reading

Regime language can become too clean. Real politics is usually mixed: constitutional ideals, party machines, personal ambition, bureaucratic inertia, and public myth all operate at once. Do not force a case into one label when the useful truth is the interaction among labels.

## Output Prompts

- Write a one-page regime note: form, legitimacy claim, persuasion mechanism, veto points, and pressure risk.
- Draw a five-node power map: executive, legislature, courts, party network, public/media field.
- Name one place where legitimacy language hides material coercion.
- Name one place where cynicism misses genuine public attachment.
