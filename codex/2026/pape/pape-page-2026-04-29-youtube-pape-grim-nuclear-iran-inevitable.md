### Verbatim

**A —** Pape says the United States has already **lost control** of the Iran war, and treats the **UAE exit from OPEC** as a visible sign of wider **GCC fragmentation**. His core claim is not simply that Iran is stronger; it is that **American system authority** is visibly weakening, so regional actors are no longer organizing around Washington as the decisive anchor. In his phrasing, Iran is emerging as a **“fourth center of world power,”** while Gulf states stop behaving like a coherent U.S.-aligned balancing bloc.

**B —** The interview then returns to Pape's familiar **chokepoint / escalation-trap** mechanism. The Strait of Hormuz is not framed as a one-time market shock but as a sequence: first **price rises**, then **physical shortages**, then broader **economic contraction** and domestic political pain. He places the system in the shortage phase already and links that to the return of a **misery-index** style politics: inflation, layoffs, route cuts, and compounding distress rather than a quick normalization.

**C —** On negotiations, Pape insists that the word **“open”** is doing misleading work. He argues that an Iranian offer to reopen the Strait still implies **Iranian control**, prior coordination, and toll extraction rather than a return to status quo freedom of navigation. He pairs that with a hardening read on the nuclear file: the Strait and the nuclear question are becoming less separable, not more, and U.S. coercion is pushing Iran toward a longer-horizon power strategy rather than capitulation.

**D —** The interview closes by widening the frame beyond the Gulf. Pape treats Russia and China not as background atmospherics but as live beneficiaries and possible co-shapers of the crisis. His strongest geopolitical claim is that the U.S. has burned so much **Patriot / THAAD** capacity that the Iran war now creates downstream exposure in Asia, especially around Taiwan. He also treats any Obama-style reconstruction of a JCPOA coalition as requiring concessions to Russia at a scale Washington is unlikely to make.
