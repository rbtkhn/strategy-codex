## Redundancy findings

- `same conclusion, different mechanism`: `law gives way to raw power` and `diplomacy becomes performance` both point to U.S. decay, but one is about legality collapse while the other is about process theater
- `same source spine, different use`: `Iran turns from target into system lever` and `China becomes the quiet beneficiary` often draw from the same Hormuz-era captures, but the first owns the direct leverage object while the second owns the second-order beneficiary logic
- `collapse pressure watch`: `U.S. domestic decay and strategic decay merge` can easily absorb material from `law gives way to raw power` if editors stop distinguishing external legality from internal constitutional decay

## Recommended actions

- keep the six-thread atlas as is
- do not merge `law` and `diplomacy`; they are adjacent but not redundant
- keep using `Distinct from:` blocks inside the atlas entries
- if future edits start routing most China material through Iran leverage, revisit whether `China becomes the quiet beneficiary` still has enough independent source weight
- if more domestic-constitutional receipts arrive on Freeman, consider a later split inside `U.S. domestic decay and strategic decay merge` only if it creates a distinct retrieval use rather than just extra nuance
