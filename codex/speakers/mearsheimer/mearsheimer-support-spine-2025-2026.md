## Core maturity claim

Mearsheimer is mature enough to deserve a stronger speaker-owned spine, but still not the same type of speaker-owned month layer as Ritter.

Why:

- the shelf has real 2025 continuity across Davis, Diesen, and Napolitano
- 2026 January, March, April, and May are all materially reusable months
- authored surfaces and non-core bench items widen the object beyond the two core host strands
- but the useful chronology still belongs more naturally to host-local arcs and the non-core bench than to speaker-native month pages

So the right reading is:

`mature speaker object with host-led chronology and a wider non-core bench`

not:

`speaker shelf waiting for native month chapters`

And under the current repo doctrine, that reading now has an explicit class name:

`host-led mature-month exception shelf`

This is not migration lag. It is the current best structural description of the shelf.

## Reinforcing orbit vs non-core bench

Use this distinction carefully:

- `reinforcing orbit` = routeable host lane material that strengthens the Mearsheimer shelf without becoming an equal helix strand
- `non-core appearance bench` = resolved shelf material outside the stable core host lanes, including authored surfaces and non-core interviews

Examples:

- `Napolitano x Mearsheimer` is reinforcing orbit
- `authored Mearsheimer`, `Hedges x Mearsheimer`, and `Redacted x Mearsheimer` belong to the non-core bench

This is why Mearsheimer is broader than Macgregor while still remaining host-led at the month level.

## Why this shelf is still not Ritter-shaped

Ritter owns more speaker-side chronology because his shelf needs speaker-native bounded month support.

- the shelf widens not only by month density but also by non-core bench growth
- speaker-native month pages would mostly restate host chronology and bench variety rather than clarify ownership
