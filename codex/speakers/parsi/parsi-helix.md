## Core claim

The Parsi helix is a **two-strand policy-process helix**:

- The **authored strand** states the diplomacy mechanism: symbolic politics must become architecture, coercion cannot substitute for settlement, Israel repeatedly drives escalation demand, and Iran's leverage must be converted into guarantees, sanctions relief, and regional security architecture.
- The **interview strand** tests that mechanism under host pressure: Davis asks what makes coercion painful enough to stop repeat attacks, Napolitano asks how Washington misread the war, Duran/Mercouris asks what blockade failure implies for statecraft, and Diesen asks what the Iran war means for American primacy.

This is not the same shape as Crooke or Ritter. Parsi has fewer host-local strands, but his authored policy spine is stronger and more procedural. The helix matters because it prevents the notebook from treating his interviews as loose commentary or his essays as isolated papers.

## Orthogonality guide

Quick separation rule:

- The `authored diplomacy strand` is where Parsi states settlement architecture, sanctions logic, and implementation-heavy policy mechanism in his own terms.
- The `interview pressure strand` is where that mechanism is pressured by host demands for blame, leverage, off-ramp design, and primacy interpretation.

Do not use the interview layer as a substitute for Parsi's authored policy architecture when drafting statecraft language, and do not use the authored ledger as a substitute for host-pressure translation when the question is how his diplomacy logic behaves in live argument. The helix only works if authored procedure and interview testing remain separate.
