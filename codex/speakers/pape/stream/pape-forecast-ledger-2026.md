## Phase Spine

**Smart Bomb Trap, February 25-March 4.** The sequence starts with the claim that precision bombing creates a false sense of controllable coercion. The trap is not simply military overconfidence; it is the conversion of tactical success into political commitment, verification uncertainty, decapitation pressure, and nationalist hardening. Day-one military optics are treated as especially misleading.

**Systemic Escalation, March 5-March 13.** The war widens from strike assessment to system behavior. Pape tracks Gulf chokepoints, the Caucasus, victory narratives, leadership transition signals, Russia's intelligence lifeline, and the difference between battlefield events and strategic patterns. The main arc: the United States can appear to be winning tactically while losing control of the escalation system.

**Ground / Logistics Threshold, March 16-March 29.** The center shifts to the global economy and to the logistics evidence that would precede a ground war. Pape argues that rhetoric matters less than deployments, sustainment, ports, airfields, Marines, airborne forces, and operational throughput. Vietnam becomes the historical analogy for when air wars begin pulling ground forces into the logic of the campaign.

**Blockade / Shortage Clock, April 2-April 24.** The conflict becomes a coupled military-economic blockade problem. The key mechanism is no longer oil price alone, but insurance, shipping refusal, inventories, reserves, LNG, diesel, jet fuel, fertilizer, and political intervention. The essays introduce clock discipline: blockade clocks, shortage clocks, and the moment when short-run buffers stop working.

**Global Contraction / China Relative Gain, May 3-May 18.** The sequence culminates in a claim that the first 60 days changed the structure of the crisis and that the next 30 days could move from shortage to synchronized contraction. China becomes the central relative-gain actor: not immune, but better buffered, more industrially deep, and strategically less trapped than the United States and its exposed allies.

Power metric guard:

- **India** remains the stronger fourth-comprehensive-power candidate by population, market scale, technical depth, military scale, and long-run demographic-industrial potential.
- **Iran** is the fourth-center candidate in Pape's narrower sense: resource self-sufficiency, strategic depth with Russia and China, Hormuz leverage, denial capacity, nuclear latency, and ability to make global systems respond.
- Do not write that Iran "replaces India" as the fourth world power. Write that Pape is changing the metric from **comprehensive capability** to **active coercive system leverage**.
