# Predictive History YouTube Comment Lab

WORK only; not Record.

## Purpose

Use this sheet when `ph-civ` is the practical client and the question is whether AI-assisted public interventions can create real distribution, audience formation, and reusable outreach leverage.

This is not merely a comment campaign. Inside `singularity-academy`, it is a public-interface lab for testing:

- whether AI can help generate sharper public reasoning
- whether beginner or cohort contributors can help draft or review useful comments
- whether the workflow improves over repeated cycles
- whether public-output systems can create compounding attention rather than one-off noise

## Client Frame

Treat `ph-civ` as the client substrate.

The comments are in service of:

- attracting the right viewers into deeper Predictive History curiosity
- creating profile clicks, replies, and channel recognition
- improving reader acquisition for public `ph-civ` surfaces
- building a repeatable public-distribution loop rather than isolated clever comments

The comments are not the product. They are a distribution and positioning interface for the product.

## Boundary

Keep these two things distinct:

- `ph-civ` repo/folder work: corpus, chapter spine, commentary, source hygiene, navigation, publication structure, and editorial integrity
- `singularity-academy` comment lab work: distribution experiments, audience formation, public-interface testing, and AI-assisted outreach workflow

The first is the product and canonical substrate.

The second is a WORK-only promotion/control-plane experiment that serves the product.

Do not treat comment activity as if it were corpus progress.
Do not treat corpus maintenance as if it were the same thing as public distribution.

Crossing the membrane should be deliberate:

- use `ph-civ` surfaces when the work changes the corpus itself
- use this lab when the work changes how the corpus is introduced, noticed, discussed, or distributed in public
- if a public intervention generates a durable editorial insight, route that insight back into the proper `ph-civ` or strategy surface intentionally rather than letting the distinction blur

## Cross-Lane Weave

| Surface | Role |
|---|---|
| `ph-civ` | client objective: turn YouTube attention into durable reader interest |
| `work-business` | tests whether public attention can later become economic demand, trust, or monetizable audience |
| `work-cici` | supplies apprentice-safe drafting, review, and scoreboard tracking work |
| `work-dev` | owns templates, scoring, tracking, and any tooling or control-plane support |
| `singularity-academy` | evaluates whether the loop is real, compounding, and reusable |
| Operator | approves posting, protects tone/doctrine fit, and decides what patterns deserve reuse |

## Working Thesis

The singularity claim here is not "AI can write comments."

The claim is narrower and stronger:

AI plus human review may be able to create a repeatable public-reasoning loop that improves distribution quality, lowers drafting friction, and teaches newer contributors how to make useful interventions in a real audience environment.

## Allowed Work

- lecture-specific comment drafting
- candidate comment ranking
- doctrine-fit review
- comment archetype refinement
- quality and reach tracking
- reply triage notes
- proof-packet style posting receipts

Not the purpose of this module:

- generic praise comments
- spammy self-promotion
- engagement bait
- comment flooding across the whole channel without prioritization
- auto-posting without operator review

## Control Plane

Human-gated by default.

AI may help with:

- extracting the lecture's core claim
- proposing 1-3 comment candidates
- labeling candidate type: pattern, distinction, bridge, counter-intuition, or question
- checking for repetition, vagueness, or hype drift

Human review still owns:

- final selection
- posting
- tone calibration
- judgment about whether the comment actually serves `ph-civ`
- escalation when a reply thread becomes strategic, adversarial, or relationship-sensitive
- preserving the boundary between corpus/editorial work and promotion/distribution work

## Comment Archetypes

- `pattern`: name the civilizational or institutional pattern beneath the episode
- `distinction`: sharpen one useful conceptual distinction
- `bridge`: connect the episode to a broader Predictive History frame
- `counter-intuition`: name the less obvious takeaway
- `question`: invite serious replies without sounding generic

The right optimization target is not "most comments posted." It is "most useful public interventions per unit of operator effort."

## Workflow

1. Pick target videos by leverage, not by backlog completion.
2. Extract the video's central claim and likely audience.
3. Draft 1-3 candidate comments.
4. Score them for doctrine fit, usefulness, specificity, and tone.
5. Operator picks one.
6. Post and log a public receipt.
7. Track quality and reach separately.
8. Review weekly for reusable patterns.

## Metrics

### Quality

Measure whether the comment:

- is specific to the lecture
- says something worth reading
- sounds like a serious human reader
- avoids hype, boilerplate, and template smell
- offers a clean bridge into the Predictive History frame

### Reach

Measure whether the comment:

- was actually posted on a relevant target video
- received likes, replies, or creator interaction
- produced visible thread engagement
- created signs of audience recognition over time

### Learning

Measure whether the system:

- reduces operator drafting time
- teaches newer contributors what a good comment looks like
- improves candidate quality over repeated cycles
- produces reusable archetypes or scoring rules

## Acceptance Criteria

Treat the loop as worth keeping only if it produces all three:

- visible public receipts
- comment quality that holds up under review
- evidence that drafting, selection, or training gets easier over repeated cycles

Weak evidence:

- many posted comments with no doctrine fit
- comments that sound machine-made
- reach without seriousness
- operator hand-editing everything so heavily that the system is not actually teaching or scaling

Strong evidence:

- multiple lecture-specific comments rated high quality
- some public engagement from the right audience
- one or more reusable comment patterns
- newer contributors producing better drafts over time

## First Scoreboard Wedge

Use the `Predictive History comment rollout` row in the `cici-ai` dashboard as the live scoreboard.

Track:

- comments posted
- quality average
- reach average
- notes on what archetypes worked
- what AI did versus what the operator still had to rescue
