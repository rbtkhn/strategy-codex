# Academy Method


Academy shelves are active study surfaces. They organize canons, workshops, and bridges so the notebook can turn reading into reusable judgment.

## Pattern

Every mature academy shelf should develop three layers:

1. **Canon.** A reading path, source shelf, or curated set of anchors that answers "where do I begin?"
2. **Workshop.** An active-use bench with methods, instruments, sheets, drills, and output prompts.
3. **Bridge.** A link to adjacent shelves, strategy work, Predictive History, speaker lanes, or Bookshelf rows.

The canon keeps attention disciplined. The workshop makes the canon usable. The bridge prevents the shelf from becoming a private cabinet.

## Cross-Shelf Rule

Pressure may explain action, but it must not excuse it.

When a shelf names pressure, it must also preserve agency:

- What pressure made action likely?
- What alternatives remained?
- Who commanded, executed, benefited, or refused?
- What moral risk appears if the pressure becomes an excuse?
- What would be lost if agency were erased?

## Bridge Trigger

Use a bridge when a shelf's strength starts to become a blind spot.

- Theology needs the military bridge when doctrine floats above command, force, institutions, logistics, or execution.
- Military needs the theology bridge when maps, losses, targets, or victory language begin to erase faces, conscience, sacred legitimacy, or moral risk.
- Statecraft needs both bridges when necessity, order, raison d'etat, or legitimacy starts doing too much explanatory work.

The bridge is not a detour. It is the check that keeps a shelf from becoming fluent in only one kind of reality.

## Source Boundary

Academy shelves do not replace raw-input, speaker objects, source corpora, Bookshelf catalog rows, or Record surfaces. They may link, compare, and produce WORK notes. They do not merge into the Record.

## Maturity Checklist

A mature shelf should have:

- a reading path or canon
- reusable instruments
- source and provenance boundaries
- at least one bridge to another academy shelf or strategy surface
- test prompts that force the shelf to produce usable output
- clear distinction between primary sources, interpretive companions, and operator synthesis

## Output Standard

An academy session should leave behind one useful thing:

- a sharper question
- an instrument
- a source sheet
- a cross-shelf bridge note
- a Bookshelf path
- a correction to an overread
- a reusable warning
