# Instruments

WORK only; not Record.

These are reusable interpretive tools for academy military, strategy work, and cross-shelf analysis.

## Campaign Reading Sheet

Use when reading a campaign, battle, or theater narrative.

- What is the political objective?
- What is the operational objective?
- What forces are available and where are they?
- What terrain, weather, distance, and infrastructure matter?
- What can each side supply, repair, rotate, replace, or sustain?
- What is the likely decision point?

## Escalation Ladder Test

Use when a conflict may move to a higher level of violence or commitment.

- What action changes the conflict's scale?
- What actor is forced to respond?
- What off-ramp remains after the escalation?
- What domestic or alliance pressure makes restraint harder?
- What new target set becomes thinkable?

## Civil-Military Control Check

Use when commanders, presidents, cabinets, parties, or publics pull in different directions.

- Who has legal command authority?
- Who has practical control of the forces?
- What advice was given, rejected, or hidden?
- Is military success being used to override political judgment?
- Is political theater forcing military risk?

## Logistics Vs Narrative Test

Use when a campaign story sounds coherent but material support is unclear.

- What tonnage, fuel, munitions, maintenance, manpower, and medical flow is required?
- What route carries it?
- What can the enemy disrupt?
- What rate of loss can be absorbed?
- What part of the narrative collapses if the logistics are wrong?

## Attrition Vs Decision Test

Use when analysis claims victory, collapse, or stalemate.

- Is the conflict being decided by maneuver, attrition, exhaustion, morale, politics, or external supply?
- What evidence would show a real decision?
- What evidence would show only local success?
- Which side can replace losses faster?
- What timeline does the claim require?

## Command Responsibility Check

Use when pressure fields are real but responsibility must not disappear.

- Who ordered the action?
- Who executed it?
- Who enabled or supplied it?
- What alternatives were visible at the time?
- Who benefited?
- Who suffered?
- What responsibility remains after pressure is named?

## Primary Interpretive Lane Guardrail

Use when working with Big Serge, Simplicius, or any writer lane.

- What is primary evidence?
- What is official claim?
- What is imagery or map interpretation?
- What is writer synthesis?
- What is operator inference?
- What claim needs a clearer source boundary before it enters analysis?

## Face Category Map-Abstraction Check

Use when military analysis becomes too clean.

- What map category is replacing concrete persons?
- Which civilians, soldiers, prisoners, families, or cities are hidden by the abstraction?
- What action becomes easier once the abstraction takes over?
- What would change if the face returned to view?
- Does the analysis still need to recommend the action after the face is restored?

## Theology Bridge Check

Use when a campaign or strategy claim depends on sacred legitimacy, martyrdom, sacrifice, providence, holy order, civilizational mission, or moral purification.

- What has been made sacred: ruler, people, land, victory, suffering, revenge, or order?
- Who benefits from the sacred framing?
- What violence becomes easier to justify once the sacred framing is accepted?
- What conscience claim resists the framing?
- Does the analysis still preserve command responsibility?
