# Bookshelf Crosswalk


This crosswalk wires academy-politics into the Bookshelf as a study path, not a ranking. It is title-first for reading, with `Shelf-*` ids for catalog lookup.

## Reading Path

1. **Plato: The Republic (Shelf-0041)** - Plato; justice, education, regime form, philosopher rule, civic myth, and the temptation to make politics metaphysical.
2. **The Landmark Thucydides (Shelf-0003)** - Thucydides; fear, interest, honor, democracy, empire, alliance logic, faction, and escalation.
3. **The Landmark Julius Caesar (Shelf-0007)** - Julius Caesar; command memoir, republican crisis, conquest, propaganda, civil-military ambition, and political war.
4. **Tacitus: Annals and Histories (Shelf-0045)** - Tacitus; empire, court politics, tyranny, corruption, succession, and the moral psychology of rule.
5. **The Prince (Shelf-0211)** - Niccolo Machiavelli; necessity, virtu, fortune, force, reputation, and the founding shock of modern statecraft.
6. **Utopia (Shelf-0212)** - Thomas More; ideal commonwealth, property, law, social design, and the ambiguity of political perfection.
7. **The Social Contract and The Discourses (Shelf-0121)** - Jean-Jacques Rousseau; sovereignty, general will, inequality, civic religion, and revolutionary legitimacy.
8. **Reflections on the Revolution in France (Shelf-0197)** - Edmund Burke; inheritance, prudence, prescription, revolution, and the defense of political continuity.
9. **The Wealth of Nations (Shelf-0199)** - Adam Smith; political economy, markets, moral order, commerce, state capacity, and commercial society.
10. **The Communist Manifesto (Shelf-0215)** - Karl Marx & Friedrich Engels; class conflict, capitalism, revolution, party, historical rupture, and mass politics.
11. **Democracy in America (Shelf-0262)** - Alexis de Tocqueville; equality, association, majority power, localism, religion, manners, and democratic anxiety.
12. **A History of the English-Speaking Peoples (Shelf-0305)** - Winston S. Churchill; English constitutional continuity, parliament, empire, liberty language, and historical self-narration.
13. **A New World Begins (Shelf-0086)** - Jeremy D. Popkin; French Revolution, regime collapse, popular sovereignty, terror, and the modern revolutionary grammar.
14. **Paine: Collected Writings (Shelf-0105)** - Thomas Paine; pamphlet politics, rights, revolution, republican persuasion, and democratic mobilization.
15. **Jefferson: Writings (Shelf-0106)** - Thomas Jefferson; declaration, republican liberty, agrarian imagination, constitutional tension, and founding contradiction.
16. **Madison: Writings (Shelf-0107)** - James Madison; faction, constitutional design, federalism, representation, and institutional constraint.
17. **Hamilton: Writings (Shelf-0108)** - Alexander Hamilton; executive energy, finance, union, administration, political economy, and national power.
18. **Founding Partisans (Shelf-0091)** - H. W. Brands; party formation, founding conflict, factional legitimacy, and the early republic as political contest.
19. **The Age of Jackson (Shelf-0094)** - Arthur M. Schlesinger Jr.; mass democracy, party organization, executive populism, patronage, and Jacksonian realignment.
20. **The Years of Lyndon Johnson: Master of the Senate (Shelf-0253)** - Robert A. Caro; legislative power, persuasion, procedure, ambition, and institutional mastery.
21. **The Crisis of the Old Order, 1919-1923 (Shelf-0241)** - Arthur M. Schlesinger Jr.; postwar strain, class politics, institutional exhaustion, and the prehistory of New Deal realignment.
22. **The Coming of the New Deal (Shelf-0242)** - Arthur M. Schlesinger Jr.; state expansion, executive experiment, coalition building, and democratic response to crisis.
23. **The Politics of Upheaval (Shelf-0243)** - Arthur M. Schlesinger Jr.; coalition stress, populist pressure, reform limits, and the turbulence inside realignment.
24. **Animal Farm (Shelf-0177)** - George Orwell; revolutionary betrayal, party myth, propaganda, equality language, and the corruption of liberation.
25. **Nineteen Eighty-Four (Shelf-0171)** - George Orwell; totalitarian language, surveillance, memory control, fear, and politics as reality management.

## Study Arc

**Regime grammar.** Plato, Thucydides, Caesar, and Tacitus establish the first questions: justice, power, faction, empire, civic rhetoric, and republican failure.

**Sovereignty and revolution.** Machiavelli, More, Rousseau, Burke, Smith, Marx, Tocqueville, Churchill, and Popkin make the modern hinge: statecraft, constitutional inheritance, political economy, mass equality, revolution, and English-speaking constitutional memory.

**American political formation.** Paine, Jefferson, Madison, Hamilton, Founding Partisans, The Age of Jackson, and Master of the Senate move from founding argument to party democracy and institutional power.

**Mass state and warning.** The Age of Roosevelt trilogy and Orwell close the path with crisis government, coalition realignment, propaganda, and totalitarian danger.

## V1 Gaps

This is a catalog-bound v1. Aristotle, Hobbes, Locke, Montesquieu, The Federalist Papers, Arendt, Fanon, and modern campaign/media theory are deferred until they exist as catalog rows or a later politics expansion deliberately adds them.

## Academy Hooks

- Pair **Thucydides (Shelf-0003)** with **Democracy in America (Shelf-0262)** when democratic energy becomes imperial or majoritarian.
- Pair **Burke (Shelf-0197)** with **A New World Begins (Shelf-0086)** when revolution claims necessity and opponents claim inherited order.
- Pair **Madison (Shelf-0107)**, **Hamilton (Shelf-0108)**, and **Master of the Senate (Shelf-0253)** when institutional design becomes lived power.
- Pair **Churchill (Shelf-0305)** with **Orwell (Shelf-0171, Shelf-0177)** when political memory, language, and liberty claims need source-critical pressure.
