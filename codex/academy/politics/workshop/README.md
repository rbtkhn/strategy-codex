# Politics Workshop

WORK only; not Record.

The Politics Workshop is the active-use layer of academy politics. The shelf answers "what should I read?" The workshop answers "what political pressure is being formed, organized, persuaded, legitimized, or resisted?"

## Workflow

1. **Read regime.** Start with one Bookshelf anchor, case, election, party, constitution, or public conflict.
2. **Name the form.** Identify regime type, institutional design, party structure, faction map, public mood, and legitimacy claim.
3. **Test persuasion.** Ask what language, symbol, fear, promise, media channel, or patronage network moves people.
4. **Test power.** Ask who can command, fund, obstruct, certify, count, prosecute, mobilize, or veto.
5. **Separate lanes.** Keep primary text, campaign claim, polling, media narrative, legal structure, historian synthesis, and operator inference distinct.
6. **Produce reuse.** End with a regime note, persuasion test, party map, legitimacy warning, or bridge note.

## First Instruments To Build

- **Regime Form Check:** What kind of order is this, and what does it reward?
- **Legitimacy Claim Test:** Why should people obey, and who contests that answer?
- **Party Machine Map:** Who recruits, funds, disciplines, communicates, and turns out?
- **Persuasion Pressure Test:** What words, images, fears, or promises move the public?
- **Institutional Veto Map:** Who can stop action even without winning the argument?

## Boundary

This workshop may generate WORK notes. It does not replace raw-input, polling, legal text, campaign clips, speaker folders, Bookshelf catalog rows, or Record surfaces.
