# Politics Workshop

WORK only; not Record.

The Politics Workshop is the active-use layer of academy politics. The shelf answers "what should I read?" The workshop answers "what political pressure is being formed, organized, persuaded, legitimized, or resisted?"

## Front Door

Use this README as the workshop front door when the real question is not "what happened?" but "what political formation is taking shape here, and what is the right reusable form for it?"

Short governing line:

1. start from one real regime, party, election, constitution, or public conflict
2. identify the form of legitimacy, persuasion, and veto power actually in play
3. stop in the smallest honest politics form that the object can support
4. do not force every live seam into a grand theory, total regime diagnosis, or bridge claim

This workshop is healthiest when it converts raw political noise into reusable political form without pretending every object has already matured into a full doctrine note.

Supporting roles:

- [Regime form](sheets/regime-form.md) is the first reusable worksheet and the best first local continuation
- the academy shelf owns reading order and source accumulation
- the workshop owns active-use political diagnosis, pressure mapping, and reusable note forms

## Layer Contract

- academy politics shelf = what to read and retain
- politics workshop = active-use pressure reading and reusable political forms
- source texts, polling, campaign clips, legal material, and speaker folders = evidence and adjacent inputs, not substitutes for workshop judgment

Short split:

- shelf asks `what should I read?`
- workshop asks `what political pressure is being formed here?`
- workshop output asks `what is the smallest reusable political form this object honestly supports?`

## Workflow

1. **Read regime.** Start with one Bookshelf anchor, case, election, party, constitution, or public conflict.
2. **Name the form.** Identify regime type, institutional design, party structure, faction map, public mood, and legitimacy claim.
3. **Test persuasion.** Ask what language, symbol, fear, promise, media channel, or patronage network moves people.
4. **Test power.** Ask who can command, fund, obstruct, certify, count, prosecute, mobilize, or veto.
5. **Separate lanes.** Keep primary text, campaign claim, polling, media narrative, legal structure, historian synthesis, and operator inference distinct.
6. **Produce reuse.** End with a regime note, persuasion test, party map, legitimacy warning, or bridge note.

## Valid Terminal States

A politics object may validly end its life as:

- regime note
- persuasion test
- party map
- legitimacy warning
- bridge note

Those are not fallback leftovers. They are the normal reusable forms of the workshop.

## False Terminal States

Watch for these false closures:

- `interesting conflict -> full regime theory`
- `good rhetoric read -> complete persuasion doctrine`
- `one election -> total party-system map`
- `one scandal or court fight -> settled constitutional conclusion`
- `strong paragraph -> bridge claim by momentum`

If the object is still mainly descriptive, contested, or thinly sourced, stop earlier in one of the valid workshop forms instead of forcing a heavier synthesis.

## Audit Questions

1. Can a new operator tell what kind of political object belongs here?
2. Can they tell the difference between shelf reading and workshop use?
3. Can they tell what the valid reusable output forms are?
4. Can they tell when a politics object should stop at note, test, map, or warning instead of becoming a larger claim?
5. Can they tell what counts as false completion here?

If several answers are no, the workshop surface is still thin.

## First Instruments To Build

- **Regime Form Check:** What kind of order is this, and what does it reward?
- **Legitimacy Claim Test:** Why should people obey, and who contests that answer?
- **Party Machine Map:** Who recruits, funds, disciplines, communicates, and turns out?
- **Persuasion Pressure Test:** What words, images, fears, or promises move the public?
- **Institutional Veto Map:** Who can stop action even without winning the argument?

## Boundary

This workshop may generate WORK notes. It does not replace raw-input, polling, legal text, campaign clips, speaker folders, Bookshelf catalog rows, or Record surfaces.
