Provide a `civ-emp` source scaffold for the Freeman theme in which legitimacy decline, epistemic decay, and shrinking institutional competence mutate regime form.

## Backward Historical Question

How do legitimacy breakdown, loss of confidence in truth-bearing institutions, and coercive local protection mutate a system from universal order into successor fragmentation?

## Arc Pattern

Large political orders do not fail only through invasion. They can degrade through loss of institutional confidence, narrowing truth standards, and the rise of localized coercive protection as a substitute for universal order.

Short form:

`imperial legitimacy -> epistemic and institutional decay -> personalized protection -> successor fragmentation`

## Continuity / Transformation / Current Carrier / Failure Mode

- Continuity: universal orders rely on law, administration, shared legitimacy, and trusted intellectual standards
- Transformation: once those weaken, political energy shifts toward personal, local, or improvised protection
- Current carrier: executive concentration, elite irrationality, weak public institutions, and coercive replacement structures
- Failure mode: the analogy becomes theatrical declinism rather than a disciplined test of regime-form mutation

## Statecraft Use

Use this scaffold when:

- asking whether a crisis is exposing policy failure or regime-form deterioration
- testing successor-legitimacy and institutional-carrying capacity
- comparing universal-order claims against fragmentation signals

## Counterweight

Roman-collapse rhetoric can overdramatize ordinary decline and flatten very different successor orders into one doom script.

Counterweight rule:

`use collapse analogies to test regime mutation, not to declare inevitable apocalypse`

## Transaction Hooks

- ask what institution still carries legitimacy
- ask whether truth-bearing institutions are being displaced by personal authority
- ask whether the draft assumes universal order where only fragmented protection now exists
