
# Monroe Doctrine And Hemispheric Prerogative

## Purpose

Provide a `civ-emp` source scaffold for the Freeman theme in which republics or empires justify privileged spheres and degrade legitimacy by claiming asymmetrical sovereignty.

## Backward Historical Question

How do republics or empires justify privileged zones, and how does the resulting asymmetry degrade the legitimacy of the order they claim to defend?

## Arc Pattern

Sphere doctrines begin as security or anti-intervention claims and often harden into asymmetrical privileges: the center claims exceptional rights while denying equal standing to nearby polities.

Short form:

`security doctrine -> privileged sphere -> asymmetrical sovereignty -> legitimacy decay -> resistance or drift`

## Continuity / Transformation / Current Carrier / Failure Mode

- Continuity: major powers repeatedly seek nearby security zones or civilizational buffers
- Transformation: a doctrine of protection becomes a doctrine of prerogative when reciprocity disappears
- Current carrier: executive doctrine, naval reach, sanctions, covert or overt pressure, and rhetorical ownership of a region
- Failure mode: the doctrine overreaches, drives balancing or defection, and reveals that order claims were really exceptional privilege

## Statecraft Use

Use this scaffold when:

- testing hemisphere or sphere-of-influence claims
- diagnosing lawful reach versus emergency empire
- asking when protective language launders hierarchy

## Counterweight

Not every local-security doctrine is automatically illegitimate. The issue is whether the doctrine remains reciprocal, bounded, and politically ownable by others in the region.

Counterweight rule:

`security perimeter is not the same thing as blank-check hemispheric prerogative`

## Transaction Hooks

- ask whether the draft recognizes equal sovereignty for secondary states
- ask what would prove the doctrine has become extraction or coercive privilege
- ask whether the claimed protected order is producing drift away from the center
