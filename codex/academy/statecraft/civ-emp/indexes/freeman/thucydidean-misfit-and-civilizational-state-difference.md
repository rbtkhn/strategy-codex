## Arc Pattern

Strategic models often inherit the history that produced them. A model built from rival small or medium powers in one civilizational field may misfire when applied to a different state form whose continuity, scale, and legitimacy grammar were produced differently.

Short form:

`historical model -> exported analogy -> civilizational mismatch test -> revised reading or overreach`

## Continuity / Transformation / Current Carrier / Failure Mode

- Continuity: strategic theory often carries deep assumptions about state form, legitimacy, and political fragmentation
- Transformation: once exported beyond its original historical setting, the model may still illuminate one actor's fear behavior while failing to describe the other actor's civilizational logic
- Current carrier: analysts, strategic schools, and policy elites who rely on imported great-power templates
- Failure mode: European power-history is universalized, China is flattened into a familiar rival type, and policy overreacts to a bad analogy

## Statecraft Use

Use this scaffold when:

- testing imported strategic models
- distinguishing incumbent-hegemon anxiety from rising-power intention
- comparing civilizational-state continuity against multi-polity balance traditions

## Counterweight

Rejecting one analogy does not mean no analogy is possible. The risk is replacing a bad model with civilizational exceptionalism that explains everything and disciplines nothing.

Counterweight rule:

`test model fit without turning cultural difference into immunity from comparison`

## Transaction Hooks

- ask whether the current draft assumes one civilizational form where several exist
- ask whether the model explains both actors or only the incumbent's reaction
- ask what historical substrate produced the theory being imported
