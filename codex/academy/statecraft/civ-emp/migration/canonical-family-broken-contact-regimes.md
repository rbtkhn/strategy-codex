Reviewer / date: Codex / 2026-05-22

## Family Claim

Escalation risk jumps when contact procedure decays faster than coercive habits do.

Across all four lanes, the shared danger is not simply that force remains available. It is that states keep relying on older habits of restraint, signaling, or deconfliction after the channels that once made those habits governable have thinned or broken.

The sequence is usually:

- a rivalry keeps its familiar instruments
- actors assume they still understand the ladder
- proxy or indirect pressure feels politically cheaper than direct settlement
- but the review channels, inspection habits, hotline logic, or reciprocal formulas that once kept the ladder legible are weaker than the operators admit

At that point, the proxy script becomes misleading. The crisis still looks indirect, but the inherited contact architecture is too degraded to keep indirect pressure bounded.

## Shared Structural Rule

The four lanes converge on one deeper rule:

- rivalry becomes more dangerous when actors inherit the weapons, proxies, and signaling habits of an older order without inheriting the contact procedure that once made those tools legible

This is why the family belongs between `war` and `empire-instrument`.

- `war` names the coercive grammar, escalation ceilings, and enoughness problem
- `empire-instrument` names the outward carrier: alliances, buffer demands, route security, partner networks, maritime leverage, or sanctions architecture

The family lesson is:

- contact is not cleanup
- contact is part of the coercive design itself

When contact is treated as a later diplomatic afterthought, the system quietly starts borrowing risk from a procedural world that no longer exists.

## Distinct Lane Failure Forms

The shared rule travels, but it fails differently in each lane.

1. **America: proxy-war availability outlives contact discipline**

   America is most vulnerable when executive speed, allied choreography, and proxy-war convenience keep the coercive instrument moving after the republic's contact discipline has thinned. Deconfliction, review, and termination channels then look secondary compared with maintaining pressure. The danger is not only battlefield escalation. It is a constitutional system still acting as if it can manage indirect war through inherited Cold War habits after the political ownership and direct contact habits have degraded.

2. **Russia: deterrent habits outlive reciprocal procedure**

   Russia is most vulnerable when it still speaks the language of warning time, parity, and deterrence after the reciprocal inspection and deployment procedures that once stabilized those concepts have eroded. Security claims then become easier to escalate than to verify. A proxy or borderland script starts carrying more danger than admitted because each side is still interpreting moves through a deterrent grammar that no longer has thick enough procedural ballast.

3. **China: quiet signaling outlives quiet coordination**

   China is most vulnerable when it still expects crisis management to stay administratively quiet after ports, insurers, regional navies, and ministries have been forced into public escalatory classification. Incident channels, commercial reassurance, and tacit understandings may still nominally exist, but once outside actors must prudently rename the event for themselves, the anti-disorder script is already thinner than Beijing's preferred vocabulary assumes.

4. **Iran: deterrent leverage outlives believable pause channels**

   Iran is most vulnerable when deterrent signaling, partner networks, and latency bargaining remain active after the backchannels, verification inheritance, or de-escalatory trust needed to narrow them have weakened. At that point, restraint becomes harder to narrate internally because the channels that once made bounded pause look serious have lost credibility. Proxy action becomes more dangerous not only because it can spread, but because the stop signal no longer carries.

## Main Failure Modes

Across the set, this family most often breaks through five recurring mechanisms:

1. **Procedural overhang**
   States assume that because the old ladder is still familiar, the old control channels still work.

2. **Proxy comfort**
   Indirect pressure feels cheaper than direct confrontation, so actors underprice how much degraded contact has raised the real escalation risk.

3. **Classification divergence**
   The initiator and the outside system stop using the same category for the same act, so deconfliction and review begin from incompatible descriptions.

4. **Reciprocity decay**
   One side still invokes stability, inspection, or caution language after the reciprocal practice that gave those words meaning has thinned.

5. **Stop-signal disbelief**
   The coercive system still knows how to start or intensify, but no longer knows how to make a bounded pause look credible.

These are all versions of the same break:

- the instruments remain
- the contact world that once governed them does not

## Strongest Shared Falsifiers

Across all four lanes, broken contact is probably making escalation materially worse when one or more of these conditions hold:

1. operators still rely on indirect or proxy pressure while treating contact restoration as optional
2. the coercive act has more clearly defined entry tools than review tools
3. outside carriers such as allies, insurers, ports, mediators, or partner networks must improvise their own risk classification because the formal channels are too thin
4. later carriers cannot explain what concrete procedural event would count as successful stabilization
5. the system still talks as if inherited deterrent or arms-control habits are active, but no one can name the current reciprocal mechanism that keeps them active

These falsifiers travel well because they all indicate the same thing:

- the crisis is being managed with the vocabulary of a thicker contact regime than the one actually available

## Drafting Implications

For the helix-first corpus, this family gives a reusable drafting test:

1. What contact or verification procedure is the clause assuming still exists?
2. Is that procedure actually present now, or only inherited rhetorically from an earlier order?
3. Which outside carriers would be forced to improvise if the contact regime fails under stress?
4. What concrete review, hotline, inspection, incident, or mediator event proves the coercive ladder is still governable?
5. What bounded pause or reciprocal reset would still look credible inside the lane's own legitimacy grammar?

If a draft cannot answer those five questions, it may still sound prudent about escalation, but it has not yet solved the stronger statecraft problem of contact decay.
