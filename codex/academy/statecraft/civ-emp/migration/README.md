# CIV-EMP Migration Workspace

This workspace governs the migration from legacy `civ-mem` dependency into the active `civ-emp` source base. It exists because the statecraft lanes already cite large upstream `civilization_memory` files directly, while the CIV-EMP contract requires shorter two-volume source objects that can support drafting without reopening the full legacy corpus every time.

The migration rule here is symmetric first. Phase one does not allow `Civilization` to fill up while `Empire` trails behind as a future clean-up promise. The first wave deliberately materializes both sides together across the four active lanes: `America`, `Russia`, `China`, and `Iran`. Every phase-one slice must be legible as a pair, even when the `Empire` side is shorter or presently less developed.

## Two-Volume Rule

Phase one uses this fixed split:

- `Civilization`: `state-memory`, `geo`, `war`, `peace`
- `Empire`: `empire-instrument`
- `Hinge`: arc pattern, helix-facing degradation/restoration logic, counterweight, and transaction hooks

The hinge layer is not a separate volume. It is the operating discipline that keeps the two volumes from drifting into isolated shelf systems.

## Canonical Family Translation

Each canonical question family must appear on both sides of the phase-one corpus.

1. `what makes a settlement real rather than theatrical`
   Civilization: legitimacy, memory, identity, recognition, continuity
   Empire: guarantor structure, enforcement carrier, instrument durability
   Hinge: settlement design must pair civilizational recognition with a governable enforcement carrier

2. `when does a pressured hegemon misread its own power, limits, or durability`
   Civilization: self-image, legitimacy narrative, inherited mission
   Empire: overextension, amplification trap, decaying instrument set
   Hinge: restoration requires trimming instrument reach before civilizational self-image turns brittle

3. `when do older strategic memories continue to constrain present actors`
   Civilization: living memory, sacred grammar, civilizational continuity
   Empire: how those memories alter deterrence, corridors, alliance, and coercive behavior
   Hinge: active policy must read memory as a live strategic carrier rather than as inert heritage

4. `when does coercion fail to convert into the political outcome it claims to serve`
   Civilization: what political legitimacy or social acceptance coercion cannot generate
   Empire: instrument failure, escalation without settlement, amplification without control
   Hinge: statecraft must reattach force to a bounded political end before entropy outruns control

5. `when do broken contact regimes and arms-control inheritances make escalation more dangerous than the proxy-war script admits`
   Civilization: memory of danger, successor fear, procedural caution
   Empire: command channels, deterrence architecture, escalation thresholds
   Hinge: restoration means rebuilding contact procedure before rivalry management collapses

6. `how does remembered exclusion shape the politics of direct great-power settlement`
   Civilization: status memory, humiliation, exclusion anxiety
   Empire: room geometry, bargaining architecture, outer-power settlement mechanics
   Hinge: durable bargaining must answer symbolic inclusion and actual room design together

## Waves

`Wave 1`: inventory, ledger, manifest, pair-map  
`Wave 2`: materialize source objects in paired slices  
`Wave 3`: cut over lane-local files so they prefer `civ-emp`  
`Wave 4`: verify symmetry, completeness, and provenance discipline

## Acceptance Rule

A first-wave slice is only `verified` when:

- both volume sides needed for that slice exist
- lane-local files prefer `civ-emp`
- at least one canonical family is served on both sides
- counterweight exists
- transaction hook exists
- provenance to upstream `civ-mem` is preserved
- the slice's symmetry partner is at least `cut_over`

## Pilot-Lane Audit Gate

Before any lane is treated as the reusable scaling template, it must pass a six-audit gate:

1. `civ-emp` object contract audit
2. cutover honesty audit
3. budget / generator audit
4. provenance membrane audit
5. scaffold-answer audit
6. public orientation and attractiveness audit

The first five audits test internal correctness. The sixth tests whether the pair can orient, invite, and guide a serious outsider in a PH-CIV-like way rather than reading as migration residue or insider shorthand.

Pass thresholds:

- Hard pass: no audit category below `4`, and the pair-level public orientation average is at least `4.2`
- Conditional pass: one or two categories may score `3` when paired with explicit revision notes and no pair-level category falls below `4`
- Fail: any category scores `1-2`, or the pair remains internally correct but publicly inert, jargon-heavy, or unguided

Lane promotion rule:

- no lane becomes the reusable template until both pilot objects pass the first five audits and the pair passes the public orientation and attractiveness audit

## Operational Boundary

This workspace is a control plane, not the migration itself. It names what must be built, in what order, and under what acceptance rule. It does not justify mirroring the legacy corpus file-for-file, and it does not authorize direct `civ-mem` use to remain the default once a corresponding `civ-emp` slice exists.
