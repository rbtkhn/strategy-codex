## Realism / Incentive Filter

- Does the alleged actor benefit more than it loses? America loses strategic freedom if civilian-harm denial makes talks impossible; it benefits from a non-admission recognition mechanism that restores bargaining room.
- Is the target something the actor still depends on? America depends on a credible Iranian counterpart, Gulf transit stability, and domestic legal survivability.
- Accident / negligence / private actor / third-party alternative: preserve legal investigation rather than demanding instant confession.
- Evidence needed: targeting authority, strike logs, intelligence products, casualty records, school location data, and command review.
- Reclassified premise if needed: even absent final culpability, Minab functions as a civilian-harm legitimacy constraint.

## Historical Continuity

- Current government: federal executive, State Department, Defense Department, Treasury, Congress, and intelligence community.
- Current state: America as a continental power trying to bound overseas coercion while preserving credibility.
- Predecessor institutions: war powers, military law, treaty-making, humanitarian law, and executive sanctions practice.
- Geography: Gulf transit and southern Iran sit outside America's natural defensive perimeter, increasing overextension risk.
- Possible successor authority: if federal rupture deepens, only successor-stable humanitarian and channel rules remain credible.
- What survives regime or constitutional change: the American interest in lawful command, maritime stability, and avoiding endless wars of choice.

## Head-Of-State Carrier Check

- Required authority carrier: President, Secretary of State, Secretary of Defense, Treasury, congressional leadership, and mediator liaison.
- What this carrier can sign credibly: protected channel, humanitarian review, no-regime-change statement, sanctions waiver, and military deconfliction instruction.
- What this carrier cannot concede without breaking legitimacy: immediate total liability without process, unlimited reparations, or abandonment of all allied security claims.
- Succession / continuity risk: unilateral executive assurances are brittle unless supported by congressional notification and mediator verification.

## Pattern / Narrative Conversion

- Civilizational pattern or narrative: lawful republic versus imperial impunity.
- Authority: named U.S. offices authorize protected channels and review.
- Restraint: no targeting of channel carriers; no regime-change or partition signal; no public humiliation of Iranian authority.
- Settlement: civilian-harm review, channel protection, first-step blockade / sanctions relief, and non-repetition language.

## State Interest

America's durable interest is to prevent Minab from becoming permanent proof that Washington only bargains through murder, while preserving enough lawful authority to end the crisis and restore transit stability.

## Leverage / Constraint

- Leverage being spent: sanctions relief, blockade relief, public no-regime-change language, protected channels, humanitarian review, and allied restraint.
- Constraint that limits the leverage: legal exposure, allied spoiler pressure, domestic hawk backlash, and the need not to make civilian-harm review an automatic liability confession.
- Volatility note: Pape's Iran leverage score and Mearsheimer's overextension warnings both point against further coercion; the America lane should spend restraint before Iran's leverage hardens.

## Richelieu Validation

- Institutional carrier: White House, State, Defense, Treasury, Congress, mediator secretariat, and military legal review.
- Capacity: sanctions waivers, command directives, channel protection, review mechanism, and allied deconfliction.
- Continuity risk: social-media volatility, executive turnover, and donor / allied pressure may wreck credibility.
- Ideology or faction pressure: regime-change advocates will call recognition weakness; legal hawks may fear admission.
- Long-pressure burden: America must maintain the channel long enough for Iran to believe it is not a tactical pause before renewed attack.

## Bismarck Validation

- Limited aim: restore bargaining room and transit stability; do not solve every U.S.-Iran grievance in the first clause.
- Actor to isolate: decapitation advocates, civilian-harm denial maximalists, and allied spoilers.
- Actor to reassure, restrain, or keep neutral: Iran, Oman, Gulf states, Congress, China, Russia, and energy-market actors.
- Alliance geometry: reassure Gulf states through transit stabilization while restraining actions that make them targets.
- Restraint after advantage: if Iran accepts humanitarian review and protected channels, do not convert the opening into humiliation theater.
- Off-ramp: non-admission civilian-harm review plus no-regime-change statement plus reversible sanctions / blockade relief.

## Instrument Text

Draft clause:

> The United States shall affirm that this framework is not a regime-change or partition instrument and shall support a mediator-administered civilian-harm review mechanism for Minab-class incidents. Participation shall not constitute admission of liability or waiver of claims by either side. While the review and talks are active, the United States shall protect named diplomatic channels, refrain from public humiliation of Iranian authority carriers, and pair any Iranian transit or military restraint with reversible sanctions or blockade relief.
