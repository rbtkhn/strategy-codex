# Russia Empire

WORK only; not Record.

Purpose: name Russia's outward striving for security and control so statecraft can distinguish necessary depth from overbinding expansion.

## Desired Control

Russia seeks control over security depth, escalation buffers, energy routes, military basing conditions, borderland neutrality, recognition formulas, and multipolar institutions that reduce Western coercive reach.

## Instruments

- Military force, nuclear deterrence, air defense, and defense-industrial production.
- Energy supply, transit, pricing, and infrastructure.
- Security guarantees, recognition formulas, and mediator channels.
- Eurasian institutions, sanctions workarounds, and diplomatic linkage.
- Ambiguity that preserves optionality.

## Adaptive Expression

Russian empire is adaptive when it secures depth, isolates hostile infrastructure, preserves optionality, and avoids making Moscow hostage to an ally's escalation calendar.

## Pathological Overreach

Russian empire becomes pathological when disruption becomes open-ended commitment, when recognition claims unify adversaries, or when partners can set Russia's timing and costs without reciprocal control.

## Limiting Principle

Russia's control must preserve strategic optionality. A commitment that removes Moscow's ability to regulate escalation weakens the security it was meant to protect.

## Drafting Prompts

- What leverage weakens U.S. primacy without trapping Russia?
- Which ambiguity preserves options rather than postponing a reckoning?
- What recognition formula increases security without maximal demand?
- Where should Russia restrain an ally to preserve its own long game?
