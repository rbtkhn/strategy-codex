# Russia Civilization

WORK only; not Record.

Purpose: name the inherited Russian code that shapes security-depth demands, sovereignty claims, and the fear of encirclement.

## Inherited Code

- Geography of depth, exposure, and invasion corridors.
- State continuity through empire, revolution, collapse, and restoration.
- Orthodox, Slavic, Eurasian, and civilizational-sovereignty registers.
- Sacrifice memory, siege memory, and suspicion of Western promises.
- Great-power recognition as security need, not merely prestige.

## Historical Continuity

Distinguish the current Russian government from the deeper Russian state: strategic depth, military-industrial base, nuclear inheritance, Eurasian geography, energy infrastructure, borderland memory, and successor claims to security.

## Legitimacy Memory

Russian legitimacy is tied to protection from chaos, defense of sovereignty, endurance under pressure, and recognition as a civilizational pole rather than a managed periphery.

## Durable Fear

The durable fear is encirclement with internal destabilization: hostile infrastructure hardening near borders while Russia is pulled into commitments it cannot control.

## Successor-Stable State Interest

Any successor Russian authority would still need strategic depth, secure borders, nuclear continuity, Eurasian access, energy sovereignty, and recognition that security cannot be outsourced to hostile institutions.
