# Statecraft Method

WORK only; not Record.

Academy-statecraft turns political judgment into draftable instruments. It is not a biography shelf for famous ministers. Richelieu and Bismarck are used as pressure tests for treaties, policy papers, negotiation briefs, sanctions designs, alliance architecture, and institutional memoranda.

## Drafting Loop

1. **Name the state interest.** State the concrete interest in one sentence without moral theater or bureaucratic fog.
2. **Identify the institutional carrier.** Specify which offices, agencies, coalition bodies, courts, forces, secretariats, or treaty organs can carry the instrument after the headline moment passes.
3. **Map the balance.** Name the parties, dependencies, veto points, outside patrons, escalation ladders, and likely spoilers.
4. **Limit the aim.** Define what the instrument does not try to solve, so it can succeed at the thing it is actually built to do.
5. **Draft the mechanism.** Convert the judgment into obligations, procedures, thresholds, review cadence, enforcement hooks, and exit/off-ramp language.
6. **Stress-test incentives.** Ask what each party gains by compliance, what it gains by cheating, and how violations become observable.
7. **Preserve agency and legitimacy.** Keep necessity from becoming excuse; name who commands, who implements, who can refuse, and who bears responsibility.
8. **Produce reuse.** End with a treaty clause, policy recommendation, negotiating instruction, warning, or amendment that can be reused.

## Richelieu Check

Use [Richelieu](lenses/richelieu.md) when a draft risks becoming morally expressive but institutionally weak.

- What state interest persists after this administration, coalition, or news cycle?
- Which institution can carry the policy when personalities leave?
- What ideological alignment is tempting but strategically irrelevant?
- What administrative, fiscal, legal, or intelligence capacity is required?
- What long-war or long-pressure burden does the draft quietly create?

## Bismarck Check

Use [Bismarck](lenses/bismarck.md) when a draft risks becoming strategically expansive, emotionally satisfying, or victory-drunk.

- What is the limited objective?
- Which adversary is isolated, and which one must not be accidentally unified with it?
- What alliance sequence keeps options open?
- What settlement preserves equilibrium after advantage?
- What off-ramp prevents a tactical win from becoming a strategic trap?

## Combined Equilibrium Test

A statecraft draft is stronger when it can answer both lenses at once:

- **Richelieu:** What durable interest and institution carry this?
- **Bismarck:** What equilibrium and restraint keep it from overreaching?

If the draft has interest without equilibrium, it may become coercive drift. If it has equilibrium without institutional carrier, it may become diplomatic theater.

## Comparative Perspective Test

Use [National Perspective Orthogonality](sheets/national-perspective-orthogonality.md) when a problem needs several national points of view. Do not make other states passive objects of analysis. Give each actor its own durable interest, institutional carrier, fear, leverage, preferred instrument, and off-ramp.

When the analysis should become a draft, open the matching national lane: [America](america/), [Russia](russia/), [China](china/), or [Iran](iran/). Use the lane to join contemporary speaker-state inputs with Richelieu / Bismarck checks and produce an instrument rather than a commentary paragraph.

For the Iran / India / fourth-center problem, the default questions are:

- **United States:** How do we bound Iran's coercive leverage?
- **Russia:** How do we exploit Iran's leverage without entrapment?
- **China:** How do we preserve Iran's leverage without energy disorder?
- **Iran:** How do we monetize and legitimate the leverage without losing control?

## Singularity Bridge

Use [Sovereignty Under Acceleration](../singularity/workshop/sheets/sovereignty-under-acceleration.md) when the statecraft problem is triggered by AI infrastructure power: compute, models, agents, memory, identity, permissions, data centers, audit, rollback, or automated decision systems. The bridge keeps human agency central while converting acceleration signals into treaty, policy, and authority artifacts.

## Output Standard

Each session should produce one compact WORK artifact:

- a treaty clause or annex;
- a policy-paper thesis and recommendation set;
- a negotiation brief;
- a sanctions or relief mechanism;
- an alliance architecture note;
- a warning against overreach;
- or a cross-shelf bridge to military, politics, theology, or strategy work.

## Boundary

This method can use raw-input, speaker lanes, historical analogies, and existing strategy drafts as source material. It does not replace provenance, speaker objects, legal review, policy review, or Record surfaces.
