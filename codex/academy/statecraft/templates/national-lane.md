# National Lane Template

WORK only; not Record.

Use this template when seeding a national drafting lane inside academy-statecraft. The lane is not a country encyclopedia. It is a compact bench for turning contemporary analysis and historical statecraft discipline into draftable instruments.

## Purpose

Name what this national lane is for in one paragraph.

## Core Question

What statecraft problem does this actor need to solve?

## State Interest

What durable interest persists after the current leader, faction, coalition, or news cycle?

## Historical Continuity

What links the current government, current state, predecessor institutions, geography, civilizational memory, and possible successor authority? What survives if the regime or constitutional form changes?

## Fear

What outcome does this actor most need to prevent?

## Leverage

What can this actor deny, enable, disrupt, recognize, finance, mediate, or legitimate?

## Constraints

What resource, geography, alliance, legitimacy, administrative, domestic, or military constraint limits action?

## Internal Pressure / Face-Saving Test

What domestic audience punishes compromise? What institution benefits from escalation? What word, legal classification, or visible procedure would humiliate the state? What face-saving phrase permits restraint? What settlement form lets the state preserve authority at home while limiting action abroad?

## Historical Lens

Use Richelieu for durable interest and institutional carrier. Use Bismarck for limited aims, alliance geometry, optionality, and restraint after advantage.

## Speaker-State Intake

Open contemporary speaker-state surfaces for mechanisms, clocks, warnings, and settlement logic. Do not import an entire speaker worldview; extract the useful statecraft mechanism and preserve source boundaries.

Suggested intake order:

1. Forecast clocks and coercive leverage.
2. Force constraints and escalation warnings.
3. Diplomacy, settlement architecture, and off-ramps.
4. Structural misreading, order rupture, and alliance psychology.

## Preferred Instruments

List the instruments this actor is likely to favor: treaty clause, side letter, sanctions sequence, military deconfliction channel, transit guarantee, coalition forum, recognition formula, energy compact, or domestic mandate.

## Red Lines

What demand, concession, or ambiguity would likely break the instrument?

## Off-Ramps

What lets this actor de-escalate without humiliation or loss of core interest?

## Mirror Test

After drafting from this actor's view, write the shortest plausible objection from the other national lanes.

## India Shadow Comparator

When the problem involves Iran, energy, or fourth-power theory, keep India as a comparison object: India may be a stronger comprehensive-power candidate, while Iran may be a more immediate coercive or chokepoint center.

## Drafting Prompts

- What one clause would this actor insist on?
- What one clause would this actor quietly need but prefer not to say publicly?
- What would this actor accept if framed as equilibrium rather than concession?
- Which institutional carrier can keep the deal alive after the headline moment passes?
