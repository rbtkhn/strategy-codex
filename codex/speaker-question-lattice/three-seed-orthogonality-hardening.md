
# Three-Seed Orthogonality Hardening

Purpose: normalize the Freeman, Crooke, and Mercouris seed themes into a clearer cross-seed family map, separating stable canonical families from single-seed-only and subordinate families.

## Method

This note does not rescore the three shelves. It asks a narrower question:

- which families are now strong enough to be treated as canonical cross-seed lattice families
- which are still only speaker-local strengths
- which should remain subordinate unless a fourth seed elevates them

Decision rule:

- `keep` means a stable canonical lattice family
- `single-seed` means strong but not yet cross-seed-validated
- `subordinate` means useful but should live beneath another family for now

## Current Three-Seed Verdict

The three-seed lattice is strong enough to support six top-level canonical families:

1. what makes a settlement real rather than theatrical
2. when does a pressured hegemon misread its own power, limits, or durability
3. when do older strategic memories continue to constrain present actors
4. when does coercion fail to convert into the political outcome it claims to serve
5. when do broken contact regimes and arms-control inheritances make escalation more dangerous than the proxy-war script admits
6. how does remembered exclusion shape the politics of direct great-power settlement

The important shift here is that families `5` and `6` should no longer be treated as mere detail beneath broader overlap buckets. Mercouris has supplied enough pressure to make both of them feel structurally independent.

The main remaining uncertainty is not the existence of those six families. It is whether some strong single-seed families will later earn top-level promotion:

- redemptive permanent war versus bounded politics
- civilizational-state analogy misfit
- domestic legitimacy collapse and regime mutation
- prestige reversal and unipolar exhaustion
- post-hegemonic regional architecture

## Recommended Next Action

Do not edit the three speaker shelves yet. Instead:

1. treat this note as the current normalization layer
2. use these six canonical families as the live top-level lattice
3. test the single-seed families against the fourth major seed speaker
4. only then decide which of the single-seed families deserve promotion into the canonical set
