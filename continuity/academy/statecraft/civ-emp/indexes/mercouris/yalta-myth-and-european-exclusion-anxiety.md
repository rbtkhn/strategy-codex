
# Yalta Myth And European Exclusion Anxiety
<!-- word_count: 326 -->

## Purpose

Provide a `civ-emp` source scaffold for the Mercouris theme in which conference memory, myth, and exclusion anxiety shape how secondary powers react to direct great-power bargaining.

## Cybernetic Activation

Mercouris-side activation surface: [Yalta myth and European exclusion anxiety](../../../../../../statecraft/voices/mercouris/yalta-myth-and-european-exclusion-anxiety.md)

## Backward Historical Question

How do remembered conferences, whether accurately or mythically remembered, shape later fears about who gets a seat in settlement design?

## Source Basis

- Mercouris modern examples: Yalta memory, Munich warning memory
- `civ-emp` retrieval contract for `peace`
- lane-local conference or settlement-memory objects where they exist
- legacy `civ-mem` only if a specific conference lineage needs deeper provenance

## Arc Pattern

`great-power conference -> remembered inclusion/exclusion script -> later diplomatic anxiety and posture`

## Continuity / Transformation / Current Carrier / Failure Mode

- Continuity: diplomatic memory outlives literal conference records
- Transformation: conference myth can become more politically active than the archive itself
- Current carrier: elite memory, media shorthand, alliance fear, venue symbolism
- Failure mode: mythology overwhelms the actual design space or becomes an excuse for theatrical resistance

## Statecraft Use

Use this scaffold when drafting or testing:

- participation design
- who must be inside the room
- venue choice and symbolic baggage
- the political costs of direct great-power settlement without secondary actors

## Counterweight

Exclusion anxiety is real, but it does not by itself prove that every excluded actor has enough leverage to shape the settlement outcome.

## Transaction Hooks

- ask whether a venue carries accumulated grievance
- ask whether a side fears the deal itself or the fact of being excluded from making it
- ask whether conference mythology is obscuring the actual negotiable content
