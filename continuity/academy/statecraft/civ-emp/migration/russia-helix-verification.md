## Helix-Primary Verification Standard

For the Russia transfer case, helix-primary verification requires all of the following:

1. the helix is now the preferred top-level interpretive surface for the lane
2. the lane README routes to the helix first and to strand objects second
3. the helix routes clearly to all five first-wave strand objects
4. all five first-wave strand objects route back to the helix
5. canonical-family service is legible through helix-plus-strand interaction
6. the lane no longer depends on the two-volume split as its governing interpretation, even though the strand split remains valid as scaffold history
7. the strand layer still preserves provenance, counterweight, and transaction hooks
8. the control plane reflects Russia as a transfer case from the America proof rather than as an unbounded all-lanes rewrite

## Verdict

- Helix-primary lane: `yes`
- Ready to transfer pattern beyond Russia: `not yet`

Russia now works as a **helix-primary lane**.

That claim is justified because the lane no longer needs the two-volume split to provide its top-level interpretation. The split still exists as a supporting strand scaffold, but a reader can now enter at the helix, understand the continuity / exposure / leverage problem as one integrated object, and then descend into the correct strand when the question narrows.

At the same time, this transfer remains bounded. Russia is the second successful helix-primary lane because the America proving case held strongly enough to transfer, not because Phase 2 has silently become the default for every lane. China and Iran still remain on the Phase 1C architecture until explicitly promoted.

## Recommended Interpretation

Use Russia to establish that Phase 2 is transferable:

- **America** proves the architecture can work
- **Russia** proves the architecture is not America-specific

That distinction keeps the repo from confusing one successful proving case with a generally transferred layer while still showing that the helix-first model can travel across very different civilizational material.
