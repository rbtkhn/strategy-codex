# Great-Power Energy Absorber Threshold
<!-- word_count: 760 -->


Use this sheet when a current-event read shows great-power alignment around energy continuity, but the event has not yet become a transaction object. The test protects the router from overproducing new transactions when a summit, statement, or purchase signal is still only a near fit.

Core rule:

> Alignment signal is not yet a transaction object. Mechanism makes transaction.

## Working Definition

A great-power energy absorber is a state that stabilizes another sanctioned or pressured supplier by buying its energy, financing or insuring the trade, rerouting flows, absorbing sanctions risk, and converting that role into diplomatic leverage.

For China-Russia, the transaction becomes real when the contested object is no longer "China and Russia are aligned," but:

> Who may absorb sanctioned energy flows, through what finance, shipping, or insurance architecture, without inheriting supplier escalation or sanctions liability?

## Exact Trigger

Recommend a new transaction candidate only if at least **three of six** appear:

1. **Binding energy instrument:** long-term oil/gas, pipeline, LNG, refinery, storage, or power-sector agreement that materially redirects Russian supply toward China.
2. **Payments architecture:** yuan/ruble settlement expansion, clearing facility, bank workaround, commodity exchange mechanism, or digital payment rail designed to sustain Russian energy trade under sanctions.
3. **Shipping or insurance mechanism:** Chinese, Russian, or joint shipping insurance, tanker registry, maritime guarantee, logistics pool, or risk-sharing structure meant to keep flows moving despite sanctions or war-risk premiums.
4. **Sanctions-boundary dispute:** U.S. / EU response explicitly treats China's energy absorption as sanctions evasion, hostile bloc formation, or material support for Russian escalation.
5. **Third-theater linkage:** arrangement is tied to Hormuz, Suez, Arctic routes, Iran, Central Asia, or another corridor where energy continuity becomes a bargaining object.
6. **Diplomatic ownership language:** China publicly frames the role as global energy-order stabilization, sanctions resistance, multipolar governance, or protection of developing-country supply security.

Revisit if post-event reporting shows concrete instruments in at least two categories and one of them is payments architecture, shipping / insurance, or sanctions-boundary dispute.

## Candidate Shape

- **Candidate name:** Great-Power Energy Absorber Framework
- **Crisis object:** great-power energy absorption under sanctions and chokepoint stress.
- **Primary lanes:** China, Russia, America, Iran.
- **Settlement spine:** Energy continuity without sanctions impunity; absorption without escalation ownership.
- **Entropy signal:** The stabilizer role mutates into a counter-system energy bloc. China begins as absorber of disorder, then becomes responsible for the disorder its partners create.
- **Recursive utility:** Trains the router to distinguish market stabilization from adversarial energy architecture.

## Falsifiers

Do not recommend a new transaction if:

- summit language remains rhetorical;
- energy trade remains normal bilateral commerce;
- China avoids payments, shipping, or insurance exposure;
- Washington does not contest the absorption mechanism;
- no third-theater corridor or sanctions-boundary issue appears.

## Objection Matrix

Near-fit reading: the Putin-Xi energy signal remains under existing Hormuz / Suez-style transaction logic until concrete energy, payments, insurance, shipping, sanctions, pipeline, or transit instruments appear.

| Actor | Likely objection | Real concern | Patch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| China | "This is normal sovereign cooperation, not an energy-absorber role." | Beijing does not want commercial energy ties converted into liability for Russian escalation. | Use bounded-stabilizer language: China may stabilize energy flows without becoming guarantor of supplier conduct. |
| Russia | "Dependency on China is Western framing; this is multipolar partnership." | Moscow needs the summit read as strategic depth, not proof of buyer dependence. | Separate diplomatic depth from price, route, and finance dependency; judge by whether Russia gains optionality or only absorption. |
| United States | "Near-fit underplays an emerging hostile bloc." | Washington fears China-Russia energy coordination will defeat sanctions and strengthen Iran-style chokepoint leverage. | Track mechanisms, not rhetoric: sanction finance, shipping, and military-support instruments, but do not treat all energy trade as bloc proof. |
| Iran | "This framing makes Iran secondary to China-Russia energy order." | Tehran wants its own leverage recognized, especially Hormuz, sanctions relief, and regional legitimacy. | Keep Iran as a lane when chokepoint, sanctions, or Gulf transit issues enter; do not let China absorb Iran's bargaining identity. |
| Energy markets / insurers | "Political analysis misses the actual risk premium." | Markets care about contracts, routing, insurance, settlement, and disruption probability, not summit symbolism. | Require observable instruments: premiums, coverage exclusions, tanker routing, pipeline terms, settlement rails, or official guarantees. |

## Best Patch

Use a mechanism threshold:

> Rhetoric is alignment signal. Instruments make transaction.

Until instruments appear, keep the event as a near fit. If instruments appear, route it toward the Great-Power Energy Absorber Framework candidate.
