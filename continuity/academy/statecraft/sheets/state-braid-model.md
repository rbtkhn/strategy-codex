# State Braid Model
<!-- word_count: 1325 -->

## Purpose

The braid is a statecraft drafting aid for linked crisis objects that cannot be handled through one arc alone. Some problems become draftable only when several strategic lines are kept together long enough to design authority, restraint, and settlement rather than treating each line as an isolated topic.

Canonical hinge: `pattern / narrative -> authority / restraint / settlement`.

## Definitions

- **Arc**: a durable strategic line that carries a recurring problem through time.
- **Arc-lens**: the reading frame that makes the arc legible in the present.
- **Braid**: a linked bundle of arcs that must be handled together if authority, restraint, and settlement are to work as one instrument rather than as disconnected reactions.
- **Helix**: a longer-horizon pattern in which linked lines recur, coil, and return around a relatively stable civilizational or state axis across time.

Short form:

- arcs answer `what is moving`
- lenses answer `how it should be read`
- braids answer `what must be drafted together`
- helices answer `what keeps returning in transformed form`

## Braid Versus Helix

These are related but not interchangeable.

- Use a **braid** for a live drafting problem where several arcs must stay separate yet be handled together inside one instrument.
- Use a **helix** for a longer historical or civilizational pattern where the same pressures recur through successive turns, adaptations, and reappearances over time.

Practical split:

- braid = simultaneous coupling
- helix = longitudinal recurrence

Hormuz as a transaction is best treated as a braid. A century-scale pattern of chokepoint leverage, outside pressure, recognition struggle, and regime-survival adaptation would be better treated as a helix.

## Why Braids Matter For Statecraft

Some crisis objects cannot survive contact with institutions if they are drafted one line at a time. A route question may also be a coercion question and a legitimacy question. A sanctions bargain may also be a recognition question and an implementation question. A negotiation channel may also be a deterrence channel and a prestige channel.

The braid matters because statecraft is not only classification. It is conversion. Several arcs may each be analytically true, yet still fail as policy if they are not bundled into one authority-bearing, restraint-bearing, settlement-bearing object.

Use a braid when:

- one arc changes the meaning of another
- the same object must pass through several state layers to be real
- the clause language fails when the lines are drafted separately
- the failure mode is coupling blindness rather than missing information

Reach for a helix instead when the main task is to track repeated transformations across time rather than to draft one present instrument.

## Folder Choreography

In academy-statecraft, the braid belongs inside the workshop's existing folder movement:

- `civilization/` holds the long memory that authorizes the arc.
- `empire/` holds the reach, pressure, amplification, and ambition lines that complicate the object.
- `state/` identifies the current authority carriers, implementers, veto points, and successor burdens.
- `transactions/` spend the braid as an instrument: treaty clause, policy paper, negotiation brief, memo, compact, or framework.
- `sheets/` hold the reusable braid method so the same logic can be applied across lanes without duplicating doctrine.

This means the braid is not a substitute for lane work. It is the reusable scaffold that helps a lane move from memory and pressure into a draftable instrument.

## Minimal Braid Contract

Every braid use should name at least:

1. **Arcs**
Which durable strategic lines are in play?

2. **Lens**
What reading frame makes those lines legible here?

3. **Coupling claim**
Why do these arcs belong to one statecraft object rather than to several unrelated notes?

4. **Authority carrier or state layer**
Which offices, ministries, security organs, carriers, or implementation bodies must actually carry the bundle?

5. **Transaction use**
What draft object is this braid supposed to help produce?

6. **Tensions / falsifiers**
What would weaken the braid reading, split the bundle, or force rerouting into a different object?

## Hormuz Worked Example

Hormuz is a good first braid because it is not only a passage question.

Possible arcs:

- commercial passage
- coercive signaling
- negotiation-room leverage

Primary lens:

- announced-vs-material

The braid claim is that transit language, coercive posture, and bargaining leverage cannot be drafted as three separate topics if the goal is a real instrument. Passage claims affect whether coercive moves look like blockade, deterrent signaling, or controlled bargaining. Coercive signaling affects whether commercial openness sounds credible or merely rhetorical. Negotiation-room leverage affects whether openness becomes a settlement device, a face-saving formula, or a trap.

In statecraft terms:

- `civilization/` contributes memory around sovereignty, dignity, passage, endurance, and humiliation.
- `empire/` contributes the temptation to convert geography and chokepoint position into strategic reach.
- `state/` identifies who can actually authorize, implement, market, restrain, or deny a Hormuz formula.
- `transactions/` convert the braid into a compact, recognition clause, transit framework, sanctions-relief sequence, or de-escalation mechanism.

If those arcs are drafted separately, the instrument tends to fail by category mistake: one clause assumes commercial openness, another assumes latent coercion, and a third assumes bargaining leverage without specifying which authority carrier can keep them aligned.

### Worked Path: From Braid To Router To Transaction

Use the Hormuz bundle as the default example for this layer:

1. **Start in the braid.**
Name the three arcs together:

- commercial passage
- coercive signaling
- negotiation-room leverage

Keep the lens `announced-vs-material`. The drafting claim is that Iran cannot turn Hormuz into a usable statecraft object if transit language, coercive posture, and bargaining leverage are split into separate notes.

2. **Route the crisis object.**
Open [Transaction Router](transaction-router.md) and ask which settlement spine is actually being drafted.

- If the object is cross-lane and the main question is regional or system-wide bargaining over shipping, sanctions relief, escorts, and verification, route to [Hormuz Transit / Sanctions Relief Compact](../transactions/hormuz-transit-sanctions-relief-compact/).
- If the object is Iran-lane first and the main question is how Tehran converts restraint into recognition without losing leverage, route to [Hormuz Recognition Transit Transaction](../persia/transactions/hormuz-recognition-transit-transaction.md).

The worked example here takes the second path. The fit is not "shipping disruption in general." The fit is "Iran-local recognition through transit restraint."

3. **Spend the braid inside the transaction.**
In [Hormuz Recognition Transit Transaction](../persia/transactions/hormuz-recognition-transit-transaction.md), the braid becomes a clause with four coupled parts:

- transit guarantee
- reciprocal security recognition
- sanctions-relief sequence
- complaint / review mechanism

The credibility hinge is observability. The bundle should let outside actors see that restraint is being carried by named institutions, published notice, and recorded compliance milestones rather than by verbal reassurance alone.

That is the point of the braid. It preserves the linked arcs long enough for the transaction to draft one authority-bearing, restraint-bearing, settlement-bearing instrument instead of three incompatible gestures.

Short operator rule:

`braid the arcs -> route the settlement spine -> spend the bundle as a transaction`

## Validation

### Richelieu Check

- Which authority carrier can actually carry this braid through institutions?
- What durable state interest persists after the current headline or government mood?
- What administrative, diplomatic, fiscal, military, or legal machinery turns the braided object into a real instrument?
- Where does necessity clarify the bundle, and where might it become excuse?

### Bismarck Check

- What limited aim is sufficient for this braid?
- What demand would make the object expand into overreach?
- Which actor must be reassured, isolated, or kept neutral for the bundle to hold?
- What restraint clause prevents one successful line inside the braid from destabilizing the larger settlement?

The braid is only strong if it survives both checks at once: it must have a carrier, and it must not demand more than equilibrium can bear.

## Boundary

Braids are statecraft drafting structures. They are not truth labels, not substitutes for source capture, and not proof that a crisis has one correct reading. They exist to help the workshop preserve linked lines long enough to turn them into authority, restraint, and settlement.
