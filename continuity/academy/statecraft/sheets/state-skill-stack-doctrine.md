# State Skill Stack Doctrine
<!-- word_count: 425 -->


This note defines the constitutional split between the academy-statecraft deployment surface and the lane surfaces.

Short form:

`Deploy decides the lane. The lane decides the instrument.`

Precedence rule:

`Explicit coffee wins for entry ritual. Ambiguous ownership calls for state-deploy. Obvious ownership goes straight to state-*.`

## Two Layers

There are two different jobs in the skill stack:

1. **Routing layer**
   - owned by `state-deploy`
   - answers: `who owns this object now?`
2. **Substantive lane layer**
   - owned by `state-america`, `state-china`, `state-persia`, and `state-russia`
   - answers: `what can this owner legitimately draft, carry, accept, reject, and institutionalize?`

Do not collapse those jobs into one surface.

## State-Deploy

`state-deploy` is a router with judgment, not a fifth civilization.

Its responsibilities are:

- lane selection under ambiguity
- lane-local versus cross-lane classification
- one-lane versus two-lane versus compact depth choice
- work-shape choice:
  - commentary
  - braid
  - comparison
  - objection matrix
  - compact
  - transaction draft
- clean handoff into the chosen substantive lane

`state-deploy` must not become:

- a generic geopolitics commentator
- a substitute for lane doctrine
- a parallel statecraft theory layer

## State Lanes

The `state-*` skills are thick lane machines, not general routers.

They assume the lane has already been chosen, either explicitly by the operator or by `state-deploy`. Their job is to use the lane's identity grammar, helix, strands, state carriers, and transactions to decide what the lane can actually do with the object.

Each lane should therefore:

- start `README`-first
- descend `helix`-default
- route further only with a named reason
- keep folded literature testing inside the lane rather than beside it
- hand back toward `state-deploy` if the object is obviously cross-lane or misassigned

## Why The Split Exists

This split protects three things:

1. **Routing clarity**
   - the repo keeps one explicit lane-selection surface instead of teaching every lane to re-litigate ownership from scratch
2. **Lane orthogonality**
   - America, China, Persia, and Russia keep distinct civilizational grammars rather than becoming branded versions of one voice
3. **Governed recursive improvement**
   - live work can teach the system something without silently turning routing judgments into doctrine or doctrine into routing blur

## Recursive-Improvement Membrane

Live statecraft work may generate reviewable update candidates, but it must not silently rewrite lane books.

Durable discoveries should be staged into the appropriate lane update surface rather than directly mutating:

- `helix.md`
- `civilization/`
- `empire/`
- `state/`
- `transactions/`

The deployer may also surface cross-lane routing lessons, but those remain governed review material rather than automatic architecture drift.
