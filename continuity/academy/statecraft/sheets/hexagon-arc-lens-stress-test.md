# Hexagon Arc-Lens Stress Test
<!-- word_count: 877 -->


## Purpose

Use this sheet when a crisis, transaction, clause, current-event read, or policy memo needs the six civilization arc-lenses to act as a diagnostic system rather than as separate notes.

The hexagon asks whether an instrument can survive meaning, form, terrain, coercion, settlement, and sacred boundary at the same time.

## Six-Lens Structure

| Lens | Owns | Diagnostic Question |
|---|---|---|
| `god` | sacred boundary / forbidden bargain | What must not be profaned, invalidated, humiliated, or traded away? |
| `lit` | narrative, moral language, witness, grievance | What story makes the action legible, and what grievance could capture it? |
| `art` | form, beauty, spectacle, public legitimacy | What visible form makes authority believable, and where could spectacle replace substance? |
| `geo` | terrain, ecology, routes, resources, constraints | What land, water, route, chokepoint, resource, or climate fact permits or punishes the design? |
| `war` | coercion, command, sacrifice, escalation | What force, sacrifice, deterrence, or command burden is being activated? |
| `peace` | settlement, restraint, recognition, off-ramp | What makes restraint legitimate, verifiable, and politically survivable? |

## Balancing Axes

| Axis | Balance Test | Overreach Warning |
|---|---|---|
| `god <-> peace` | Can a sacred boundary become negotiated restraint without becoming surrender or profanation? | Peace erases the sacred claim, or sacred claim blocks every settlement. |
| `lit <-> war` | Does the moral narrative clarify coercive cost, or does it romanticize sacrifice? | Story becomes grievance theater, or force loses narrative legitimacy. |
| `art <-> geo` | Does the symbolic form match material terrain, ecology, route, and resource limits? | Spectacle outruns reality, or geography becomes determinism without human meaning. |

## Use Sequence

1. Name the crisis or transaction object.
2. Name the active lane or lanes.
3. Give each lens one distinct vote.
4. Test the three balancing axes.
5. Score consensus.
6. Name the brittle point.
7. Check whether the event extends an existing historical arc-lens.
8. Convert the result into the smallest useful statecraft artifact.

## Consensus Score

Use a lightweight 0-12 score:

- `0-2` points for each lens.
- `0` = lens rejects or exposes a severe brittleness.
- `1` = lens accepts with a named constraint.
- `2` = lens supports the instrument and improves the route to settlement.

Interpretation:

- `10-12`: strong hexagon consensus; draft or patch the instrument.
- `7-9`: usable but brittle; run the weakest axis before drafting.
- `4-6`: unstable; produce an objection matrix or reclassify the crisis object.
- `0-3`: do not draft yet; the premise is probably misclassified or overextended.

## Dry-Run Calibration

- **Taiwan maritime access / quarantine:** `geo` and `war` will usually carry the danger signal; `peace` must design inspection without blockade recognition; `god` and `lit` test humiliation, unity, and face.
- **Hormuz recognition / transit:** `geo` identifies the chokepoint; `war` prices escalation; `peace` converts transit into restraint; `god` and `lit` test dignity, sovereignty, and surrender language.
- **Panama canal water / transit sovereignty:** `geo` owns water and throughput; `art` checks sovereignty theater; `peace` protects transit reliability; `war` should remain low unless control claims militarize the object.

## Orthogonality Guard

- Do not let `geo` become `war`; terrain constrains coercion but does not itself command escalation.
- Do not let `peace` erase `god`; a settlement that crosses a sacred boundary will not hold.
- Do not let `art` become generic culture; it owns visible form, legitimacy, ceremony, spectacle, and public image.
- Do not let `lit` become commentary; it owns narrative, moral language, witness, grievance, and the wording that makes action intelligible.

## Narrative Discipline

When the object is great-power coordination, do not default to moralized bloc labels such as `axis of autocracy`. They may be rhetorically satisfying, but they often hide the mechanism.

Prefer mechanism labels that can be tested:

- `pressure-induced alignment`
- `sanctions-resistance architecture`
- `coercion-adaptation network`
- `energy / payments / insurance routing system`
- `asymmetric dependency alignment`

Core rule:

`If pressure builds the architecture, analyze the pressure paths before naming the bloc.`

## Arc Extension Check

Use this only when a live event mutates an existing arc-lens into a durable new form.

Ask:

- What older historical spine is being extended?
- What is the new form of the old pattern?
- What rule does the event add or sharpen?
- Does the event create optionality, dependency, brittleness, or a new settlement constraint?
- Should the insight remain an observation, become a lane-local recursive candidate, or patch a transaction?

Examples:

- Russia depth can mutate from land-depth into energy-depth, sanctions-resistance depth, nuclear warning-time depth, or dependency-depth.
- China maritime access can mutate from naval geography into insurance, customs, ports, face-saving restraint, and quarantine ambiguity.
- America transit sovereignty can mutate from continental insulation into infrastructure, water, climate, and allied trust constraints.

Do not mark an event as an arc extension merely because it fits the old pattern. It must reveal a durable new form, threshold, or rule.

## Membrane Rule

If the stress test reveals a durable lane improvement, stage it only as a recursive candidate in `<lane>/updates/pending.md`. Do not directly rewrite transactions, CIV-MEM, PH-CIV, Record, raw-input, or current carrier files unless the operator separately asks.
