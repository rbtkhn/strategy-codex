- Civilization input: Russian security memory treats regime-change pressure and strategic encirclement as linked coercive patterns.
- Empire input: Russia wants multipolar leverage, partner resilience, and reduced U.S. unilateral control over nuclear thresholds.
- Statecraft regulation: Support Iran's security recognition without making Moscow sponsor uncontrolled proliferation.

## State Interest

Russia's durable interest is to prevent U.S. coercive precedent while avoiding a proliferation cascade that destabilizes Eurasian partners and Russia's own security geometry.

## Leverage / Constraint

- Leverage being spent: Diplomatic backing, nuclear-technical credibility, mediator role, sanctions workarounds, and Security Council positioning.
- Constraint that limits the leverage: Open Iranian weaponization could increase regional chaos, pressure Russia's partners, and justify wider militarization.
- Volatility note: Iran's latency may weaken U.S. coercion, but breakout can convert leverage into balancing pressure.

## Richelieu Validation

- Institutional carrier: Foreign ministry, nuclear regulatory expertise, Security Council channels, and mediator mechanisms.
- Capacity: Verification language, technical fuel-cycle proposals, sanctions-relief sequencing, and diplomatic guarantees.
- Continuity risk: Anti-U.S. advantage can tempt Russia to tolerate instability that later rebounds.
- Long-pressure burden: Russia must preserve nonproliferation credibility while resisting coercive regime-change logic.

## Bismarck Validation

- Limited aim: Recognize Iran's security and enrichment dignity while stopping weaponization.
- Actor to isolate: Preventive-war advocates and actors trying to use nonproliferation as regime-change cover.
- Actor to reassure, restrain, or keep neutral: China, India, Gulf states, Israel, and Iran.
- Alliance geometry: Support Iran enough to block unilateral coercion; restrain Iran enough to prevent a regional nuclear race.
- Restraint after advantage: Encourage transparency once sanctions relief and assurances are credible.

## Instrument Text

Draft clause:

> Russia shall support a nuclear-latency recognition framework in which Iran retains civilian nuclear rights under enhanced, non-humiliating verification while accepting renewable non-weaponization commitments. Russia shall back phased sanctions relief and security assurances, and shall oppose military action or regime-change measures that would incentivize weaponization.

## Mirror Test

- America: This may legitimize Iran's threshold status.
- Russia: This must not make Russia responsible for Iranian breakout.
- China: This is useful only if it reduces disorder.
- Iran: This must not become a great-power cage around Iran's sovereign rights.

## Falsifiers / Revisit Triggers

- Falsifier: Iran rejects Russian-backed verification or Russia distances from Iran's nuclear rights.
- Revisit trigger: Security Council move, IAEA access shift, Russian technical proposal, or U.S. attack threat.
- Scoreboard movement: Review if Mearsheimer balancing pressure rises faster than Pape coercive leverage.
- Actor signal: Russian mediation offer, Iranian acceptance of technical limits, or China/Russia joint language.
