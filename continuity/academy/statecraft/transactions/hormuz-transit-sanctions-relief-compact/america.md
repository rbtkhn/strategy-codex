## Civilization / Empire Helix Input

- Civilization input: American legitimacy depends on lawful authority, maritime order, allied confidence, and domestic consent.
- Empire input: America wants control over sea lanes, sanctions access, insurance, coalition command, and escalation tempo.
- Statecraft regulation: Bound the control claim so reopening Hormuz does not require unlimited war or executive drift.
- Relevant lane helix: [America helix](../../america/helix.md).

## State Interest

America's durable interest is to restore predictable Gulf transit and allied confidence without proving that Iran has a permanent veto over global commerce.

## Leverage / Constraint

- Leverage being spent: Sanctions relief authority, maritime guarantees, coalition convening, insurance / finance influence, and military deterrence.
- Constraint that limits the leverage: Overextension, domestic war fatigue, allied free-riding, legal authority, and the danger that coercion confirms Iran's system-shaping power.
- Volatility note: If Pape's coercive-center score rises while Mearsheimer's overextension warnings also rise, America needs a compact faster, not a larger war plan.

## Richelieu Validation

- Institutional carrier: State Department, Treasury, Congress, naval command, allied maritime coordination cell, and mediator channel.
- Capacity: Sanctions waiver mechanics, maritime notification, congressional reporting, allied burden-sharing, and insurance stabilization.
- Continuity risk: Presidential impulse or donor pressure may undermine the authority required for durable relief.
- Long-pressure burden: America must preserve deterrence without making emergency power the normal instrument.

## Bismarck Validation

- Limited aim: Restore transit predictability and regain freedom of action.
- Actor to isolate: Regime-change maximalists and actors profiting from uncontrolled escalation.
- Actor to reassure, restrain, or keep neutral: Gulf states, China, India, Europe, and domestic Congress.
- Alliance geometry: Make allies carry verification and maritime costs rather than only endorse U.S. escalation.
- Restraint after advantage: Do not treat partial reopening as permission for new coercive demands.

## Instrument Text

Draft clause:

> The United States shall support a renewable 30-day Hormuz transit guarantee through limited sanctions waivers, maritime insurance stabilization, and reciprocal non-regime-change assurances, provided that Iran maintains safe passage for non-belligerent commercial vessels through a neutral notification channel. Any suspension of relief shall require documented breach, allied consultation, and congressional notification within 72 hours.

## Mirror Test

- America: Does this preserve credibility or merely disguise retreat?
- Russia: Does this reduce pressure on U.S. primacy too cheaply?
- China: Does this actually stabilize energy flows?
- Iran: Does this offer relief and dignity, or only temporary restraint without recognition?

## Falsifiers / Revisit Triggers

- Falsifier: Transit resumes without U.S. relief or guarantee, proving the compact was unnecessary.
- Revisit trigger: Congress blocks waivers, allies refuse burden-sharing, Iran changes transit terms, or China creates a parallel insurance mechanism.
- Scoreboard movement: Review if Pape leverage falls or Mearsheimer overextension rises sharply.
- Actor signal: Gulf state buy-in, Iranian controlled reopening, congressional authorization, or allied maritime cost-sharing.
