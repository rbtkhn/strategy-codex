## State Interest

Iran's durable interest is to monetize Hormuz leverage into recognition, sanctions relief, non-regime-change assurances, and regional architecture without losing control of escalation.

## Leverage / Constraint

- Leverage being spent: Hormuz denial capacity, missiles and drones, nuclear latency, resource self-sufficiency, partner channels, and endurance.
- Constraint that limits the leverage: Prolonged disruption can alienate China, India, Russia, Gulf states, and non-aligned opinion.
- Volatility note: Iran has immediate coercive leverage but not unlimited structural insulation; the compact should trade temporary pressure for durable recognition.

## Richelieu Validation

- Institutional carrier: Foreign ministry, Supreme National Security Council, port authorities, central bank / sanctions-relief channel, and mediator secretariat.
- Capacity: Transit notification, complaint channel, sanctions-relief benchmarks, defensive command discipline, and verification without humiliation.
- Continuity risk: Factional escalation can outrun institutional bargaining.
- Long-pressure burden: Iran must prove it can regulate leverage, not merely threaten disruption.

## Bismarck Validation

- Limited aim: Recognition, sanctions-relief sequence, and transit-restraint bargain.
- Actor to isolate: Regime-change advocates and actors trying to keep Iran outside regional architecture.
- Actor to reassure, restrain, or keep neutral: China, Russia, India, Gulf states, Oman, and Europe.
- Alliance geometry: Preserve partner depth while reopening enough commerce to keep partners aligned.
- Restraint after advantage: Reopen transit in phases as relief and assurance benchmarks begin.

## Compact Position

The Iran lane enters the compact with four non-negotiable requirements derived from the canonical transaction:

1. Transit restraint must be coupled to reciprocal recognition and a front-loaded, renewable relief step.
2. Gulf participation may support notification and deconfliction, but not supervisory authority over Iranian sovereign decision-making.
3. Observability must be neutral and market-legible through published notices, a complaint channel, and a compliance ledger.
4. Relief cannot be reversed unilaterally outside the same mediated compliance process that judges transit performance.

## Mirror Test

- America: This risks rewarding coercive leverage.
- Russia: This may spend leverage too early.
- China: This is acceptable only if it produces reliable energy flow.
- Iran: This must not freeze Iranian leverage while relief remains reversible.

## Falsifiers / Revisit Triggers

- Falsifier: Iran cannot define acceptable relief and recognition conditions for reopening transit.
- Revisit trigger: New sanctions offer, mediator proposal, China / Russia pressure, Gulf guarantee, or U.S. non-regime-change language.
- Scoreboard movement: Review if Pape leverage falls or Mearsheimer balancing pressure isolates Iran.
- Actor signal: Iranian controlled reopening, formal mediator channel, verified sanctions relief, or partner distancing.
