# Bookshelf Crosswalk
<!-- word_count: 727 -->


This crosswalk wires academy-theology into the Bookshelf as a study path, not a ranking. It is title-first for reading, with `Shelf-*` ids for catalog lookup.

## Reading Path

1. **The Old Testament (Shelf-0036)** - Covenant, law, prophecy, exile, kingship, lament, and sacred history as the deep grammar of later Abrahamic imagination.
2. **The New Testament (Shelf-0037)** - Incarnation, universal love, mercy, apocalypse, and the pressure that turns empire into a spiritual problem.
3. **The Koran (Shelf-0299)** - Revelation, submission, judgment, law, ummah, and the Islamic challenge to Christian and post-Christian order.
4. **Hindu Scriptures (Shelf-0038)** - Dharma, cosmic order, divine plurality, selfhood, duty, and liberation outside the Abrahamic frame.
5. **Lao-Tzu: Tao Te Ching (Shelf-0039)** - Non-coercive order, emptiness, yielding, and rule by alignment rather than domination.
6. **Confucius: The Analects (Shelf-0040)** - Ritual, filial order, moral cultivation, and civilization as disciplined form.
7. **The New Roman Empire: A History of Byzantium (Shelf-0058)** - New Rome, Orthodoxy, imperial Christianity, liturgy, continuity, and sacred legitimacy as state form.
8. **Heart of Europe (Shelf-0301)** - Holy Roman order, Latin Christendom, layered jurisdiction, confessional structure, and negotiated sovereignty.
9. **Plato: The Republic (Shelf-0041)** - Soul and city, justice, education, myth, philosopher rule, and the permanent temptation to sacralize political order.
10. **History of Western Philosophy (Shelf-0300)** - A map of the Western argument from Greek metaphysics through Christianity, Enlightenment, Kant, and modern rupture.
11. **Meditations (Shelf-0051)** - Inner rule under imperial pressure; attention, duty, mortality, and conscience without theatrical metaphysics.
12. **Aeschylus: The Oresteia (Shelf-0044)** - Blood revenge, divine judgment, law, civic order, and the fragile birth of public justice.
13. **Sophocles: The Theban Plays (Shelf-0043)** - Fate, family, blindness, piety, authority, and the limits of political command.
14. **The Iliad (Shelf-0046)** - Glory, rage, mortality, sacrifice, and the sacred violence beneath heroic civilization.
15. **The Aeneid (Shelf-0048)** - Duty, founding, loss, empire, and the cost of turning suffering into destiny.
16. **Inferno (Shelf-0080)** - Sin as ordered vision; the moral imagination of consequences, hierarchy, and misdirected love.
17. **Paradise Lost (Shelf-0130)** - Pride, rebellion, freedom, obedience, fall, and the grandeur and danger of Satanic self-assertion.
18. **Candide and Other Stories (Shelf-0119)** - Voltaire as skeptical acid against providential optimism, metaphysical complacency, and pious explanations of suffering.
19. **Selected Works (Shelf-0145)** - Goethe as Faustian striving: knowledge, appetite, creation, temptation, and the modern soul's bargain with power.
20. **Les Miserables (Shelf-0132)** - Mercy versus law, grace against social machinery, and redemption as public as well as private reality.
21. **The Brothers Karamazov (Shelf-0195)** - Orthodox conscience, freedom, patricide, suffering, and the moral catastrophe of explaining away the soul.
22. **War and Peace (Shelf-0193)** - Tolstoy-lens history: pressure fields, false great men, providence anxiety, and the moral life inside mass motion.
23. **Anna Karenina (Shelf-0194)** - Love, family, social judgment, delusion, renunciation, and the religious question inside ordinary life.
24. **Fear and Trembling (Shelf-0216)** - Faith under impossible command; the individual before God, beyond ethics, system, and public justification.
25. **Beyond Good and Evil (Shelf-0214)** - Nietzsche as rupture: genealogy, moral suspicion, will, aristocratic critique, and the destabilization of inherited order.

## Kaldellis And Wilson

Read Kaldellis and Wilson as a paired hinge, not as background history.

**Kaldellis: New Rome.** Byzantium keeps Roman continuity alive in Greek, Christian, imperial, and liturgical form. Its theological importance is that Orthodoxy is not merely a set of doctrines; it is a civilizational pattern where empire, church, ceremony, law, and memory make one another legible.

**Wilson: Heart of Europe.** The Holy Roman Empire offers a different Christian political form: plural, layered, negotiated, corporate, and jurisdictional. Its theological importance is that Latin Christendom does not simply become the nation-state; it passes through a long experiment in sacred legitimacy, distributed authority, and confessional settlement.

**Comparison question.** What changes when Christian empire is centered on Constantinople's Roman continuity versus German Europe's layered Holy Roman order?

**Tolstoy-lens question.** In both cases, rulers matter, but they are acting inside pressure fields: sacred legitimacy, institutional memory, military geography, dynastic inheritance, ritual order, and rival visions of Christian universality.
