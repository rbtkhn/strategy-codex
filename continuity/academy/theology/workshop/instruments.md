# Instruments
<!-- word_count: 538 -->


These are reusable interpretive tools for academy theology, Predictive History notes, and strategy work.

## Tolstoy Lens Theology Check

Use when a historical actor appears decisive but may be carried by deeper sacred pressure.

- What sacred story, ritual memory, or moral imagination shaped the action?
- What non-sacred pressures were also active: geography, economy, institutions, war, dynasty, status?
- What responsibility remains with visible actors?
- What would be lost if the explanation became "great man" history?
- What would be lost if the explanation erased agency entirely?

## Providence Versus Pressure Boundary

Use when a text, ruler, movement, or civilization describes events as providential.

Core sentence: sacred pressure can make an action historically legible without making it divinely authorized, morally innocent, or inevitable.

Checks:

- Name the providential claim.
- Name the historical pressure.
- State what the workshop is not affirming.
- Identify the moral risk created by the claim.

## False Universal Love Detector

Use when universal love is invoked in a way that may hide domination.

- Does the claim preserve the face of the concrete person?
- Does it require the other to disappear into a category?
- Does it create permission for coercion "for their own good"?
- Does it remain non-zero-sum under disagreement?
- Does it make room for refusal, grief, and patience?

## Sacred Coordination Pattern

Use when a doctrine, ritual, or story coordinates a group.

- What behavior becomes easier because this sacred form exists?
- What sacrifice or restraint does it make intelligible?
- What outsiders does it classify?
- What institutional form carries it across generations?
- What breaks when the sacred form is removed?

## Face Category Blade Test

Use when theology, politics, or strategy begins to harden into enemy abstraction.

- What category is replacing the person?
- What face, name, story, or vulnerability is being hidden?
- What action becomes thinkable only after the category takes over?
- What would change if the specific person were restored to view?
- Does the interpretation return to the condition before it returns to action?

## Empire Sacralization Test

Use when political order borrows sacred legitimacy.

- What part of empire is being made holy: ruler, law, land, mission, church, people, sacrifice, victory?
- Which tradition authorizes or resists that sacralization?
- Does sacred order restrain power or decorate it?
- What happens to dissenters, heretics, foreigners, and defeated enemies?
- Where does the empire confuse continuity with innocence?

## Command Responsibility Check

Use when sacred, moral, institutional, or historical pressure is real but responsibility must not disappear.

- Who ordered the action?
- Who executed it?
- Who enabled, funded, blessed, justified, or concealed it?
- What alternatives were visible at the time?
- Who benefited?
- Who suffered?
- What responsibility remains after pressure is named?

## Pressure And Agency Check

Use when theology explains an action through sacred pressure, historical pressure, or civilizational momentum.

- What pressure explains the action?
- What did the actor still choose?
- What alternatives were visible at the time?
- What does the explanation risk excusing?
- What would a responsible account still have to condemn?
- What would be lost if agency were erased?
