# Bookshelf Crosswalk
<!-- word_count: 768 -->

This crosswalk wires academy-military into the Bookshelf as a study path, not a ranking. It is title-first for reading, with `Shelf-*` ids for catalog lookup.

## Reading Path

1. **The Iliad (Shelf-0046)** - Rage, honor, mortality, heroic violence, sacrifice, and the moral imagination underneath war.
2. **The Art of War (Shelf-0302)** - Strategy, deception, indirectness, economy of force, museum identity knowledge (archive), enemy knowledge, and victory before battle.
3. **The Landmark Herodotus (Shelf-0002)** - Empire, expedition, hubris, geography, ethnography, and war as civilizational encounter.
4. **The Landmark Thucydides (Shelf-0003)** - War as fear, interest, honor, alliance logic, maritime empire, faction, and escalation.
5. **The Landmark Xenophon's Anabasis (Shelf-0005)** - Retreat, command under stress, expeditionary fragility, cohesion, and leadership after strategic collapse.
6. **The Landmark Arrian: The Campaigns of Alexander (Shelf-0006)** - Operational tempo, decisive battle, imperial advance, logistics, and the charisma-problem in conquest.
7. **The Landmark Julius Caesar (Shelf-0007)** - Command memoir, campaign narrative, engineering, civil-military ambition, and political war.
8. **The Punic Wars (Shelf-0018)** - Maritime rivalry, strategic endurance, coalition strain, adaptation, and the long contest for Mediterranean primacy.
9. **Hannibal (Shelf-0019)** - Operational brilliance, logistics, enemy-center strategy, and the limits of battlefield genius.
10. **Rome and Persia: The Seven Hundred Year Rivalry (Shelf-0034)** - Frontier pressure, long-war equilibrium, imperial stamina, and peer rivalry across centuries.
11. **The New Roman Empire: A History of Byzantium (Shelf-0058)** - Roman continuity, Orthodoxy, imperial defense, frontier endurance, and strategy as civilizational survival.
12. **The Life and Legend of the Sultan Saladin (Shelf-0069)** - Command, legitimacy, coalition politics, jihad, crusade, and the making of military memory.
13. **Iron and Blood (Shelf-0066)** - Germany as military-political formation: Habsburg, Prussia, discipline, state-building, and institutional violence.
14. **The Thirty Years War (Shelf-0084)** - Confessional war, logistics, devastation, mercenary systems, state formation, and exhaustion.
15. **Crucible of War (Shelf-0098)** - Seven Years War, imperial competition, North American theater, and the military prehistory of revolution.
16. **The Campaigns of Napoleon (Shelf-0303)** - Operational art, decisive maneuver, corps system, logistics, empire, and the road from brilliance to overextension.
17. **On War (Shelf-0146)** - Friction, fog, center of gravity, escalation, moral forces, and war as politics continued by other means.
18. **Empires of the Sea (Shelf-0246)** - Mediterranean naval war, Malta, Lepanto, Ottoman-Habsburg rivalry, and sea control as civilizational contest.
19. **George Marshall: Defender of the Republic (Shelf-0255)** - Staff command, coalition war, mobilization, military professionalism, and strategic restraint.
20. **Eisenhower: Soldier and President (Shelf-0258)** - Supreme command, coalition management, presidential military judgment, and the bridge from WWII to Cold War.
21. **The General vs. the President (Shelf-0256)** - Civil-military control, nuclear brinkmanship, Korea, MacArthur, Truman, and command discipline.
22. **The Second World War (Shelf-0304)** - Grand strategy, coalition politics, British state memory, wartime leadership, and victory narrated by a principal actor.
23. **The Road to Stalingrad (Shelf-0293)** - Eastern Front catastrophe, Soviet adaptation, German overreach, operational depth, and industrial war.
24. **The Road to Berlin (Shelf-0294)** - Strategic recovery, attrition, operational art, coalition pressure, and the endgame of continental war.
25. **American Caesar (Shelf-0298)** - MacArthur as theater commander, Pacific war, Korea, personal myth, and the civil-military danger of charismatic command.

## Reader Lanes

Use the two academy-military writer lanes as interpretive companions, not as primary authorities:

- [Big Serge](bigserge/) for campaign narrative, theater-level synthesis, operational tempo, and the story-shape of war.
- [Simplicius](simplicius/) for military-escalation synthesis, blockade and Gulf-war narratives, and source-critical stress tests.

## Study Arc

**Ancient grammar.** The Iliad, Sunzi, Herodotus, Thucydides, Xenophon, Arrian, Caesar, the Punic Wars, Hannibal, Rome-Persia, and Kaldellis give the first vocabulary: honor, deception, inquiry, fear, interest, command, logistics, frontier, conquest, and civilizational endurance.

**Medieval and early modern pressure.** Saladin, Iron and Blood, the Thirty Years War, Crucible of War, Chandler's Napoleon, and Empires of the Sea show war as legitimacy, state formation, siege, naval contest, operational art, dynastic endurance, and civilizational memory.

**Modern command.** Clausewitz, Marshall, Eisenhower, MacArthur, Churchill, and Erickson move the shelf toward state capacity, civil-military control, coalition command, industrial war, grand strategy, and escalation discipline.

## Academy Hooks

- Pair **The Art of War (Shelf-0302)** and **On War (Shelf-0146)** with Big Serge when campaign narration needs theory checks: deception, friction, fog, moral forces, and political purpose.
- Pair **Rome and Persia (Shelf-0034)** and **The New Roman Empire (Shelf-0058)** with Simplicius when contemporary Gulf or frontier narratives need long-war, deterrence, and imperial-endurance analogies.
- Pair **The Road to Stalingrad (Shelf-0293)** and **The Road to Berlin (Shelf-0294)** with Big Serge when reading industrial war, attrition, operational depth, and narrative drift.
- Keep every writer-lane claim separate from primary wires, imagery, official claims, and ORBAT work.
