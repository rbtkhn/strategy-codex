# Eastern Front Industrial War
<!-- word_count: 205 -->


## Purpose

Study industrial war at scale: attrition, operational depth, adaptation, political will, logistics, production, and the limits of clean campaign narrative.

## Bookshelf Anchors

- **The Road to Stalingrad (Shelf-0293)** - catastrophe, German overreach, Soviet adaptation, operational depth, and industrial war.
- **The Road to Berlin (Shelf-0294)** - strategic recovery, attrition, coalition pressure, and the continental endgame.
- **The Second World War (Shelf-0304)** - grand strategy and war memory from a principal actor.

## Writer-Lane Companions

- [Big Serge](../../bigserge/) for campaign and theater-level narrative.
- [Simplicius](../../simplicius/) for escalation and source-critical stress tests.

## Working Questions

- Is the campaign being decided by maneuver, attrition, production, morale, or external supply?
- What evidence shows adaptation rather than collapse?
- Which side can replace losses faster?
- What does operational depth make possible?
- What does narrative hide when the scale becomes industrial?

## Counter-Reading

Industrial war analysis can become inhuman arithmetic. The face/category/map-abstraction check must remain active: production and attrition are real, but the people inside the numbers are also real.

## Output Prompts

- Apply the attrition vs decision test to one front-line claim.
- Identify one logistics constraint and one morale constraint.
- Write one warning against map-abstraction.
