## Source Notes

### S1 - Figure AI / BMW Spartanburg deployment

**Source:** Figure AI, "F.02 Contributed to the Production of 30,000 Cars at BMW"  
**Published:** 2025-11-19  
**URL:** https://www.figure.ai/news/production-at-bmw

Figure reports an 11-month Figure 02 deployment at BMW Group Plant Spartanburg. The reported use case was sheet-metal loading on an active assembly line. Reported figures include 10-hour weekday shifts, more than 90,000 parts loaded, more than 1,250 runtime hours, contribution to more than 30,000 BMW X3 vehicles, and about 1.2 million robot steps. Figure says the Figure 02 fleet is being retired as Figure 03 operational readiness begins.

**Use in benchmark:** Strong evidence for controlled industrial pilot progress; not evidence of general-purpose home or broad labor substitution.

### S2 - BMW production perspective

**Source:** BMW Group, "First humanoid robot introduced in Plant Leipzig"  
**Published:** 2026-03  
**URL:** https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/news/general/2026/humanoid-robot-in-leipzig.html

BMW describes Figure 02 at Spartanburg as a pilot in vehicle production and reports similar numbers: more than 30,000 X3s, 90,000 components, 1,250 operating hours, and 1.2 million steps. BMW also notes lessons around physical AI in production, including safety concepts with barriers/partitions and improved 5G coverage in the hall.

**Use in benchmark:** Strong corroboration that current capability is real but bounded by factory safety, connectivity, and repeatable-task constraints.

### S3 - 1X NEO consumer launch

**Source:** 1X Technologies, "NEO Home Robot | Order Today"  
**Published:** 2025-10-28  
**URL:** https://www.1x.tech/ja_jp/discover/neo-home-robot

1X presents NEO as a consumer-ready humanoid robot for home use, emphasizing household chores, personalized assistance, and a transition from research object to product. The announcement frames NEO as a direct-to-consumer category step rather than an industrial-only robot.

**Use in benchmark:** Evidence for the consumer/home meaning claim; still company-framed and should not be treated as proof of broad autonomy or adoption.

### S5 - Tesla Optimus production-line intent

**Source:** Tesla Q4 and FY 2025 Update, mirrored PDF excerpt; Tesla IR press release points to the Q4/FY25 update  
**Published:** 2026-01-28  
**URLs:** https://ir.tesla.com/press-release/tesla-releases-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-financial-results and https://electrek.co/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2026/01/TSLA-Q4-2025-Update.pdf

Tesla's Q4/FY25 update lists Optimus under Robotics with California status as construction. The update says Gen 3 is intended as the first mass-production design, that preparations are underway for the first production line and supply-chain readiness, and that start of production is planned before the end of 2026, with eventual planned capacity of 1 million robots per year. The same update cautions that installed capacity is not current production rate and depends on uptime, component supply, factory upgrades, regulatory factors, and other limits.

**Use in benchmark:** Evidence of serious production-line intent; not evidence that Optimus is already at scale or commercially proven.

## Weakest Factual Link

The weakest link is broad labor substitution. The source pack supports pilots, product announcements, factory buildout, and production-line intent. It does not yet prove mass deployment across service, care, logistics, or household labor.
