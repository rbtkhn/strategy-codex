# The Hungerless Appetite

## Chronicle

By 2031, appetite became optional for the affluent and subsidized for the poor. Weekly GLP-1 maintenance was cheaper than diabetes care, obesity treatment, school lunch logistics, and workplace wellness programs, so insurers, employers, and public health agencies all converged on the same quiet bargain: fewer cravings, fewer calories, fewer crises.

The visible result looked like abundance. Grocery aisles shrank. Fast-food chains became protein-clinic brands. Office cafeterias turned into supplement counters. Families still gathered at tables, but the meal was often ceremonial: broth, tea, a bite of fruit, a shared packet of micronutrients.

Then the black market appeared. Not for calories, exactly, but for appetite. Private dining clubs sold "unblocked weekends" under medical supervision. Underground kitchens offered high-fat, high-heat, high-risk tasting sequences designed to overwhelm the dampened reward system. Wealthy clients paid for memory menus: the exact lasagna from childhood, the heat of street noodles after midnight, the forbidden hunger before a feast.

The law had trouble naming the offense. Was this drug noncompliance, food safety evasion, vice, therapy, or theater? The market answered faster than regulators. Real food became less a commodity than an experience of being unregulated by one's own optimization.

## Reflection

The key mistake is to read this as nostalgia. The deeper mechanism is scarcity migration. When a technology makes one old scarcity disappear, status and desire often reorganize around the residue it cannot automate. Hunger becomes medically solvable; appetite becomes culturally scarce. Nutrition becomes cheap; meaning concentrates around risk, memory, and chosen excess.

The black market is therefore not a failure of the GLP-1 regime in the narrow sense. It is evidence that the regime succeeded materially while leaving symbolic demand untouched. People do not only eat to fuel bodies. They eat to mark time, rank, grief, intimacy, rebellion, and local identity. Remove the biological compulsion, and food does not vanish. It becomes more theatrical, more curated, and more unequal.

The strategic question is not whether society should reverse the drugs. It is whether institutions can build legitimate rituals for embodied pleasure before illicit markets monopolize them. If every public message treats appetite as a pathology, appetite will return as contraband.

## Predictive Outlook

**Call:** The first durable category will not be "illegal food" but licensed appetite events: clinics, retreats, religious exemptions, culinary therapy, and high-end dining that sells temporary permission to feel hunger.

**Falsifier:** If GLP-1 use rises while premium dining, food tourism, and appetite-restoration services decline for three consecutive years, the scarcity-migration thesis weakens.

**Revisit:** Revisit in 18 months by checking whether insurers, luxury hospitality, or public health regulators begin distinguishing nutrition compliance from sanctioned food experience.

## Appendix

Watch for language. When companies stop saying "weight loss" and start saying "relationship with food," the market has moved from metabolism to meaning.
