# Persia Sacred Grammar

- **Core grammar**
  Asha against Druj: righteous order against corruption, lie, and chaos. Persian sacred grammar binds justice, sovereignty, endurance, and dignity into one moral-political frame.

- **Legitimating image**
  Rule is rightful when it guards order without surrendering sovereignty. The king or state is not the source of truth, but the guardian charged with resisting corruption, humiliation, and domination.

- **Continuity through rupture**
  Zoroastrian moral order survives conquest, Islamization, Safavid Shi'a state formation, constitutional constraint, anti-imperial injury, revolution, and sanctions-era siege. The grammar is not preserved as unchanged doctrine; it is re-encoded through Persianate and Shi'a forms.

- **Relation to sovereignty chain**
  Cyrus remains the foundational sovereign because he opens the durable Persian political chain. Zoroaster stands deeper: he provides the moral architecture that later kingship, Shi'a legitimacy, and modern Iranian endurance can inherit without being identical to one regime form.

- **Statecraft relevance**
  Persia can carry reciprocal restraint when it preserves dignity, sovereign choice, anti-humiliation sequencing, and non-tutelary verification. It resists bargains that convert relief into permission, verification into humiliation, or restraint into symbolic surrender.
