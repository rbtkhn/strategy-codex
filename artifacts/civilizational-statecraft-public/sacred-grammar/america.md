# America Sacred Grammar

- **Core grammar**
  Covenant, constitutional order, rights, providence, and civil religion. American sacred grammar makes liberty feel rightful when it is carried through law, consent, oath, and governed succession rather than raw destiny alone.

- **Legitimating image**
  A people under law, authorized by consent, bounded by rights, and periodically renewed through office, oath, and constitutional procedure. Providence can energize the grammar, but becomes dangerous when it outruns legal restraint.

- **Continuity through rupture**
  Constitutional sacredness survives civil war, amendment, imperial growth, rights contradiction, global mission, and internal fragmentation. The grammar is therefore not merely procedural, but neither is it reducible to church or creed; it is covenantal, juridical, and civil-religious at once.

- **Relation to sovereignty chain**
  Alfred belongs to the longer Anglo-American continuity chain, while 1776 and 1789 mark American sovereign birth and durable carrier form. The sacred grammar explains why the American chain can treat written procedure, rights, and oath as morally thick rather than administratively thin.

- **Statecraft relevance**
  America can carry reciprocal restraint when it preserves constitutional legitimacy, rights language, lawful consent, and successor-stable obligations. It becomes brittle when providence, mission, or emergency power claims outrun law and convert liberty into empire cover.
