# Hexagon Lattice

`hexagon-lattice` applies the six civilization arc-lenses as a distributed statecraft diagnostic.

No central pillar. No master lens. Each arc-lens has partial sovereignty. Consensus emerges from distributed pressure among:

- `god` - sacred boundary / forbidden bargain.
- `lit` - narrative, moral language, witness, grievance.
- `art` - form, beauty, spectacle, public legitimacy.
- `geo` - terrain, ecology, routes, resources, constraints.
- `war` - coercion, command, sacrifice, escalation.
- `peace` - settlement, restraint, recognition, off-ramp.

## Boundary

- WORK only; not Record.

- Do not edit CIV-MEM, PH-CIV, Record, raw-input, transaction, carrier, or lane files unless the operator separately asks for implementation.

## Workflow

1. **Name the object.** Identify the crisis, transaction, clause, memo, or policy question.
2. **Interpret the command.** If the operator used a lane prefix, state it explicitly: `Command interpretation: Russia-first hexagon read on <topic>.`
3. **Name lanes.** State the active lane or lanes: America, Russia, China, Iran, or cross-lane.
4. **Give six distinct votes.** Each lens should contribute one concise judgment and must not steal a neighboring lens's role.
5. **Run the three axes.**
   - `god <-> peace`: sacred boundary versus negotiated restraint.
   - `lit <-> war`: moral narrative versus coercive cost.
   - `art <-> geo`: symbolic form versus material constraint.
6. **Score consensus.** Use the 0-12 score from the sheet.
7. **Name the brittle point.** Identify the lens or axis most likely to break the instrument.
8. **Convert to statecraft use.** Recommend the smallest useful next artifact: clause, memo, objection matrix, transaction fit patch, lane read, or recursive candidate.
9. **Offer four calibrated option paths.** A-D must be specific to the topic, not generic.

## Orthogonality Rules

- `geo` constrains coercion but does not itself become `war`.
- `peace` designs restraint but cannot erase `god`.
- `art` owns form, public image, and spectacle; it is not generic culture.
- `lit` owns narrative and moral language; it is not broad commentary.
- `war` prices command and escalation; it should not swallow all risk.
- `god` names forbidden bargains; it should not veto every hard bargain.

## Narrative Discipline

For great-power coordination, prefer mechanism labels over moralized bloc labels.

- Prefer `pressure-induced alignment` when sanctions, military pressure, energy routing, payments, insurance, technology denial, or diplomatic shelter are assembling the coalition.
- Prefer `sanctions-resistance architecture` when the key mechanism is evasion, settlement systems, shipping, insurance, or commodity routing.
- Prefer `asymmetric dependency alignment` when ceremonial equality conceals buyer concentration, pricing leverage, technology gatekeeping, escalation ownership, or route lock-in.
- Avoid treating `axis of autocracy` as an analytic conclusion. If it appears, translate it into the concrete mechanism being tested.

Core test:

`If pressure builds the architecture, analyze the pressure paths before naming the bloc.`
