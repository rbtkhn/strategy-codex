# Work membrane live examples

**Purpose:** Give the shared [work membrane v2](work-membrane-v2.md) one compact companion surface built from live repo examples, so contributors can feel the class differences instead of only reading definitions.

This is a bounded doctrine companion, not a generated inventory.

---

## The three classes that most often get confused

### `governed adjacent`

This is where durable non-Record judgment lives.

Live examples:

- [statecraft/daily/2026-05.md](../statecraft/daily/2026-05.md)
- [statecraft/states/reader-guide.md](../statecraft/states/reader-guide.md)
- [statecraft/states/indexes/failure-mode-routes.md](../statecraft/states/indexes/failure-mode-routes.md)
- [singularity/workshop/keystone-helix.md](../singularity/workshop/keystone-helix.md)

What these have in common:

- they are meant to persist
- they carry judgment, routing, or doctrine
- they are citeable inside later work
- they are not rebuildable convenience surfaces
- they are not the companion Record

Short feel:

`governed adjacent` is where the repo keeps durable thought that should stay outside identity truth.

### `instrumental work`

This is where active execution, planning, and experimentation happen.

Live examples:

- [docs/skill-work/work-strategy/README.md](../docs/skill-work/work-strategy/README.md)
- [docs/skill-work/work-dev/README.md](../docs/skill-work/work-dev/README.md)
- [singularity/workshop/sheets/coffee-d-singularity.md](../singularity/workshop/sheets/coffee-d-singularity.md)
- [statecraft/states/migration/README.md](../statecraft/states/migration/README.md)

What these have in common:

- they orient current work rather than acting as settled shelf doctrine
- they can host drafts, workflows, experiments, and temporary structure
- they may generate durable outputs, but are not identical with those outputs
- they may feed the gate, yet do not carry merge authority

Short feel:

`instrumental work` is where the machine is doing something now.

### `external complements`

This is where repo-boundary transport happens without authority collapse.

Live examples:

- [runtime/runtime-complements/README.md](../runtime/runtime-complements/README.md)
- [docs/runtime/runtime-complements.md](runtime/runtime-complements.md)

What these have in common:

- they are designed for import/export and interop
- they are freshness-sensitive
- they can carry useful context without becoming canon
- they require explicit receipts, inboxes, or normalization paths

Short feel:

`external complements` are transport membranes, not identity or doctrine surfaces.

## Two near-miss comparisons

### `statecraft/states/indexes/`

This folder *sounds* like `runtime / derived`, but many of its strongest files are actually `governed adjacent`.

Examples:

- [source-retrieval-matrix.md](../statecraft/states/indexes/source-retrieval-matrix.md)
- [interpretive-difficulty-map.md](../statecraft/states/indexes/interpretive-difficulty-map.md)

Why:

- they are not generated
- they encode durable retrieval judgment
- they are meant to be cited and reused as doctrine

This is a good reminder that folder names do not override membrane class.

### `singularity/workshop/`

This folder leans `instrumental work`, but some of its outputs mature into `governed adjacent`.

Examples:

- a live operating sheet such as [coffee-d-singularity.md](../singularity/workshop/sheets/coffee-d-singularity.md) feels `instrumental work`
- a stable doctrine note such as [keystone-helix.md](../singularity/workshop/keystone-helix.md) behaves more like `governed adjacent`

This is a good reminder that class belongs to the surface's job, not only its parent folder.

## Fast decision test

Ask four questions:

1. Is this meant to persist as citeable judgment?
2. Is it mainly executing current work?
3. Is it mainly a rebuildable helper?
4. Is it mainly crossing the repo boundary?

If the answers are:

- `1`, choose `governed adjacent`
- `2`, choose `instrumental work`
- `3`, choose `runtime / derived`
- `4`, choose `external complements`

When in doubt, prefer naming the class explicitly over letting a folder name decide for you.
