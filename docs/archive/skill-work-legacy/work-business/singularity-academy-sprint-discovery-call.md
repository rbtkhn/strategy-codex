# Singularity-Academy Sprint Discovery Call

non-authoritative. This is an operator call guide, not client proof.

## Purpose

Use this guide to decide whether a prospect has a real workflow problem that fits the AI Operating System Sprint.

The call is not for explaining all of `singularity-academy`.
It is for testing whether one recurring workflow is painful enough, bounded enough, and operator-owned enough to justify a paid sprint.

## Desired outcomes

By the end of the call, you should know:

1. whether there is one real recurring workflow to fix
2. where authority is currently blurry
3. whether the prospect fits the `$2,500` base sprint or the `$5,000` proof-loop sprint
4. whether the next step is a proposal, a parked follow-up, or a no-fit close

## Call structure

Target length:

- `30` minutes

Suggested split:

- `5` minutes: context
- `10` minutes: workflow pain
- `10` minutes: authority and proof
- `5` minutes: fit and next step

## Opening

Use a simple frame:

```text
The point of this call is not to sell you a giant AI program.
I want to understand whether there is one recurring workflow where AI is active, but trust, review, or reuse are still weak.
If that workflow is real, I can tell you whether the sprint is a fit.
```

## Questions

### 1. Workflow reality

- What is the recurring workflow you wish felt easier right now?
- How often does it happen?
- Who touches it?
- What part feels repetitive, unclear, or fragile?

### 2. Current AI use

- Where is AI already helping?
- Where is AI creating noise, rework, or overconfidence?
- What gets drafted by AI today?
- What still requires heavy human rescue?

### 3. Authority and proof

- Who decides when the work is good enough?
- What counts as proof or completion now?
- Where do things break: reporting, review, handoff, or action?
- What would be risky to automate or overstate?

### 4. Operator capacity

- Who owns this workflow internally?
- Can one person approve changes and participate during the sprint?
- Is there enough pain for a 30-day intervention to matter?

## Qualification rubric

### Strong fit

- recurring workflow is concrete
- AI already exists in the workflow
- authority blur is painful
- one human owner can participate
- buyer values trust, review, and reuse

### Weak fit

- workflow is vague
- buyer mainly wants inspiration or general education
- no one owns the process
- buyer wants fully autonomous execution
- there is no way to observe improvement inside 30 days

## Package decision

### Base sprint - `$2,500`

Choose this when:

- one workflow is enough
- reporting or review is the main problem
- the team needs a smaller first commitment

### Proof-loop sprint - `$5,000`

Choose this when:

- there is a second workflow or cohort layer
- the buyer wants a before/after readout
- there is enough operational complexity to justify the added scope

## Close options

### Option A - Strong fit

```text
I think there is a real fit here.
The workflow is clear enough that I can put this into a bounded sprint proposal.
The next step is a short written proposal with scope, package, and outcomes.
```

### Option B - Partial fit

```text
I think the pain is real, but the workflow is still a little too broad.
If you want, I can narrow it into one first workflow and then tell you whether the sprint still makes sense.
```

### Option C - No fit

```text
I do not think this is the right sprint for your situation right now.
The problem sounds either too broad, too early, or too dependent on a different kind of solution.
```

## Call receipt

After the call, capture:

- workflow named
- pain summary
- AI already in use
- main authority blur
- fit score `1-5`
- package likely
- next step

## Failure signals

The call is going badly if:

- you spend most of the time explaining the philosophy
- the prospect cannot name one recurring workflow
- the buyer wants software procurement, not a sprint
- the package discussion arrives before workflow clarity

## Success signal

The call worked if the prospect leaves with a clearer picture of one workflow and you can say yes, no, or not yet without hand-waving.
