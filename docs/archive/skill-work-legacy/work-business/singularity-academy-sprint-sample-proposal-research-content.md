# AI Operating System Sprint Proposal - Sample Research / Content Team

non-authoritative. Sample proposal for adaptation, not a live client agreement.

## Client

- organization: sample research / content team
- owner: lead editor, research lead, or founder-operator
- date: draft example

## Workflow in scope

The sprint will focus on this recurring workflow:

- AI-assisted research or editorial draft -> human review -> final publishable artifact

Current pain:

- AI is accelerating drafting, but the real trust work still depends on invisible senior review.
- The workflow produces more output than the team can reliably trust, reuse, or hand off cleanly.

## Why this sprint

The goal is not broad AI transformation.
The goal is to make one recurring editorial or research workflow easier to route, verify, and reuse without losing human authority.

## Sprint outcomes

By the end of the sprint, the team should have:

- one clearer map of the draft-to-review workflow
- one reporting or brief surface for the workflow
- one review and receipt contract for what counts as publishable
- one authority and rollback map between AI assistance and final judgment
- one operator handoff that makes the workflow easier to rerun

## Scope

Included:

- workflow mapping for one recurring research or editorial loop
- one reporting or review surface
- one review and receipt layer
- one handoff playbook for rerunning the workflow

Not included:

- redesign of the entire editorial operation
- silent posting or execution
- custom software build beyond light sprint tooling refinements
- legal, compliance, or employment commitments

## Package

- package: base sprint
- fee: `$2,500`

### Why this package fits

One workflow is enough to test whether the hidden trust and review burden is real.
The likely problem is concentrated in one handoff, not across a full organization redesign.

## Working method

### Week 1

- map the research or editorial workflow
- identify where AI helps versus where review blur still accumulates
- name the strongest current rescue points

### Week 2

- install one workflow reporting or review surface
- define what counts as proof or publishable quality

### Week 3

- install the review gate, receipt shape, and rollback conditions
- make visible what AI may do and what still requires final human judgment

### Week 4

- rerun the workflow
- compare what changed
- hand off a reusable playbook and next-step memo

## Client responsibilities

- one accountable owner for the workflow
- access to the current process and its artifacts
- timely review during the sprint
- willingness to keep scope bounded to one real workflow

## Success criteria

We will consider the sprint successful if:

- the workflow is easier to name and route
- the team has a clearer standard for what counts as publishable
- hidden rescue work is reduced or at least made visible
- the workflow becomes more reusable and less dependent on one-off correction

## Risks and boundaries

- the sprint improves workflow discipline; it does not guarantee audience, revenue, or editorial performance outcomes
- weak evidence will narrow the output instead of being polished into false certainty
- the sprint is a service install, not a claim of full automation
