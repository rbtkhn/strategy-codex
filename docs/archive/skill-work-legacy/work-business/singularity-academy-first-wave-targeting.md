# Singularity-Academy First-Wave Targeting

non-authoritative. This is a first-batch targeting map, not market validation.

## Purpose

Narrow the first `10` sprint prospects to the buyer types most compatible with current repo proof.

The rule is simple:

- start where the repo already has operational evidence
- do not start where the pitch requires too much translation

## What the repo can already prove

The strongest current proof clusters are:

1. recurring brief and reporting discipline
2. review-gated beginner or cohort workflows
3. control-plane clarity around what AI may do versus what still needs a human gate

That means the first prospects should already feel one of those pains.

## First-wave buyer archetypes

### 1. Founder-operator with reporting sprawl

#### Why this is a strong fit

The repo already has proof around:

- daily brief structure
- proof-linked reporting
- visible degradation under weak evidence
- operator-visible control-plane language

#### Typical pain

- AI drafts exist everywhere
- status updates are noisy or stale
- the founder still does the real synthesis invisibly
- the team cannot tell what is trusted

#### Offer angle

`We install one bounded AI reporting workflow that becomes easier to trust and easier to reuse.`

#### Best package

- likely `base sprint` at `$2,500`

#### Fit score

- `5/5`

### 2. Cohort or education operator with beginner motion but weak proof

#### Why this is a strong fit

The repo already has proof around:

- `cici-ai` daily brief
- first-task proof packet
- Apprentice Studio pilot structure
- beginner-safe task ladders

#### Typical pain

- people join but do not complete
- setup noise gets mistaken for progress
- instruction quality depends on operator rescue
- there is no stable proof packet for what counts as completion

#### Offer angle

`We install a proof-linked cohort workflow so beginner activity becomes easier to route, count, and improve without overclaiming activation.`

#### Best package

- likely `proof-loop sprint` at `$5,000`

#### Fit score

- `5/5`

### 3. Small research or content team with review blur

#### Why this is a strong fit

The repo already has proof around:

- control-plane questions
- review gates
- bounded operator briefs
- quality-contract thinking

#### Typical pain

- AI creates more drafts than the team can trust
- review standards are informal
- someone senior is silently fixing everything
- the team cannot separate signal from polished noise

#### Offer angle

`We install one review-gated workflow so AI-assisted output stops depending on invisible rescue.`

#### Best package

- `base sprint`, with possible upgrade if the team wants measured before/after proof

#### Fit score

- `4/5`

### 4. Small service business with recurring internal workflow friction

#### Why this is a medium fit

This fits if the business already has:

- recurring customer or internal operations
- some AI usage
- one operator who wants clearer routing

It is weaker if the business only wants lead generation or generic automation.

#### Typical pain

- repetitive communication
- draft overload
- no clear review standard
- owner rescue hidden in the background

#### Offer angle

`We make one internal workflow more operable and less dependent on invisible owner correction.`

#### Best package

- usually `base sprint`

#### Fit score

- `3.5/5`

### 5. AI-curious business wanting a broad transformation program

#### Why this is a weak first-wave fit

The repo does not yet have market proof for broad enterprise transformation.

#### Typical pain

- vague fear of being left behind
- no named workflow
- no accountable owner

#### Offer angle

Do not lead here in the first wave.

#### Best package

- not recommended for first batch

#### Fit score

- `1.5/5`

## Recommended first 10 mix

Build the first batch like this:

- `4` founder-operators with reporting or workflow sprawl
- `3` cohort or education operators
- `2` small research or content teams
- `1` small service business with one obvious recurring workflow

Do not waste first-batch slots on enterprise or generic "teach us AI" prospects.

## Pain-language map

Use the language closest to the archetype.

### Founder-operator

- `too many drafts`
- `status is noisy`
- `I still do the hard part invisibly`

### Cohort / education operator

- `setup is not proof`
- `people join but do not complete`
- `the instructions only work when I rescue them`

### Research / content team

- `we have more output than trust`
- `review is inconsistent`
- `AI makes us faster, but not more reliable`

## Package guidance by archetype

| Archetype | Default package | Upgrade condition |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------|
| Founder-operator | `$2,500` base sprint | add `$5,000` proof loop if a second workflow or before/after readout matters |
| Cohort / education operator | `$5,000` proof-loop sprint | none needed unless scope must shrink |
| Research / content team | `$2,500` base sprint | upgrade if multiple reviewers or outputs are involved |
| Small service business | `$2,500` base sprint | only upgrade if there is a clear second workflow |

## First contact rule

Do not lead with:

- singularity
- future of work
- recursive self-improvement

Lead with:

- one workflow
- one pain
- one review problem
- one claim that can be tested in 30 days

## Add these columns to the pipeline

When you use the first-wave mix, fill in:

- archetype
- proof cluster matched
- pain sentence to use

## Boundary

This file narrows targeting. It does not prove that any one archetype will buy.
