# Ukraine Peace-Leverage Arc, Jan 2025-May 20 2026

WORK only; not Record.

## Continuity Pointer

The Jan-May 2025 stream inputs captured on 2026-05-20 form a useful continuity arc on the collapse of Western and Ukrainian bargaining leverage in the Ukraine war.

Repo-current 2026 captures extend that arc into a sharper present-tense frame:

**Ukraine Peace-Leverage Arc -> Terms Become Recognition, Not Choice**

The throughline is not merely "Ukraine peace talks." It is the widening gap between public language about support, strength, and negotiation and the material constraints visible in the captures: manpower, industrial capacity, territorial loss, battlefield morale, and Russia's stated root-cause demands.

## Core Arc

1. **Missed off-ramps and sunk cost logic.** Daniel Davis's Jan 10 monologue frames the war as a chain of avoidable inflection points: Maidan, Minsk, Istanbul, November 2022, the 2023 offensive, and the 2024 aid package. Its function in the arc is historical spine: a map of where leverage was spent rather than preserved.

2. **Battlefield reality bounds diplomacy.** The Davis/Freeman Jan 10 interview states the diplomatic principle most sharply: negotiation cannot reverse the battlefield. Freeman's role in the arc is to translate military facts into diplomatic limits.

3. **Trump inherits weak leverage.** The Feb 10 Davis/Baud and Mercouris captures turn the frame toward Trump: Russia's June 14 terms, NATO neutrality, demilitarization/denazification language, and the four oblast issue appear less as maximalist rhetoric than as the baseline Moscow believes the battlefield has earned.

4. **Kursk as failed bargaining chip.** The Mar 10 captures make the battlefield-diplomacy link concrete. Kursk was treated as potential Ukrainian leverage; the reported collapse turns it into evidence that the bargaining position is deteriorating faster than the negotiation language admits.

5. **European resolve becomes performative constraint.** The Apr-May captures extend the arc into European posture: calls for ultimatums, sanctions, or continued escalation run against visible military and economic limits. The continuity question becomes whether Europe is preserving leverage or preserving a narrative after leverage has drained away.

## Extension To May 20, 2026

Repo-current 2026 captures suggest the peace-leverage arc has moved from "what settlement can be chosen?" toward "which accumulated facts must eventually be recognized?"

On Ukraine, the April 26 Mercouris inbox and raw-input handles preserve a local frame in which Europe is said to have no Ukraine plan while Ukraine wants more EU funds. Read beside the Jan-May 2025 origin set, that line is not just a capacity claim. It is a leverage claim: if the remaining patron side needs more funding, more sanctions, and more rhetorical escalation to hold the position, then the available peace terms are narrowing rather than expanding.

On Europe, the May 16 Diesen/Mercouris capture keeps the negotiation problem explicit. It preserves a critique that European leaders treat Russian attacks as evidence Moscow does not want talks while refusing direct diplomacy themselves, and that they appear to ask Russia to stop fighting and give up leverage. In this arc, that matters because peace terms cannot be separated from leverage: demanding that the stronger battlefield side abandon leverage is not a negotiation position so much as a refusal to recognize the bargaining structure.

On ceasefire and guarantees language, the 2026 inbox handles around Mearsheimer, Freeman, and Hormuz sequencing are useful as analogy rather than direct Ukraine equivalence. They preserve a recurring pattern in repo-current captures: the side with initiative tries to order talks around war termination, guarantees, and chokepoint or territorial leverage, while Washington-preferred sequencing starts elsewhere. The Ukraine continuity point is that "ceasefire" and "security guarantee" language can sound like peace architecture while leaving the root leverage dispute unresolved.

On U.S. leverage, the April 28 Mearsheimer handle preserves strategic bandwidth limits, force depletion, and no quick-reset military option across Gulf, Ukraine, and East Asia. Used cautiously, this extends the Jan 2025 Freeman point: diplomacy cannot conjure leverage that the battlefield, inventory, industrial base, or political coalition no longer supplies.

On Russia-facing terms, the May 16 Davis/Polyanskiy capture preserves "save what can be saved" language and a Russian-side insistence that the future shape of Ukraine remains the core issue. This does not verify Moscow's claims; it gives the WORK layer a local handle for the present negotiation problem. The terms under discussion increasingly look less like discretionary concessions and more like recognition of which side can continue imposing costs.

## Useful Synthesis Line

The Jan 2025-May 2026 captures trace a transition from **choosing terms** to **recognizing accumulated facts**: the longer the war continues without settlement, the less "peace talks" look like a discretionary diplomatic process and the more they look like delayed acknowledgment of battlefield, industrial, fiscal, and political arithmetic.

## Distinction From The Other Two Arcs

This peace-leverage arc asks: **What terms remain available as battlefield facts accumulate?**

The capacity-description arc asks: **Why do officials keep describing options that their own material base cannot sustain?**

The proxy-patron arc asks: **Why do patrons keep clients fighting after victory becomes unavailable, and how do they later transfer or disguise the cost of exit?**

The three arcs should be read together but not collapsed. Peace-leverage is about the feasible bargaining set: how missed off-ramps, failed leverage plays, and battlefield deterioration turn negotiation from a choice among preferred outcomes into recognition of accumulated constraints.
