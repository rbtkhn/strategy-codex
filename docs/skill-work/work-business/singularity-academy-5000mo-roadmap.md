# Singularity-Academy `$5k/mo` Roadmap

WORK only. Planning surface, not demand proof.

## Objective

Create a realistic path for `singularity-academy` to generate `$5,000` per month by selling one narrow service that the repo can already partially deliver.

## Chosen wedge

Primary offer:

- [Singularity-Academy AI Operating System Sprint](singularity-academy-ai-operating-system-sprint.md)

Reason for choosing it:

- it reuses live repo assets
- it sells an outcome instead of content
- it keeps authority and proof boundaries explicit
- one sale can satisfy the monthly target

## Revenue paths

### Path A - One premium sprint

- `1` client
- `$5,000`
- strongest path if the buyer already feels workflow pain and has authority to act

### Path B - Two base installs

- `2` clients
- `$2,500` each
- best if buyers are smaller or want a lower-risk entry

### Path C - Hybrid

- `1` base sprint at `$2,500`
- `1` workshop or follow-on support block worth `$2,500`

Inference:

Path A is the cleanest story. Path B is the safer sales reality.

## Ideal first buyers

Prioritize buyers who already live close to the repo's strengths:

1. founder-operators drowning in AI tool sprawl
2. cohort or education operators needing proof-linked beginner workflows
3. research or content teams with recurring brief and review pain

Avoid first:

- buyers needing enterprise procurement
- buyers demanding end-to-end autonomous execution
- buyers who only want inspiration content

## What must be true for the offer to sell

- the buyer feels daily AI workflow pain
- one operator owns the process
- the workflow is recurring, not one-off
- the buyer values review and rollback
- there is enough proof to show the sprint is real

## Sales motion

### Step 1 - Pick 10 close-fit prospects

Choose people or teams already adjacent to:

- education
- research
- small-team operations
- founder workflow pain

Do not start with cold enterprise outreach.

### Step 2 - Send a narrow offer

Lead with one pain:

- AI is active but not governed
- reporting exists but does not drive action
- the operator is still doing the hard part invisibly

### Step 3 - Offer a bounded first call

Goal of the call:

- identify one recurring workflow
- confirm the operator pain is real
- decide whether a base or premium sprint fits

### Step 4 - Sell the sprint, not the philosophy

Use `singularity-academy` as the explanatory frame, but sell:

- one workflow
- one reporting layer
- one review contract
- one handoff

## Delivery sequence after sale

1. workflow map
2. brief/report layer
3. receipt and rollback layer
4. second-cycle proof
5. handoff and next-step memo

## 30-day operator sequence

### Days 1-3

- finalize the one-page offer
- assemble the proof packet
- list `10` prospects

### Days 4-10

- send first outreach batch
- book `3-5` calls
- log objections and confusion

Execution surfaces for this phase:

- [Singularity-Academy First-Wave Send Checklist](singularity-academy-first-wave-send-checklist.md)
- [Singularity-Academy First-Wave Outreach Log](singularity-academy-first-wave-outreach-log.md)
- [Singularity-Academy First-Response Handling](singularity-academy-first-response-handling.md)

### Days 11-20

- refine the pitch from call language
- close one base or premium sprint
- tighten the proof packet around the real objection pattern

### Days 21-30

- deliver the sprint
- capture before/after notes
- turn the first client result into stronger proof for the second sale

## Failure conditions

Rerank the plan if any of these happen:

- every prospect likes the theory but not the offer
- buyers want software, not service
- the repo cannot produce a clean proof packet quickly
- the sprint delivery burden is much higher than the price supports

## What this roadmap does not claim

- that external demand is already proven
- that singularity-academy should become a media business first
- that the current repo proof is enough for a large market claim

## Best next move

Use the first-wave send checklist on the top `3` prospects, log the real sends, and route the first reply without widening the offer.
