# AI Operating System Sprint Proposal - Sample Cohort Proof-Loop

WORK only. Sample proposal for adaptation, not a live client agreement.

## Client

- organization: sample cohort or education operator
- owner: cohort operator, program lead, or learning operations owner
- date: draft example

## Workflow in scope

The sprint will focus on this recurring workflow:

- onboarding -> first-task proof -> operator review -> member re-entry

Current pain:

- setup activity is easier to generate than real proof of completion
- operator rescue is carrying too much of the real learning and verification load

## Why this sprint

The goal is not to make a community feel more active.
The goal is to make one beginner-facing workflow easier to route, count, review, and improve without overclaiming activation.

## Sprint outcomes

By the end of the sprint, the cohort should have:

- one clearer member progression path
- one proof-linked reporting or brief layer
- one review and receipt contract for first real completion
- one authority and rollback map around what AI may scaffold versus what still needs human verification
- one operator handoff that makes the workflow easier to rerun

## Scope

Included:

- workflow mapping for one beginner or cohort progression loop
- one daily or weekly reporting surface
- one proof and receipt layer
- one handoff playbook plus a before/after operator readout

Not included:

- a full program redesign
- automatic certification or advancement decisions
- silent posting or execution
- custom platform build beyond light sprint tooling refinements

## Package

- package: proof-loop sprint
- fee: `$5,000`

### Why this package fits

This kind of buyer usually needs the second layer:

- before/after readout
- visible member progression logic
- proof-linked operator review

One workflow is still the center, but the proof loop is part of the value.

## Working method

### Week 1

- map the onboarding to first-task workflow
- identify where setup is being mistaken for proof
- name the strongest rescue and re-entry failures

### Week 2

- install one reporting or brief layer
- define what counts as proof and what does not

### Week 3

- install the review gate, receipt shape, and rollback conditions
- make explicit what the AI scaffolding may do versus what still requires human verification

### Week 4

- rerun the workflow
- compare before and after using a simple operator scorecard
- hand off the playbook plus a follow-on decision memo

## Client responsibilities

- one accountable owner for the workflow
- access to current onboarding, task, and proof surfaces
- timely review during the sprint
- willingness to keep the intervention bounded to one progression loop

## Success criteria

We will consider the sprint successful if:

- the cohort has a clearer definition of what counts as proof
- operator rescue is reduced or at least made visible
- members can re-enter the workflow more cleanly
- one progression workflow becomes reusable rather than rebuilt each cycle

## Risks and boundaries

- the sprint improves workflow discipline; it does not guarantee retention, revenue, or community growth outcomes
- weak evidence will narrow the output instead of being inflated into activation claims
- the sprint is a service install, not a promise of autonomous program management
