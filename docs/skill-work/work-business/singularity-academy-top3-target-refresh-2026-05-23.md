## Purpose

Capture the strongest current official-page facts for the top three outreach targets so first-contact notes stay anchored to what is publicly true now.

## 1. AI Adjacent

### Current public facts

- the About page says each issue is drafted by an agentic research pipeline
- AI agents ingest sources across internet, academic, and social ecosystems
- relevance is evaluated, findings are clustered into storylines, and drafts are structured before final review
- a human editor curates the weekly issues
- daily briefs are described as lightly automated
- all content lives in a git repository and every page, RSS item, and email is a build artifact of a commit
- public contact routes:
  - `hello@aiadjacent.com`
  - `tips@aiadjacent.com`

### What this changes

This is an even stronger fit than the earlier draft assumed.

The pitch does not have to infer an AI-first editorial architecture anymore.
The prospect says it directly.

### Strongest pain hypothesis

The likely problem is not whether the system exists.
It is whether the review, receipt, and reuse layer between generated draft and final judgment is still too dependent on invisible editor trust work.

### Best line to preserve

`You already have an AI-first editorial pipeline, but there may still be hidden trust work between draft generation and final review.`

## 2. 2389 Research

### Current public facts

- the About page says the company builds software and publishes research about AI agents, developer tools, and human-computer interaction
- the company says products ship early, the blog covers what they are learning, and research gets published whether or not the results are flattering
- current product pages and homepage language emphasize:
  - local-first or secrecy-preserving agent posture
  - AI pipeline workflows
  - DAG runners
  - human gates
  - checkpointing
- public contact routes:
  - `hello@2389.ai`
  - `+1 (814) 666-2389`

### What this changes

2389 is not just a founder-operator fit.
It is also a control-plane fit.

The strongest hook is no longer generic founder trust work.
It is that a team building human-gated AI workflow infrastructure may still have one internal research or publishing workflow where review burden has not been made as reusable as the product language suggests.

### Strongest pain hypothesis

The likely problem is one internal research-to-public-output workflow where the external product story is ahead of the internal reporting, review, or receipt layer.

### Best line to preserve

`You already ship in public across products and research, but one operating workflow may still be carrying too much invisible founder or senior trust work.`

## 3. deep.org

### Current public facts

- the About page says deep.org is a high-signal briefing on AI and the near future
- public formats include:
  - concise briefs
  - explainers
  - tool radar
- editorial principles include:
  - signal over volume
  - clarity over hype
  - independent judgment
  - quick corrections
- audience is described as operators, researchers, investors, and curious builders
- public contact route:
  - `hello@deep.org`

### What this changes

The core fit remains the same.
The best pitch angle is still editorial trust and hidden founder filtering, but the current page makes the correction and evidence discipline angle stronger than before.

### Strongest pain hypothesis

The likely problem is one editorial or briefing workflow where the public standard is clear but the operating layer still depends on invisible human filtering to keep that standard real.

### Best line to preserve

`Your editorial principles already value clarity over hype, but the trusted operating layer behind that standard may still depend on invisible founder work.`
