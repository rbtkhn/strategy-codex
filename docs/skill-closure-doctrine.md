# Skill Closure Doctrine

WORK documentation. Not Record.

Purpose: define when a skill, route, or scaffold is mature enough to absorb stronger synthesis rather than stopping earlier.

## Core Rule

Do not let synthesis outrun route maturity.

A skill should only absorb stronger synthesis when the receiving surface is already strong enough to carry the claim honestly.

Short form:

`signal -> route -> owning surface -> maturity check -> synthesis`

If the route or owner is still thin, the honest result is usually a packet, routing hint, arc note, queue item, scaffold, or unresolved seam.

## Maturity Test

A skill, route, or scaffold is usually mature enough to absorb stronger synthesis only when it can answer all of these clearly:

1. **Front door** - can a future operator tell when to open this surface?
2. **Ownership** - does the surface own the kind of claim being strengthened?
3. **Output shape** - does it have a stable output form rather than improvised prose?
4. **Next surface** - can it point cleanly to the next durable route rather than acting as a parking lot?
5. **Closure rule** - can the skill say what counts as complete and what still counts as open?

If several answers are no, the synthesis is ahead of the architecture.

## Typical Honest Stopping Points

When the maturity test fails, stop earlier:

- packet
- source-hygiene note
- discovery receipt
- routing hint
- host-local arc
- bench item
- queue or candidate note
- unresolved seam held open deliberately

Those are not failures. They are the correct forms when the stronger receiving surface has not earned the claim yet.

## False Closure Patterns

Watch for these skill-level traps:

- `route substitute`: a scaffold starts pretending to be the durable destination
- `beautiful sentence over thin shelf`: synthesis makes the destination sound more mature than it is
- `mention as completion`: a hint, roster row, or queue entry gets treated as if the route were now real
- `capture as judgment`: materialization gets mistaken for interpretation
- `packet avoidance`: a seam that should stay layered is prematurely flattened into doctrine

## First Applications

This doctrine currently governs three nearby skill families:

1. **speaker arc**
   A strong host x guest run may still stop at a stream-local arc unless a fuller speaker route is actually warranted.
2. **check streams**
   A clean ingest run may still stop at verified appearance plus route hint rather than speaker doctrine or lattice update.
3. **packet-first**
   A valuable seam may still stop at packet or layered note when the destination surface is not architecturally ready for stronger synthesis.

## Boundary

This doctrine does not mean synthesis should be postponed forever.

It means stronger synthesis should land only where route, ownership, and closure are already strong enough to keep the claim honest on reuse.
