# Glossary

This glossary defines the governing vocabulary for **Civilizational Statecraft**. It is concept-first, but it also names peoples, polities, dynasties, churches, empires, and transformed carriers that repeatedly structure the books.

Where a concept or name is still unevenly evidenced across the five cases, keep the entry narrower rather than pretending to finality.

## Core Concepts

### Civilizational Statecraft

The **book title** — the public comparative work (five civilization-state volumes plus whole-work apparatus). Published at [`rbtkhn/civ-state`](https://github.com/rbtkhn/civ-state).

### Civilization and Empire

The **whole-work opening essay** ([introduction.md](introduction.md)) — civilizational motion and the five-volume frame. This title is **not** the book title and **not** a volume introduction.

### Civilizational motion

The recurrent three-beat rhythm — civilization beautifies, empire amplifies, entropy degrades — plus the retrieval movement memory → legitimacy → carrier → pressure → settlement. Canonical section: [theory/memory.md — Civilizational rhythm](../theory/memory.md#civilizational-rhythm). Distinct from paired-civilization **double-helix** analysis in external memory corpora.

### Civilizational Statecraft Framework

The governing frame — **six terms** (civilization, empire, entropy, faith, science, memory) on the [theory shelf](../theory/README.md). Rhythm and era law live on [memory](../theory/memory.md). Diagnostic retrieval grammar, not prescriptive command doctrine. **Desire** is retired; overreach → [empire](../theory/empire.md).

### Civilization (governing term)

Inherited order and legitimacy substrate. Canonical page: [theory/civilization.md](../theory/civilization.md). Not the whole-work essay title *Civilization and Empire* ([introduction.md](introduction.md)).

### Empire (governing term)

Outward instrument and amplification stack. Canonical page: [theory/empire.md](../theory/empire.md).

### Entropy

Historical causes and manifestations of civilizational degradation — war, revolution, disease, famine, ecological disaster, and compound shocks; the degrades beat. Canonical page: [theory/entropy.md](../theory/entropy.md).

### Faith

Sacred and moral truth-order. Canonical page: [theory/faith.md](../theory/faith.md).

### Science

Procedural and evidentiary truth-order. Canonical page: [theory/science.md](../theory/science.md).

### Memory (governing term)

Continuity-bearing inheritance, civilizational rhythm, era law, and retrieval entry. Canonical page: [theory/memory.md](../theory/memory.md). Distinct from lane **`state-memory`** surfaces and from the retrieval-sequence step also named memory.

### Era law

The shared form-historical spine — Ancient → Medieval → Colonial → Industrial → Cybernetic — with the post-`1991` **Industrial / Cybernetic** boundary load-bearing for bibliography retrieval. Canonical section: [theory/memory.md — Era law](../theory/memory.md#era-law). Per-volume tables: [era-spine.md](era-spine.md).

### civ-state

The **workshop folder slug** and GitHub repository name for the source-memory substrate. Operators use it for paths and export; **readers** should say **Civilizational Statecraft** for the book.

### Volume introduction

Each volume's thematic opening essay (for example Mandate of Heaven, Order of Asha) — distinct from **Civilization and Empire** and from the **Civilization** / **Empire** chapter pair inside that volume.

### Civilization-state

A political order in which sovereignty is carried through a long continuity chain, legitimized by a deeper sacred or civilizational grammar, and still borne by a present carrier usable for live statecraft judgment.

### Legitimacy

The condition under which authority feels rightful rather than merely effective. In CIV-STATE it is usually carried by law, covenant, ritual order, providence, doctrine, historical memory, or some durable mix of them.

### Carrier

The institution, regime, church, state form, or transformed political body that currently bears the continuity chain.

### Continuity

The persistence of a political-civilizational order through rupture, mutation, reconstitution, and carrier change.

### Restoration

The attempt to recover a continuity-bearing order after humiliation, fracture, usurpation, encirclement, decay, or illegitimate expansion.

### Overreach

The point at which outward instrument outruns the legitimacy, maintenance capacity, or civilizational inheritance that once justified it.

### Equilibrium

A live political balance that allows order to hold without requiring maximal victory, permanent emergency, or universal submission.

### Settlement

A political arrangement durable enough to be carried after crisis. In CIV-STATE it is judged not only by force distribution but by legitimacy, carrier reality, and successor stability.

### Transformed carrier

A later political body that bears an earlier continuity chain without preserving its original form unchanged.

### Sovereign opening

The first figure or formation that opens the continuity chain relevant to a CIV-STATE volume.

### Deep grammar

The sacred, mythic, literary, moral, or civilizational substrate beneath the chain. It makes rule feel rightful more deeply than office or administration alone.

## Peoples, Polities, and Dynasties

### Anglo-America

The longer continuity chain by which English constitutional inheritance, maritime-commercial order, and later American concentration are read as one strained but still legible civilization-state arc.

### Achaemenid Persia

The Cyrus-founded imperial opening that anchors the Persian sovereignty chain in its strongest classical form.

### Byzantium

The Roman transformed carrier through which imperial universality, Christianized legitimacy, and legal continuity survive western fracture.

### Muscovy

The decisive Russian compression core through which regathering, Orthodox legitimacy, and post-steppe sovereign restoration become durable.

### Qin

The founding dynastic-state concentration that gives China its strongest early model of one-center political order.

### Romanov dynasty

A major Russian transformed carrier that deepens imperial scale while preserving the regathered continuity chain.

### Safavid Persia

The decisive Persian reconstitution in which Shi'a legitimacy and restored political form become inseparable from later Iranian continuity.

### United States

The present American carrier: a globally powerful but internally contested concentration of a longer Anglo-American chain.

## Churches, Orders, and Civilizational Forms

### Civil religion

A legitimacy form in which covenant, law, ritual, oath, providence, sacrifice, and public myth operate politically without reducing the order to formal church rule.

### Mandate

The Chinese legitimacy grammar by which rule is justified through order-bearing performance and can be morally revoked through chaos, corruption, or misrule.

### Orthodoxy

In the Russian and Byzantine frames especially, a truth-order and continuity-bearing sacred grammar that stabilizes rule, suffering, gathering, and non-peripheral identity.

### Third Rome

The Russian continuity claim that Moscow inherits the sacred and civilizational succession of Rome and Byzantium after their historical fracture.
