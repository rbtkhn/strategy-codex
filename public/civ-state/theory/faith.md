# Faith

**Sacred and moral truth-order** — covenant, mandate, orthodoxy, and forbidden bargains.

Status: Active doctrine  
Surface: `civ-state`  
Purpose: govern reads where sacred, moral, covenantal, or civil-religious authorization is load-bearing  
Last updated: 2026-06-17

Volume **sacred-grammar** files name lane-local legitimacy grammar; this page names the **faith** governing term across the whole work.

## Why this term

Most live objects mix moral or sacred authorization with procedural or expert authorization. **Faith** names what feels rightful, forbidden, or covenant-bound before it is verified or administered.

## Definition

Faith is sacred, moral, symbolic, covenantal, or civil-religious truth-order: how an actor understands rightful authority, meaning, duty, and moral limit.

## Governing question

Ask first:

> Is this object faith-heavy — about sacred authorization, covenant, mandate, orthodoxy, or moral boundary?

## Causal connections

### Science

[Faith](faith.md) and [science](science.md) are coequal truth-orders — not religion versus laboratories. They may align, compete, or hybridize (constitutional creed + technocratic expertise). Collapsing faith into science yields hollow proceduralism; collapsing science into faith yields ungovernable moral theater.

### Civilization

[Faith](faith.md) often carries [civilization](civilization.md) — mandate of heaven, covenant, providence, orthodoxy as legitimacy substrate. When civilization is faith-heavy, sacred grammar usually governs before instrument.

### Entropy

[Entropy](entropy.md) may leave faith as **rhetoric without carry** — sacred language surviving after practice hollows. Distinguish living authorization from performed legitimacy.

## Failure modes

- **faith without science** — sacred order loses procedural competence or governability
- **faith without carry** — rhetoric of mandate or covenant without institution or succession that can bear it
