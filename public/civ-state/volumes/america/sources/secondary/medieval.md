# America Secondary Sources - Medieval

## What This Secondary Shelf Is For

Use this shelf after the medieval America primary shelf when Anglian inheritance, Alfred, charter, common law, or constitutional-kingship carry need stronger documentary context. This file owns the main America-side explanation of why Churchill's *A History of the English-Speaking Peoples* matters here: civilization-specific continuity narration for the Anglo-American chain, not cross-civilizational authority.

## Core Clarifiers

- Winston Churchill, *A History of the English-Speaking Peoples*, for broad Anglo continuity narration and the long Alfred-to-Atlantic chain.
- J. C. Holt and Magna Carta scholarship, for legal and charter context behind later constitutional inheritance.
- F. W. Maitland and common-law history, for institutional and legal carry.
- Will Durant, brief supporting use only where broad civilizational sweep helps situate Anglian inheritance inside the wider Western story.

## Counterweights And Corrections

- Medieval England scholarship, as a correction against Churchillian over-smoothness and heroic continuity compression.
- Common-law and charter historians, as a correction against treating medieval Anglia as already identical to later Anglo-American constitutionalism.

## Use Rule

Stay with the primary shelf when the issue is charter language, royal law, or early common-law wording. Open this shelf when the problem is inheritance, transmission, or keeping the shelf from becoming either fabricated antiquity or mythic English continuity.
