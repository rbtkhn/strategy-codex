# China Secondary Sources - Ancient

## What This Secondary Shelf Is For

Use this shelf after the ancient China primary shelf when mandate grammar, ritual order, early state hardening, or Qin-Han consolidation need chronology, translation, or institutional clarification. This file owns the main China-side explanation of why Will Durant is useful here: broad civilizational sweep and readable transition framing, but not specialist final authority.

## Core Clarifiers

- Yuri Pines, *The Everlasting Empire* and related early-China work, for durable state form, political thought, and Qin-Han consolidation.
- Mark Edward Lewis, *The Early Chinese Empires: Qin and Han*, for institutional hardening, administrative formation, and imperial scale.
- Michael Nylan, work on the Five Classics and canonical transmission, for translation, textual role, and ritual-intellectual context.
- Will Durant, *Our Oriental Heritage*, as a broad civilizational synthesis clarifier for the early China shelf and major transition compression.

## Counterweights And Corrections

- Benjamin Elman, work on classical learning and state orthodoxy, as a correction against treating early canon as timeless uniform doctrine.
- Martin Kern, work on textual formation and transmitted classics, as a correction against reading the earliest shelf as fully settled documentary transparency.

## Use Rule

Stay with the primary shelf when the issue is legitimacy language, canonical wording, or the civilization's own self-description. Open this shelf when the problem is how those texts were formed, transmitted, institutionalized, or overly simplified by later survey reading.
