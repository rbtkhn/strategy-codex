# China Shelf Reader

## What This Shelf Is For

Use this shelf to read China as an order-bearing civilization-state through its own source doors. The point is to see how mandate, bureaucratic continuity, anti-chaos legitimacy, rupture, and reconstitution are carried across eras before widening into comparison or present-tense statecraft.

## How To Read The Eras

Read China from durable founding form toward restored present carriage. Start with canon and state hardening, then watch how continuity survives conquest, humiliation, fragmentation, industrial reconstitution, and post-1991 cybernetic order. The key question is always whether the center is being restored, absorbed, or over-hardened.

## What Each Era Is Doing

- `Ancient` establishes mandate, canon, administrative hardening, and the first durable state form.
- `Medieval` tests continuity through dynastic restoration, bureaucratic carry, and conquest absorption.
- `Colonial` records pressure, humiliation, failed reform, and the breakdown of older order.
- `Industrial` shows revolutionary refounding, party-state consolidation, and industrial reconstitution through `1991`.
- `Cybernetic` carries post-`1991` rejuvenation, digital governance, maritime-frontier order, and centralized control.

## What To Look For

- mandate and anti-chaos legitimacy
- canon, law, and bureaucracy as continuity carriers
- rupture answered by reconcentration rather than dispersion
- Japan and Manchuria as late pressure shocks
- the PRC as the dominant late carrier after fragmentation
