# Rome Shelf Reader

## What This Shelf Is For

Use this shelf to read Rome as a transformed-continuity civilization-state through its own source field. The shelf exists to show how Roman order survives not by one uninterrupted territorial carrier, but by mutation across law, church, empire residue, and successor-order memory.

## How To Read The Eras

Read Rome as a migration shelf. Start with founding law, empire, and universality, then track how Roman continuity survives contraction, Christianization, Eastern continuity, **classical Latin/Greek dual heritage**, geographic Latin-Catholic dispersion, and post-`1991` residue rather than pretending that Rome is either simply dead or theatrically revived.

**Language note:** **Classical Latin** and **Greek** inheritance (see [language spine](rome-memory.md#language-spine)) are not the same object as geographic **Latin-Catholic** or **Latin-American** residue labels.

## What Each Era Is Doing

- `Ancient` establishes republic, empire, law, universality, and the classical state form.
- `Medieval` carries Christian empire, Eastern Roman survival, papal emergence, and transformed continuity.
- `Colonial` shows Latin-Catholic expansion, successor carriers, and outward residue beyond the ancient core.
- `Industrial` carries narrowed Vatican, national, concordat, and Latin-world residue through `1991`.
- `Cybernetic` carries post-`1991` Roman residue as comparative successor-order memory rather than full state return.

## What To Look For

- law and universality as continuity carriers
- mutation rather than smooth institutional sameness
- papal and Latin-Catholic residue after territorial loss
- successor-order dispersion rather than repeated reconcentration
- the danger of both false death and theatrical revivalism
