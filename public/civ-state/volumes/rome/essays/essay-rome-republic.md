## Reading Posture

Read this essay **standalone**—complete without peer essays. After **c. 390**, when Gauls sack the city and Romans vote to rebuild without kings, Rome learns **scale**: Italian federation, Punic war, Mediterranean hegemony—all while arguing internally about who counts as citizen, who commands, and what law binds the conqueror.

Polybius supplies structure: people, senate, magistrates in balance. Livy supplies moral memory: *virtus*, discipline, and the cost of forgetting them.[^1] Gibbon reads Livy across centuries as **citizen historian**, not court flatterer—recalling republican virtue so later readers measure their distance from exempla.[^2] The essay closes when **republican form is visibly strained**—not when autocracy has won. For the transformation hinge: [hexagonal-republic-principate](hexagonal-republic-principate-rome.md); for hinge figures: [Essay-rome registry](connectivity-rome.md#essay-rome-registry) (caesar · augustus slugs).

**Membrane:** empire overreach ≠ entropy; transformation ≠ rupture—do not preload **476** or decadence theater here.

## Italian federation after 390

Recovery from the Gallic sack is **re-foundation under republican habit**—not return to a village. Samnite wars teach federation by ordeal: allies bound by treaty, citizenship graduated, Latin rights extended without dissolving Roman primacy. Rome absorbs enemies as **participants in order**, not only as subjects—technique the empire will later universalize.

**Pyrrhus** brings Hellenistic kingship and the phalanx against the legion—brilliance that wins battles but not transmission. Plutarch catches the moral: one more such victory and Pyrrhus is undone.[^3] Rome wins by **attrition and alliance**, institution over charisma—the same grammar genesis named against Alexander, here proved on Italian ground.

Livy's republican books teach **exempla under expansion**—discipline, oath, shared sacrifice when collegial command must repeat victory without a king.[^4] Censor **Appius Claudius** paves the Appian Way and brings the Aqua Appia—public utility as sovereignty argument; the **form promise** now binds the peninsula.[^5] Roads become **federation glue**: allies linked to Rome; strategic *viae* follow Samnite and Pyrrhic ordeals.

Military depth: [military-history-rome](military-history-rome.md). Pyrrhus mirror clarifier: Everitt, *Alexander the Great* (Scholarship).

## Conflict of Orders and constitutional deepening

Internal fracture is republican, not regal. Plebeians refuse a permanent military underclass; patricians refuse to dissolve privilege without compensation. The Conflict of Orders is Rome's **long constitutional argument**—tribunes, *concilium plebis*, access to magistracy, law moving toward public written form.

The **Twelve Tables** anchor that move: law posted for all to read—the community bound by visible text, not priestly memory alone.[^6] Cicero states the republican ideal: the state exists for the **common good** of its citizens united by justice.[^7] *De Officiis* sharpens the ethic: office is trust—who seeks profit from public station betrays the res publica.[^8]

Aristotle's middle-regime theory names why mixed forms endure: the middle class stands between extremes and stabilizes the whole.[^9] Here theory meets **operating machinery**—not democracy theater like Athens (Scholarship: Everitt, *Rise of Athens*), but collegial endurance under expansion.

## Polybius VI — mixed constitution at work

Polybius writes when Rome has broken Greek independence—and explains **why** Greek categories fit Roman practice. A hostage who became analyst, he gives Book VI as this essay's constitutional spine:

The Roman constitution distributes power among people, senate, and magistrates so that **no part plainly dominates**—each checks the other by necessity of survival.[^10] That balance explains endurance where brilliant single-regime cities fail.

He also records reciprocal penetration at **republic scale**: conquerors adopt conquered culture—language, education, habit.[^11] The republic is therefore **bilingual in soul** before it is universal in law: Greek analysis, Latin command. Herodotus taught that **nomos** binds peoples; Rome now applies nomos upward and outward—Hellenic poleis under *imperium*, assimilation by incorporation where Third Punic later chooses annihilation.

## Punic sequence and Mediterranean hegemony

The Punic sequence is the republic's **sea exam**. **Carthage** holds naval commerce and African grain; Rome holds the legion and Italian manpower. First contact teaches humility; Second Punic teaches **near-death**—**Hannibal** at **Cannae** as wound in memory, not only defeat.[^12]

Scipio Africanus supplies Rome's **answer**: take the war to Africa, break Carthage's alliance system, win by **political geometry** as much as battle—institution and strategy over charisma. Zama closes the arc; Third Punic ends rivalry by **elimination**—a harsher instrument Rome will not always repeat, but proof the republic can choose annihilation when security demands it. Rival-city civilization depth: planned **carthage** slug in [Essay-rome registry](connectivity-rome.md#essay-rome-registry).

Mediterranean hegemony follows: Spain, Greece, Macedon, Asia—each annexation asks **what Rome is now**: city, federation, or sea-power with provinces? Strabo later sees the mature emporium; the republic builds toward it—merchandise and meaning converging at the Tiber.[^13]

## Provinces and citizenship grammar

Expansion forces **category work**. Allies become citizens; citizens become soldiers; soldiers become voters—sometimes far from Rome. The republic grows an **imperial vocabulary** before it grows an emperor: *provincia*, governor, *imperium*, publicani, courts for foreigners.

Cicero's prosecution of Verres is exemplary: a governor may not treat a province as private farm—the republic claims **jurisdiction over its magistrates**.[^14] That claim is republican ethics pressed against empire-scale temptation—Civilization's ethic before Empire's machinery fully separates.

**Aqueducts and roads at scale** carry the same logic: order visible, shared, maintained—water to the city, *viae* to the province; law and logistics travel with command.[^5] Procedural birth of Roman law: [science-history-rome](science-history-rome.md). Empire chronicle: [rome-empire — sovereignty chain](../rome-empire.md#sovereignty-chain-empire-lens).

## Late republic strain — form under pressure

Success breeds scale; scale breeds **competition among elites** for offices that control armies and provinces.

The **Gracchi** test whether republican form can redistribute without breaking—tribune weapon against senatorial order. Plutarch reads Tiberius as man who loved the poor and sought justice—and died for pushing too hard against settled power.[^15] **Marius** reforms the army to save the state—and risks loyalty shifting from *res publica* to general; Sallust's Jugurthine war exposes provincial greed and Marius's rise in one breath.[^16]

**Sulla** suspends form to "restore" it—dictatorship, proscriptions, civil-war grammar before the Rubicon. **Pompey** becomes the senate's strongman—eastern *imperium*, extraordinary commands, rival legions without a crown—foreshadowing the threshold this essay stops before.

Sallust diagnoses the mood: luxury and faction corrode *virtus*; empire's fruits poison the city that earned them.[^17] That is **strain**, not universal rupture—the chain continues; the instrument creaks. Gibbon helps the standalone reader see **distance from exempla**, not sudden fall.[^2]

**Caesar** is named only at the close: crossing the Rubicon is the hinge—open [hexagonal-republic-principate](hexagonal-republic-principate-rome.md) for the six-lens transformation read; open [Essay-rome registry](connectivity-rome.md#essay-rome-registry) for caesar and augustus when those nodes ship.

Standalone closure: the reader sees **what the republic achieved** (Italian union, Punic victory, Mediterranean order, mixed constitution at work) and **where it strained** (office-as-prize, provincial wealth, army loyalty shifting)—without needing principate resolution inside this file.
