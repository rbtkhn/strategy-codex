## Reading Posture

Rome carries one military grammar through **force-structure mutation** — legion, thematic army, crusader host, standing monarchy, colonial garrison — not through an unbroken legion. This is not one army under one flag forever. It is **one political chain** with a **parallel military dimension** tracking which institution carries Roman war-making at each rupture, alongside [chain spine](../rome-memory.md#chain-spine) and [language spine](../rome-memory.md#language-spine).

## Core Thesis

Rome metabolizes conquest into durable order by **mutating institutions** while preserving operational grammar: professionalization (Marian reforms), command personalization (Caesar), imperial field systems (Augustus through Eastern Roman bridge), and successor instruments (Frankish, Habsburg, Iberian colonial) that carry frontier-maintenance without replacing the papal chain head.

The Punic Wars integrate **naval–legion coherence** — Mediterranean interior becomes militarily governable. The Marian → Caesarian → Augustan sequence is load-bearing: standing force, memoir-command, institutional limes.

- **Eastern rival rows** (Persia · Arab · Turk) index Roman-facing frontier grammar in parallel — not a claim that Parthians, the early caliphate, and Ottomans are one historical actor.
- **`operator-synthesis-chain`** on the Persia row links Herodotus's Persia-wars analysis to Rome–Parthia/Sassanian memory as **intellectual lineage** — not proof of direct institutional succession.
- **Reconquista** language on the Iberia military row uses contested retrieval framing; **chain-spine** remains retrieval-primary.
- **NATO** appears only as `operator-present-residue-stretch` — a comparative footnote on frontier-maintenance analogy, not a present military period row.
- **Latin-American colonial tail** — colonial history is mainstream; Roman chain framing is operator placement (`contested-weight` on oceanic-colonial index).

Mainstream dates and battles (476, 1204, 1453, Westphalia, Waterloo) align with standard histories; the **triple parallel spine** (chain · language · military) is volume architecture.
